# CAMPAIGN MISSIONS

### Primary Missions, Secondary Objectives & Special Rules — Revised Draft v2 (pre-layout reconciliation)

## 1. Overview

Campaign Missions represent adventures, military conflicts, investigations, rescues, expeditions, and political encounters undertaken during Campaign Play.

Unlike Matched Play, Campaign Missions do not require both sides to have identical objectives.

A Campaign Mission consists of four elements:

1. **Primary Mission:** The main encounter objective and victory condition.
2. **Secondary Objectives:** Additional tasks assigned independently to the participating sides.
3. **Special Rules:** Optional rules representing unusual conditions.
4. **Campaign Stakes:** The political, economic, military, or narrative consequences of the encounter.

Primary success determines the winner of the encounter.

Secondary success is recorded independently, including when the side completing it loses the Primary Mission.

## 1.1 Mission Construction

The GM prepares an encounter in this order:

1. Establish the relevant Campaign Stake, Company Order, or narrative situation.
2. Identify the participants and the strategic objective.
3. Select a Primary Mission.
4. Select a Battlefield Layout or Narrative environment.
5. Assign one Secondary Objective to each side.
6. Add any required Scenario Models, markers, and optional Special Rules.
7. Determine duration, deployment, and army allowances.
8. Record the Primary and Secondary consequences for both sides.

Where appropriate, the players may select their Secondary Objective from two or three available choices.

The GM ordinarily selects the opposition's Secondary Objective according to its established interests.

### Standard Encounter

A normal campaign encounter uses:

- One Primary Mission.
- One PC Secondary Objective.
- One opposing Secondary Objective.
- Zero or one optional Special Rule.

### Major Encounter

An important military engagement, Act finale, or extended adventure may use:

- One Primary Mission.
- Up to two Secondary Objectives per side.
- Up to two optional Special Rules.

The GM should avoid combining more objectives than the participating forces can realistically pursue.

## 1.2 Independent Objectives

Secondary Objectives do not determine Primary victory unless the Primary specifically says otherwise.

Both sides may complete their own Secondaries.

A side that loses the Primary retains any Secondary success already achieved.

A Secondary Objective cannot be completed automatically by performing the same action required to complete the Primary.

If the two objectives would otherwise be identical, assign a separate Target, location, or task.

For example, an Extraction Primary that requires recovering a magical artifact cannot also award Recover Treasure simply for extracting that same artifact.

The GM must designate an additional treasure or select another Secondary.

## 1.3 Mission Actions

A Mission Action is a meaningful interaction with a scenario objective. A Unit may perform **at most one Mission Action per Round**, choosing either its **Movement activation** or its **Assault activation**, but never during Combat.

Unless a specific Mission states otherwise, the Unit must be unengaged, have an eligible Model within **1"** of the Target or Site, and be legally capable of interacting with it.

- **Movement Mission Action:** Replaces the Unit's normal Movement action for that activation. It cannot also Move, Disengage, or make another ordinary Movement action during the same activation. Its subsequent Assault activation remains available, if eligible.
- **Assault Mission Action:** Replaces the Unit's normal Assault action. It cannot be combined with an Assault Move, ranged or Spell Attack, or Charge during that activation. A Withdrawn Unit cannot perform an Assault Mission Action.
- **Combat Phase:** A Unit cannot use an ordinary Mission Action during Combat, even if a specific interaction is adjacent to an Engaged Unit.

Mission Actions normally require **no CP and no Skill check**. Eligible Unit or army Commands may still be used with their normal CP cost and timing; they do not grant an additional ordinary phase action unless explicitly stated. Only one ordinary Mission Action may be attempted by the same Unit in the Round.

### Character Skill Actions

A Primary or Secondary permitting a **Character Skill Alternative** allows an eligible Character to spend **1 CP** during its Movement activation to make the stated Skill check **in place of its normal Movement action**. The default Difficulty is **Normal (2 successes)** unless the Mission states otherwise.

On success, resolve the specified interaction; on failure, no progress is earned. The Character must satisfy the interaction's normal range and positioning requirements unless that Mission explicitly permits a remote effect. A Character cannot advance the same interaction twice during the Round through its Skill Alternative and a Mission Action. Other ordinary Movement-phase Skill Checks remain governed by the Structured Combat rules.

### Narrative Encounters

In Narrative Play, Mission Actions represent equivalent meaningful actions or scene interactions. The GM determines whether positioning, preparation, or a Skill check is needed. Where an objective measures progress by Round, use a comparable meaningful Narrative Exchange or encounter stage.
## 1.4 Secondary Progress

Some Secondary Objectives require multiple successful actions.

Each successful action generates 1 Progress unless otherwise stated.

A side may gain **at most 1 Progress toward the same Secondary Objective per Round**.

Several Units may attempt the action, but only the first successful contribution can increase its Progress that Round.

Progress persists unless an opposing action or Special Rule explicitly removes it.

The side completing an objective records one Secondary Success.

A completed Secondary cannot be completed again for additional rewards during the same encounter.

## 1.5 Carried Objectives

Some objectives must be recovered and transported.

An eligible Unit may carry one Scenario Object at a time.

A carrier:

- May move normally.
- Cannot Charge.
- May perform other legal actions.
- Drops the object if defeated.
- May voluntarily drop the object during its activation.

An unengaged Unit within 1" of a dropped object may recover it using a Mission Action.

An object is extracted when its carrier reaches the appropriate Extraction Area and satisfies the Primary or Secondary's conditions.

A carried objective does not count as a Unit for objective control.

### Escorting Living Targets

Portable objects and living Targets are distinct. A Relic, document, supply cache, or similar item uses **Carried Objective** rules. A prisoner, civilian, creature, or caravan uses a **Scenario Model** or a designated escorting Unit with its own Movement, Wounds, and survival conditions; it is not literally picked up or dropped as a token. A Mission must state how a living Target follows an escort, can be transferred, and reaches an Exit. If an ordinary Unit is the designated Target, it retains its normal activation rules subject to the Mission's listed restrictions.

## 1.6 Scenario Models

Some Missions use noncombatants, vehicles, creatures, or other Scenario Models.

Their profiles must be established before the encounter.

Default noncombat Escort Target:

**M4" / W10 / Armor 5+ / Evade — / Ward — / no Attacks.**

A Scenario Model does not automatically activate like a combat Unit.

Its Mission specifies when and how it moves, interacts, or suffers damage.

Destroying a Scenario Model does not automatically award VP unless the Mission specifically provides for it.

## 1.7 Mission Duration and Victory

Each Primary Mission specifies its normal duration.

Ordinary tactical Campaign Missions generally last five to seven Rounds.

The Primary determines when the encounter ends and which side wins.

Unlike Matched Play, Campaign Missions do not automatically use the five-objective scoring system or the 10-VP Decisive Victory threshold.

A Primary with a physical victory condition ends when that condition is fulfilled, unless the Mission specifies an end-of-Round check.

Unfinished Secondary Objectives fail when the encounter ends.

Previously completed Secondary Objectives remain successful.

# 2. Primary Missions

Primary Missions represent six fundamental RPG encounter archetypes.

| **Primary**        | **Archetype**                       | **Central Goal**                                |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| P1 — Confrontation | Battle, duel, monster hunt          | Defeat the opposition or designated threat      |
| P2 — Defense       | Siege, protection, holdout          | Capture or preserve a defended position         |
| P3 — Expedition    | Exploration, investigation, dungeon | Investigate and resolve a discovery             |
| P4 — Extraction    | Heist, rescue, recovery             | Acquire and remove a Target                     |
| P5 — Passage       | Escort, pursuit, retreat            | Move designated forces through a dangerous area |
| P6 — Negotiation   | Diplomacy, political dispute        | Secure an agreement or concession               |

## P1 — CONFRONTATION

**Battle, Duel, or Monster Hunt**

**Default Duration:** 5 Rounds.

### Setup

Choose one Confrontation variant.

**Open Battle:** Both sides are attempting to defeat the opposing force.

**Targeted Confrontation:** One side must defeat a designated Character, Monster, or Unit.

The GM identifies the designated Target before deployment unless a Hidden Information Special Rule applies.

### Open Battle Rules

Track the starting Points of each side's participating army, including Units assigned to Reserves.

At the end of Round 5, calculate the **percentage of the opposing starting army's Points lost** by each side. A defeated single-model Unit contributes its entire purchased Points cost; an un-Defeated single-model Unit contributes no defeated Points from its accumulated Damage. For a multi-model Unit, use its purchased total Points multiplied by `(number of Models defeated at the end of battle ÷ starting Model count)`. This includes paid weapons and upgrades proportionally and also applies to Double Formations. Un-Defeated remaining Models with partial Wounds do not contribute fractional Model losses. Track actual surviving Models after any legally resolved reinforcement; Units still in Reserve count in the starting army but not as defeated merely because they did not enter.

The side that defeated the greater percentage wins.

A side also wins immediately if every opposing combat Unit has been Defeated and the opponent has no eligible forces remaining.

If the percentages are equal, the encounter is a Draw.

For casualty-based victory, a Downed Character counts as defeated, but uses the normal Critical Injury and recovery rules afterward.

### Targeted Confrontation Rules

The attacking side wins when the designated Target is defeated.

The defending side wins if the Target survives until the end of Round 5.

Defeating unrelated Units does not automatically complete the Primary.

### Recommended Secondaries

Eliminate Officer, Track Quarry, Protect VIP, Gather Intelligence, or Hold Strategic Position.

### Campaign Notes

Confrontation may resolve military supremacy, a contested challenge, the removal of a significant threat, or an eligible Warfare Stake.

Winning does not automatically grant territory or additional formations.

## P2 — DEFENSE

**Holdout, Siege, or Protection**

**Default Duration:** 6 Rounds.

### Setup

Designate one Primary Position.

One side is the Attacker; the other is the Defender.

The Defender begins in control of the Primary Position.

### Capture Rules

Beginning with Round 2, record control of the Primary Position during each Scoring Phase.

The Attacker captures the position by controlling it at the end of two consecutive Scoring Phases.

The sequence is interrupted if the position becomes Contested or Defender-controlled.

### Victory

The Attacker wins by capturing the position.

The Attacker also wins if it controls the position at the end of Round 6.

The Defender wins if neither condition is achieved.

### Recommended Secondaries

Protect VIP, Secure Supplies, Rescue Captives, Disable Defenses, or Disrupt Ritual.

### Campaign Notes

Defense can represent holding a settlement, assaulting a fortress, securing a strategic route, or protecting a political institution.

The actual strategic consequences depend on the established Stake.



---



## P3 — EXPEDITION

**Exploration, Investigation, or Dungeon Delving**

**Default Duration:** 6 Rounds or equivalent Narrative stages.

### Setup

Place three Investigation Sites and one central Discovery.

The Discovery begins Locked.

### Investigation Rules

An eligible Unit may investigate a Site using a Mission Action.

A Character may instead attempt an appropriate Awareness, Survival, or Mysticism check through a Character Skill Action.

Each Site can contribute one completed Investigation per side.

A side may complete at most one Investigation per Round.

Once a side has completed two different Investigation Sites, it unlocks the Discovery for itself.

An eligible Unit may then perform a Resolve Discovery Mission Action at the Discovery.

### Victory

The Exploring side wins by resolving the Discovery before the encounter ends.

The opposition wins if it prevents this.

If both sides are competing Explorers, the first to resolve the Discovery wins.

### Narrative Play

Investigation Sites may instead represent separate rooms, clues, NPC encounters, or exploration challenges.

The required actions are resolved through appropriate Narrative scenes.

### Recommended Secondaries

Recover Treasure, Uncover Secrets, Explore Hidden Site, Track Quarry, Survive Environment, or Attune Site.

### Campaign Notes

Completing an Expedition may resolve an unexplored Site, recover an established Discovery reward, or reveal information relevant to the strategic map.

Ordinary occupation of the Site does not automatically complete it.



---



## P4 — EXTRACTION

**Heist, Rescue, or Recovery**

**Default Duration:** 6 Rounds.

### Setup

Designate:

- One Primary Target.
- One Target Starting Location.
- One or more Extraction Areas.
- One Retrieving side.
- One Guarding side.

### Acquire Target

An unengaged Unit within 1" of the Target may perform an Acquire Mission Action.

The Unit becomes its carrier or escort.

Apply the normal Carried Objective rules for portable Targets, or the appropriate Scenario Model / Escort rules for living Targets. Establish which applies at setup.

If a portable carrier is Defeated, its Target is dropped. Living Targets instead follow their printed capture, defeat, or escort rules.

The Guarding side may recover the dropped Target and transport it back to its designated Starting Location.

### Victory

The Retrieving side wins by extracting the Target before the Round limit.

The Guarding side wins if extraction is prevented.

The Mission may specify that a Target must be recovered intact rather than destroyed.

### Recommended Secondaries

Recover Treasure, Rescue Captives, Gather Intelligence, Sabotage Target, or Expose Conspiracy.

### Campaign Notes

Extraction supports robberies, prison breaks, retrieving documents, recovering artifacts, and rescuing important people.

The Target's identity determines the potential campaign consequences.



---



## P5 — PASSAGE

**Escort, Journey, Pursuit, or Retreat**

**Default Duration:** 7 Rounds.

### Setup

Establish:

- One Passage Target.
- One Starting Location.
- One Exit Area.
- An eligible route between them.
- One Traveling side and one Intercepting side.

### Noncombat Target Movement

A noncombat Passage Target advances up to its Move value during the End Step if:

- At least one friendly unengaged escort Unit is within 6".
- No enemy Unit is Engaged with the Target.
- Its movement path is legal and unobstructed.

Otherwise, it does not advance.

The Target follows the designated route. For Pursuit and Retreat variants using ordinary combat Units, those Units use their own legal activations rather than the End Step movement specified for noncombat Scenario Models.

It does not make ordinary Assault Moves or Charge unless a specific rule permits this.

### Victory

The Traveling side wins by moving the Passage Target into the Exit Area before the Round limit.

The Intercepting side wins if the Target is destroyed or prevented from escaping.

### Variants

**Escort:** Use a noncombat Scenario Model.

**Pursuit:** Use an ordinary moving Unit as the Target.

**Retreat:** Designate up to three Priority Units. At least two must escape, or all if fewer than two are designated.

**Multiple Escort:** Use two or more Passage Targets with an explicit required number of successful extractions.

### Recommended Secondaries

Protect VIP, Secure Supplies, Rescue Captives, Gather Intelligence, Sabotage Target, or Survive Environment.

### Campaign Notes

Passage represents convoys, refugees, supply movements, escapes, military withdrawals, and dangerous journeys.

A normal M4" Passage Target should ordinarily have a route no longer than 24" for a seven-Round encounter if one stalled Round is intended to remain survivable.



---



## P6 — NEGOTIATION

**Diplomacy, Bargaining, or Political Conflict**

**Default Duration:** 5 Exchanges.

### Setup

Identify:

- The neutral party or institution being persuaded.
- The two competing positions.
- The agreement or concession being contested.
- The principal Negotiator for each side.

Negotiation normally uses Narrative Play.

### Negotiation Exchanges

During each Exchange, each side presents its arguments, evidence, demands, or concessions.

Each principal Negotiator rolls a number of D6 equal to the relevant Characteristic, counting each die meeting the appropriate Skill threshold as one success. This is an ordinary contested Skill Check; use the normal Surge and Blunder rules when applicable.

The usual Difficulty is Normal (2 successes).

If only one Negotiator succeeds, that side gains 1 Influence.

If both succeed, the side with more successes gains 1 Influence.

If both achieve the same number of successes, neither gains Influence.

If neither succeeds, neither gains Influence.

Each side can gain at most 1 Influence per Exchange.

### Victory

The first side to reach 3 Influence wins.

If neither reaches 3 after five Exchanges, the side with more Influence wins.

A tie means no new agreement is secured and the existing arrangement remains in place.

### Supporting Actions

A Character may undertake an appropriate supporting action instead of acting as the principal Negotiator.

A successful supporting action may reveal useful information, satisfy a Secondary Objective, or provide a declared situational benefit (for example, one additional die on a subsequent Negotiation Skill Check) at the GM's discretion. State the permitted benefit before the supporting roll.

Supporting actions do not automatically award extra Influence.

### Recommended Secondaries

Win Local Support, Gather Intelligence, Expose Conspiracy, Uncover Secrets, Protect VIP, or Recover Treasure.

### Campaign Notes

Negotiation can resolve treaties, faction relationships, recruitment rights, institutional decisions, and other political objectives.

Political agreements must remain consistent with the Campaign's eligibility, sponsorship, and institutional rules.

# 3. Secondary Objectives

Each side normally receives one Secondary Objective.

These are grouped into six thematic categories based on common fantasy RPG tropes.

| **Category**             | **Objectives** |
| ------------------------ | -------------- |
| Treasure & Discovery     | S1–S3          |
| Rescue & Relationships   | S4–S6          |
| Intrigue & Subterfuge    | S7–S9          |
| Warfare & Logistics      | S10–S12        |
| Wilderness & Monsters    | S13–S15        |
| Magic & the Supernatural | S16–S18        |
| Narrative Secondaries    | GM-identified, not numbered |

Secondary success is independent of Primary victory.

Campaign consequences listed below are examples of appropriate Stakes, not automatic rewards.

## A. Treasure & Discovery

### S1 — Recover Treasure

**Setup:** Place a Treasure marker separate from the Primary Target.

**Objective:** Recover and extract the Treasure.

**Rules:** An eligible Unit may collect the marker using a Mission Action. The marker becomes a Carried Objective.

**Completion:** Deliver the Treasure to a designated friendly Extraction Area.

**Opposition:** Enemy Units may defeat the carrier, intercept it, or recover the dropped marker.

**Campaign Notes:** The PCs might recover specified Gold, equipment, or a discovery. The opposition might preserve or seize the same asset.

### S2 — Uncover Secrets

**Setup:** Place two Clue markers at separate locations.

**Objective:** Investigate both Clues.

**Rules:** Each Clue requires a Mission Action or an appropriate Character Skill Alternative.

A side may investigate only one Clue per Round.

**Completion:** Both Clues are successfully investigated.

**Opposition:** Enemy forces may deny access, engage investigators, or prevent the required interactions.

**Campaign Notes:** Success might reveal plans, hidden routes, identities, or other information. The opposition may uncover the company's secrets or protect its own.

### S3 — Explore Hidden Site

**Setup:** Place three Potential Site markers. Secretly designate one as the True Site.

**Objective:** Discover and explore the True Site.

**Rules:** Surveying a Site requires a Mission Action or an appropriate Character Skill Alternative.

Surveying reveals whether the Site is genuine.

Once identified, the True Site requires one additional Explore Mission Action.

**Completion:** Identify and explore the True Site.

**Opposition:** Enemy Units may prevent access or undertake their own search if assigned an appropriate objective.

**Campaign Notes:** The winning side might discover a new location, route, hidden settlement, or future opportunity.



---



## B. Rescue & Relationships

### S4 — Rescue Captives

**Setup:** Place a Captive marker at a guarded location.

**Objective:** Free and extract the Captives.

**Rules:** An eligible Unit may free the Captives using a Mission Action.

The Captives then become a Carried Objective escorted by that Unit.

**Completion:** Extract the Captives through a designated friendly Exit.

**Opposition:** The enemy may intercept the escort, recover dropped Captives, or prevent their release.

**Campaign Notes:** The PCs may rescue allies, specialists, prisoners, or civilians. The opposition may preserve hostages or recover its own captured personnel.

### S5 — Protect VIP

**Setup:** Designate one friendly VIP and one relevant Meeting or Objective Site.

**Objective:** Complete the VIP's required participation while preserving their safety.

**Rules:** The VIP must reach the Site and remain there through one Scoring Phase, or perform an equivalent meaningful Narrative action.

The VIP must remain undefeated until the encounter ends.

**Completion:** Fulfill the participation requirement and preserve the VIP.

**Opposition:** Enemy forces may attack, capture, isolate, or prevent the VIP from reaching the required location.

**Campaign Notes:** Success might preserve a political relationship, important witness, specialist, or military leader.

### S6 — Win Local Support

**Setup:** Designate one Neutral Delegation or local group.

**Objective:** Secure its cooperation.

**Rules:** An eligible Character may perform an Appeal action through an appropriate social Skill check.

Alternatively, an eligible Unit may perform a suitable Mission Action when physical assistance or protection is the basis of the appeal.

Each success adds 1 Support Progress, to a maximum of one per Round.

**Completion:** Accumulate 2 Support Progress.

**Opposition:** An opposing side pursuing the same group's support may also attempt Appeals. The first side to complete its requirement secures the group's support.

**Campaign Notes:** Success may provide a contact, local access, intelligence, or an established political opportunity. It does not automatically grant a formation or transfer political control.



---



## C. Intrigue & Subterfuge

### S7 — Gather Intelligence

**Setup:** Place one Intelligence Source at a designated location.

**Objective:** Recover useful information from the Source.

**Rules:** Perform a Gather Intelligence Mission Action in Movement or Assault, or an appropriate Character Skill Alternative during Movement.

Each successful interaction generates 1 Intelligence Progress.

**Completion:** Accumulate 2 Intelligence Progress.

**Opposition:** An enemy Unit may perform a Counterintelligence Mission Action at the same Source to remove 1 Intelligence Progress before completion.

**Campaign Notes:** The PCs might reveal an enemy operation, preparations, hidden assets, or future Task. The opposition may expose the company's plans.

### S8 — Sabotage Target

**Setup:** Designate one installation, mechanism, supply depot, or military asset.

**Objective:** Disable the Target.

**Rules:** An eligible Unit may perform a Sabotage Mission Action at the Target.

Each action contributes 1 Sabotage Progress.

**Completion:** Accumulate 2 Sabotage Progress. The Target becomes Disabled.

**Opposition:** Before completion, an eligible defending Unit may perform a Repair Mission Action to remove 1 Sabotage Progress.

**Campaign Notes:** Success may disable an established Improvement, interrupt preparations, or damage a named asset when the Campaign Stakes authorize that consequence.

### S9 — Expose Conspiracy

**Setup:** Place two Evidence markers and designate a Witness or Authority.

**Objective:** Gather evidence and present it.

**Rules:** Each Evidence marker requires one successful Investigation interaction, using an eligible Movement or Assault Mission Action or a permitted Movement-phase Character Skill Alternative.

After both are collected, an eligible Character must present the Evidence through a Mission Action or Narrative Exchange.

**Completion:** Recover both pieces of Evidence and successfully present them.

**Opposition:** Enemy forces may conceal Evidence, prevent interactions, or block access to the Witness.

**Campaign Notes:** Success may expose misconduct, undermine a political plot, or reveal a concealed faction relationship. It does not independently overturn an established political agreement.



---



## D. Warfare & Logistics

### S10 — Secure Supplies

**Setup:** Designate one Supply Cache.

**Objective:** Secure the Cache while controlling its location.

**Rules:** An eligible Unit performs a Secure Supplies Mission Action at the Cache.

The assigned side must then control the Cache during the same Round's Scoring Phase.

**Completion:** Successfully secure and control the Cache.

**Opposition:** Enemy forces may contest the Cache, engage the securing Unit, or force it away before scoring.

**Campaign Notes:** Success may preserve supply access, protect an existing logistical asset, or secure a specified reward connected to the operation.

### S11 — Disable Defenses

**Setup:** Designate one defensive emplacement or installation.

**Objective:** Neutralize the defense.

**Rules:** The defense may be disabled through two successful Mission Actions.

If it has a legal destructible profile, defeating it through combat also completes the objective.

Default destructible installation:

**W8 / Armor 5+ / no Evade or Ward.**

**Completion:** Disable or destroy the Target.

**Opposition:** Enemy forces may protect the installation, engage attackers, or prevent the required interactions.

**Campaign Notes:** Success may interrupt the operation of a fortification, gate, watchtower, or other established defensive asset.

### S12 — Hold Strategic Position

**Setup:** Designate one Strategic Position separate from the Primary Objective.

**Objective:** Maintain control of the location.

**Rules:** The assigned side must control the Position at the end of two consecutive Scoring Phases.

If either check finds the Position Contested or enemy-controlled, the sequence resets.

**Completion:** Hold the Position for two consecutive Scoring Phases.

**Opposition:** The enemy may contest or capture the Position to interrupt the sequence.

**Campaign Notes:** Success may represent a foothold, defensible staging area, observation post, or control of a critical route.



---



## E. Wilderness & Monsters

### S13 — Track Quarry

**Setup:** Place two Track markers linked to a concealed creature, route, or other quarry.

**Objective:** Identify the quarry's location.

**Rules:** Investigate each Track using Survival, Awareness, or an ordinary Mission Action.

Only one Track may be investigated per Round.

**Completion:** Investigate both Tracks and reveal the quarry or its route.

**Opposition:** Enemy forces may block access to Tracks or prevent investigation.

**Campaign Notes:** The PCs may discover a Monster's lair, migration route, or hidden trail. The opposition may track the company or locate the quarry first.

### S14 — Contain Threat

**Setup:** Designate one mobile Threat and an Escape Boundary.

**Objective:** Prevent the Threat from escaping.

**Rules:** The assigned side must keep the Threat from crossing its Escape Boundary until the end of Round 4 or the encounter, whichever occurs first.

Defeating the Threat also completes Containment if defeating it is not already the Primary Mission.

**Completion:** Maintain containment for the required duration or defeat the designated Threat through an independent objective.

**Opposition:** Enemy forces may free the Threat, disrupt containment, or interfere with the protecting Units.

**Campaign Notes:** Success may prevent a creature, magical hazard, or other threat from reaching nearby territory.

### S15 — Survive Environment

**Setup:** Place two Environmental Hazard markers and designate one Expedition Unit.

**Objective:** Overcome environmental dangers while preserving the Expedition Unit.

**Rules:** Each Hazard requires one Stabilize Mission Action or appropriate Survival-based Character Skill Alternative.

Only one Hazard may be stabilized per Round.

**Completion:** Stabilize both Hazards and keep the Expedition Unit undefeated until the encounter ends.

**Opposition:** Enemy forces and existing environmental effects may interfere with stabilization or threaten the Expedition Unit.

**Campaign Notes:** Success may preserve expedition resources, prevent an established logistical loss, or establish safe passage through dangerous territory.



---



## F. Magic & the Supernatural

### S16 — Disrupt Ritual

**Setup:** Designate one active Ritual Site.

**Objective:** Interrupt the Ritual.

**Rules:** Perform a Disrupt Mission Action during Movement or Assault, or an expressly permitted Movement-phase Mysticism Skill Alternative at the Site.

Each success adds 1 Disruption Progress.

**Completion:** Accumulate 2 Disruption Progress.

**Opposition:** Before completion, a defending Unit may perform a Channel Mission Action to remove 1 Disruption Progress.

**Campaign Notes:** Success may interrupt magical preparations, delay a supernatural event, or prevent an established magical consequence.

### S17 — Attune Site

**Setup:** Designate one magical Site.

**Objective:** Attune the Site and maintain control.

**Rules:** Perform a Channel Mission Action during Movement or Assault, or an expressly permitted Movement-phase Mysticism Skill Alternative to Attune the Site.

The assigned side must then control the Site during the following Scoring Phase.

**Completion:** Establish Attunement and maintain control through the required check.

**Opposition:** An opposing Unit may contest the Site or perform a Channel Mission Action to replace its Attunement before completion.

**Campaign Notes:** Success may provide magical information, access to a specific location, or another established supernatural opportunity.

### S18 — Seal Breach

**Setup:** Place two Anchor markers and one Breach marker.

**Objective:** Disable the Anchors and close the Breach.

**Rules:** Each Anchor requires one successful Disable Mission Action during Movement or Assault, or an expressly permitted Movement-phase Mysticism Skill Alternative.

Once both are Disabled, an eligible Unit may perform a Seal Mission Action at the Breach.

**Completion:** Disable both Anchors and Seal the Breach.

**Opposition:** Enemy forces may defend the Anchors, interrupt interactions, or prevent access to the Breach.

**Campaign Notes:** Success may contain a supernatural threat, stop further incursions, or prevent a magical disaster.

## G. Narrative Secondaries

Some recommended Secondaries are deliberately left narrative rather than tied to the Mission Action / Progress framework above. The GM identifies the specific target or scene at setup and judges completion directly, instead of tracking markers or Progress.

### Eliminate Officer

**Objective:** Defeat a designated enemy Officer — a Captain, named Leader, or another Unit with the Hero or Elite designation — identified by the GM at setup.

**Completion:** The designated Officer is defeated: Downed for a Character, or its last Model removed for a multi-model Unit.

**Opposition:** Enemy forces may shield, reposition, or reinforce the designated Officer.

**Campaign Notes:** Success may remove a named commander, disrupt enemy leadership, or satisfy an eligible Warfare Stake. It does not, by itself, cause the rest of the opposing force to rout or withdraw.

# 4. Optional Special Rules

Most encounters use zero or one optional Special Rule.

Important encounters may use two.

Special Rules should introduce tactical variety without unnecessarily increasing bookkeeping.

## Ambush

One side is designated the Ambusher.

The Ambusher uses the scenario's concealed, flanking, or delayed deployment locations.

The GM establishes which Units may begin concealed and when concealed positions are revealed.

Ambush does not automatically grant additional Units or Battlefield Edges.

## Fortified

One side begins with established defensive structures or prepared positions.

The GM places the required fortifications before ordinary terrain placement.

Fortifications follow their existing rules and use established profiles where necessary.

Fortified does not automatically grant an additional Battlefield Edge.

## Reinforcements

Designate eligible reserve Units and their arrival Rounds before deployment.

Reinforcements count toward the encounter's army allowance.

Unless the scenario explicitly overrides them, use the normal reserve deployment and activation restrictions.

The entry locations must be identified before battle.

## Hazardous Conditions

Introduce a defined environmental hazard such as heavy rain, dangerous water, smoke, unstable ground, or extreme conditions.

The GM establishes affected areas and effects before play.

Prefer existing Difficult Terrain, visibility, Damage, and Cover rules rather than inventing additional status conditions.

## Hidden Information

Designate concealed Targets, Sites, traps, or enemy positions before deployment.

The GM records the hidden information.

Revelation occurs when the stated discovery conditions are satisfied.

The GM may not relocate concealed objectives in response to player decisions.

## Moving Objective

Introduce a Scenario Model or object that moves according to a predetermined route, trigger, or movement profile.

The object's activation or End Step timing must be specified before the encounter.

## Unstable Magic

Designate one magical area or object.

At the beginning or end of specified Rounds, resolve a predetermined effect or roll on an established table of magical effects.

The possible results must be determined before play.

## Third Party

Introduce one independent force, creature, or group with its own limited objective and activation behavior.

The GM determines its behavior before the encounter.

Third-party Units do not automatically control objectives for either principal side.

Additional military strength must be accounted for in the encounter balance.

# 5. Campaign Stakes and Consequences

The tactical outcome of a Mission and its broader campaign consequences are separate.

The Mission determines what happened.

The Campaign rules determine what the result means strategically.

## 5.1 Primary Success

Primary success resolves the encounter's principal objective.

A successful mission may satisfy the requirements of an eligible political Stake, established Neutral Opportunity, exploration Site, or strategic operation.

However, success does not automatically grant:

- Political territory.
- Recruitment rights.
- An additional Company Order.
- Unspecified Gold or equipment.
- An additional Act marker.
- An additional Development reward.

These benefits require an established legal source.

## 5.2 Secondary Success

Secondary success is recorded separately for each side.

An encounter may produce:

| **Primary Winner** | **PC Secondary** | **Opposition Secondary** |
| ------------------ | ---------------- | ------------------------ |
| PCs                | Success          | Failure                  |
| PCs                | Failure          | Success                  |
| Opposition         | Success          | Failure                  |
| Opposition         | Failure          | Success                  |
| Either side        | Success          | Success                  |
| Either side        | Failure          | Failure                  |

Completing a Secondary may provide a limited tactical or narrative consequence, or an explicitly established strategic benefit.

A Secondary does not independently win a political Stake unless the campaign has expressly defined that action as the Stake's actual completion requirement. In that case, it should normally be treated as part of the Primary instead.

## 5.3 Recommended Consequence Scale

| **Consequence Type** | **Examples**                                                             | **General Use**                       |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| Tactical             | Disable an emplacement, protect a Scenario Model                         | Common                                |
| Narrative            | Rescue an NPC, uncover evidence, reveal information                      | Common                                |
| Limited Strategic    | Preserve an agreement, identify a future route, secure a named advantage | Occasional                            |
| Battlefield Edge     | Advantage in a specified future battle                                   | Only when explicitly awarded          |
| Material Reward      | Gold, equipment, named Unit, Asset                                       | Requires an established reward source |
| Political Stake      | Act marker and applicable Development reward                             | Resolved through the Campaign system  |

Opposing success should be meaningful without automatically undoing the PCs' Primary victory.

For instance, the PCs may capture a fortress while the opposition successfully evacuates an important commander or preserves a supply asset.

Both results remain true.

## 5.4 Political Stakes

The current political Stake, its target, and its eligibility requirements are established through the Campaign Council before the company commits its Order.

A successful PC mission takes precedence over abstract political resolution only when the PCs satisfy the Stake's actual requirements.

A participating faction may receive the colored Act marker for the Stake it legally wins.

A mission supporting an independent Grand Faction does not transfer that faction's marker to the PCs.

**Each Campaign Turn awards exactly one Act marker.**

Winning the political Stake awards its normal political recognition and Development reward where applicable.

Physical results such as territorial transfers, damaged infrastructure, and military losses are recorded separately according to what actually happened.

## 5.5 Neutral Opportunities

A Neutral Opportunity is an alternative strategic objective, not an ordinary encounter Secondary.

Successfully resolving an eligible Neutral Opportunity awards one gray Neutral Act marker for the Campaign Turn instead of the normal colored political result.

An eligible Treasury Opportunity awards **2 Gold per participating PC** as its full Treasury reward.

A Special Opportunity awards the specifically established equipment, formation, Asset, or other benefit.

An unrelated Secondary Objective does not automatically become a Neutral Opportunity or prevent a political faction from contesting the current Stake.

## 5.6 Company Orders

Campaign Missions may be undertaken through an appropriate Company Order.

The seven Company Orders are:

| **Order** | **Typical Mission Application**                  |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Explore   | Expedition, investigation, reconnaissance        |
| Commerce  | Contracts, escorts, economic negotiations        |
| Develop   | Training, crafting, special projects             |
| Construct | Infrastructure and construction operations       |
| Recruit   | Recruitment and replenishment                    |
| Politics  | Negotiation, alliances, political disputes       |
| Mobilize  | Military engagements, escorts, relief operations |

A Mission may include activities associated with several Orders without granting the company additional strategic actions.

For example, recovering supplies during a Mobilize battle does not automatically grant an additional Commerce or Recruit operation.

## 5.7 Casualties and Persistence

Campaign casualties follow the normal Character and Formation recovery rules.

Defeated non-PC formations use the established Destroyed, Mauled, and Scattered results.

PCs use their normal Critical Injury system.

Important enemy commanders, named formations, and special Assets remain persistent campaign entities where appropriate.

A completed Secondary may preserve or recover a named entity if that is the explicitly established objective.

# 6. Example Assembled Campaign Missions

## Example A — The Siege of Blackthorn Keep

**Primary:** Defense.

**Layout:** C9 — Stronghold Breach.

**PC Role:** Attacker.

**PC Secondary:** Rescue Captives.

**Opposition Secondary:** Secure Supplies.

**Special Rule:** Fortified.

### Primary Goal

Capture the Keep's designated Command Position.

The defenders win if it remains secure through Round 6.

### PC Secondary

Free prisoners in a separate holding area and extract them.

### Opposition Secondary

Secure a designated storehouse and maintain control through the required Scoring Phase.

### Campaign Consequences

PC Primary success resolves the eligible military Stake concerning the Keep.

PC Secondary success preserves the prisoners regardless of the Keep's fate.

Opposition Secondary success preserves its specified supplies or logistical advantage, even if the fortress is captured.

The two Secondaries do not generate additional Act markers.



---



## Example B — The Lost Reliquary

**Primary:** Extraction.

**Layout:** R2 — False Reliquaries.

**PC Secondary:** Uncover Secrets.

**Opposition Secondary:** Gather Intelligence.

**Special Rule:** Hidden Information.

### Primary Goal

Identify the true Relic among three Reliquaries and extract it.

### PC Secondary

Investigate two separate Clues explaining the Relic's history.

### Opposition Secondary

Investigate the company's preparations and recover information about its intended extraction route.

### Campaign Consequences

The Primary determines whether the PCs retrieve the Relic.

The PC Secondary may reveal information about an associated Site or future discovery.

The opposition may learn useful information even if it fails to prevent the Relic's recovery.



---



## Example C — The Merchant Caravan

**Primary:** Passage.

**Layout:** E2 — Running Battle.

**PC Secondary:** Secure Supplies.

**Opposition Secondary:** Eliminate Officer.

**Special Rule:** Reinforcements, using the layout's preallocated interception force.

### Primary Goal

Escort the caravan to its destination before the pursuing and intercepting forces stop it.

### PC Secondary

Secure a designated supply cache along the route.

### Opposition Secondary

Defeat a named caravan officer.

### Campaign Consequences

If the caravan escapes, its established commercial or political operation can proceed.

The PCs may also secure the supply cache if their Secondary succeeds.

If the opposition defeats the officer but fails to stop the caravan, the Primary still belongs to the PCs.

The loss of the officer may remain a meaningful persistent consequence.



---



## Example D — The Forbidden Excavation

**Primary:** Expedition.

**Layout:** A3 — Scattered Patrol.

**PC Secondary:** Recover Treasure.

**Opposition Secondary:** Disrupt Ritual.

**Special Rule:** Unstable Magic.

### Primary Goal

Investigate the excavation and resolve the central Discovery.

### PC Secondary

Recover and extract a separate treasure cache.

### Opposition Secondary

Interrupt an unrelated magical mechanism or research operation maintained by the PCs' allies.

### Campaign Consequences

The PCs may successfully complete the expedition while losing the treasure or allowing the enemy to interrupt allied magical preparations.

Each outcome is recorded separately.

# 7. Mission Preparation Checklist

Before the encounter begins, the GM should be able to answer the following:

1. What is the Primary Mission?
2. Which side is attacking, defending, traveling, retrieving, or negotiating?
3. What is the exact Primary victory condition?
4. How many Rounds or Exchanges are available?
5. Which Battlefield Layout is being used?
6. Where are the required Primary and Secondary markers?
7. What Secondary Objective belongs to each side?
8. What interactions are required to complete each Secondary?
9. What counterplay is available to the opposition?
10. Are any Special Rules active?
11. What happens if the PCs win the Primary?
12. What happens if the opposition wins the Primary?
13. What happens if either side completes its Secondary?
14. Does this Mission resolve a political Stake, Neutral Opportunity, or other established operation?
15. Are all military rewards, political consequences, and persistent losses accounted for under the Campaign rules?

The purpose of the Campaign Mission system is to create varied fantasy RPG and wargame encounters from a small set of reusable mechanics while preserving clear victory conditions, meaningful Secondary Objectives, and persistent strategic consequences.
