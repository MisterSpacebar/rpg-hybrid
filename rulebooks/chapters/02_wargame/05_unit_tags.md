# UNIT TAGS

*Hybrid RPG / Wargame — Revised Rules*

## 1. General Rules

**Unit Tags** identify a Unit's allegiance, battlefield classification, and intrinsic properties. They are referenced by Weapons, Talents, Commands, Spells, Faction Rules, objectives, and other abilities.

Each Unit has:

- **One Faction tag**, unless a specific rule states otherwise.
- **One or more Unit Type tags**, describing what the Unit is on the battlefield.
- **Any applicable Trait tags**, describing its nature, construction, or special properties.
- **Any explicitly assigned Role or Roster designations**, used for specialized interactions and army construction.

A Unit may have multiple applicable tags within a category. Tags are normally assigned to the **Unit as a whole**, including all of its Models.

A tag does **not** grant an ability, characteristic bonus, recruitment slot, or army-construction exemption by itself. Its effects arise only when another rule explicitly refers to that tag or designation. Repeating the same tag on a Unit provides no additional benefit.

A Unit's printed profile and explicitly purchased upgrades determine its tags. Its name, appearance, weapon selection, or Armor Save does not automatically grant an unlisted tag.

---

## 2. Faction Tags

A **Faction tag** identifies the army, organization, or allegiance to which a Unit belongs.

| Faction Tag | General Identity |
| --- | --- |
| Free Realms | Kingdoms, city-states, frontier armies, guilds, and mercenaries |
| Sacred Orders | Knightly orders, holy guardians, and religious armies |
| Iron Holds | Fortress kingdoms, industrial citadels, and clan armies |
| Wild Courts | Fey courts, wilderness armies, and allied forest peoples |
| Warclans | Warrior societies, tribal armies, and aggressive warbands |
| Underclans | Subterranean clans, scavengers, infiltrators, and raiders |
| Death Legions | Organized undead armies and necromantic kingdoms |
| Blood Courts | Vampiric dynasties and their retainers |
| Spirit Hosts | Spectral armies and supernatural processions |
| Infernal Hosts | Demonic legions and infernal cults |
| Arcane Orders | Magical academies, enchanted armies, and construct forces |
| Titan Clans | Ogres, giants, monstrous tribes, and war beasts |

Additional Faction tags may be introduced for minor armies, neutral creatures, campaign organizations, and other groups. **Unaligned** may be used as a Faction tag for independent creatures that do not belong to one of the twelve listed armies.

A Generic Unit recruited into a faction normally gains that faction's Faction tag. It retains its other printed tags and designations unless a faction rule explicitly changes them.

**Faction is not creature type.** A Unit with the *Death Legions* Faction tag is not automatically **Undead**, and a Unit with the *Wild Courts* Faction tag is not automatically **Fey**. Only the tags explicitly assigned to its profile apply. For example, mortal auxiliaries in an undead army may have `Death Legions, Mortal` rather than `Death Legions, Undead`.

Faction tags can be targeted directly, for example by **Anti-Death Legions 1**.

## 3. Unit Type Tags

**Unit Type tags** describe a Unit's physical or tactical form. A Unit may possess several Unit Types if its profile explicitly lists them.

| Unit Type | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Infantry** | Conventional foot troops, including ranged infantry and elite foot formations. |
| **Cavalry** | Mounted troops and equivalent mobile combat formations. |
| **Character** | Individual combatants, officers, Player Characters, Wizards, and important specialists. |
| **Support** | Personnel primarily providing healing, engineering, reconnaissance, or battlefield assistance. |
| **Artillery** | Crewed or stationary weapons designed for long-range fire support. |
| **Vehicle** | Mobile mechanical or constructed platforms, including wagons and tanks. |
| **Monster** | Exceptional creatures or similarly powerful living or supernatural battlefield threats. |
| **Construct** | Animated objects, golems, automatons, and magically or mechanically sustained beings. |

Unit Types may overlap. An individual Artificer might be `Character, Support`; an Arcane Golem could be `Construct, Monster` if its profile assigns both. A mounted Character could possess both `Character` and `Cavalry`.

**Monster** and **Beast** are different tags: Monster is a Unit Type, whereas Beast is a Trait. Neither automatically grants the other.

## 4. Trait Tags

**Trait tags** describe a Unit's nature, physical characteristics, supernatural origin, or construction.

| Trait | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Mortal** | Ordinary living humanoids and similar mortal creatures. |
| **Undead** | Reanimated corpses, skeletons, vampires, and beings explicitly classified as undead. |
| **Spirit** | Incorporeal or spiritual beings, including supernatural or undead spirits. |
| **Infernal** | Demons, devils, and creatures fundamentally sustained or empowered by infernal forces. |
| **Fey** | Creatures originating from supernatural wilderness realms or fey courts. |
| **Beast** | Animals, mounts, war beasts, and other predominantly instinct-driven creatures. |
| **Armored** | Units explicitly designated as protected by substantial armor, heavy plating, or equivalent equipment. |
| **Unarmored** | Units explicitly designated as lacking substantial armor — unarmed, lightly clothed, or relying on natural toughness alone. |
| **Mechanical** | Units primarily powered or operated through machinery, including certain Vehicles and Artillery. |

Additional Traits—such as **Elemental**, **Plant**, or **Swarm**—may appear when specific rosters or rules require them. An additional Trait has no inherent effect until a rule references it.

A Unit may have multiple Traits. For example, a spectral skeleton might be `Undead, Spirit`; a mechanized tank might be `Armored, Mechanical`.

### Armored

**Armored is an explicitly assigned Trait tag**, not a numerical Armor threshold.

- Improving a Unit's Armor Save does **not** automatically grant Armored.
- A Unit with Armor 5+ may be Armored when its profile explicitly says so.
- A Unit with Armor 3+ is not necessarily Armored if its protection comes from natural scales or supernatural effects and it lacks the tag.
- Heavy plate, reinforced shielding, or an equipment upgrade may explicitly **grant Armored**.
- Temporary improvements to Armor, Evade, or Ward do not add or remove Armored unless their rules explicitly say so.

**Anti-Armored X** checks for the Armored tag, not the target's Armor Save characteristic.

### Unarmored

**Unarmored is an explicitly assigned Trait tag**, the counterpart to Armored, not simply the absence of an Armor upgrade.

- A Unit is Unarmored only when its profile explicitly assigns the tag — typically a baseline infantry profile that has not purchased an Armor or Shield upgrade.
- Purchasing an upgrade that explicitly **grants Armored** removes Unarmored from that Unit; the two tags are mutually exclusive on the same Unit.
- A Unit with neither tag (for example, most Cavalry, Monsters, or Constructs) is simply untagged for this Trait — it is not automatically Unarmored merely because it lacks Armored.
- **Anti-Unarmored X** checks for the Unarmored tag, not a low Armor Save characteristic.

### Mechanical and Construct

**Mechanical** describes how a Unit functions; **Construct** describes its Unit Type. A mechanically powered automaton may have both, but a magical golem may be Construct without Mechanical. A Vehicle or Artillery Unit is not automatically Mechanical unless its profile assigns that Trait.

## 5. Role Tags and Roster Designations

Some existing army profiles use additional battlefield labels or army-list classifications. These remain meaningful but are **not interchangeable with Unit Type or Trait tags**.

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Core** | A designated Core Unit; counts toward the required number of Core Units in applicable army formats. |
| **Elite** | A higher-grade Unit or specialist. Elite does not imply Hero. Elite-subtype tags (Elite Infantry, Elite Cavalry, Elite Ranged Infantry) also satisfy Commands requiring an Elite Unit. |
| **Hero** | A designated Hero, subject to the army's Hero limits where applicable. |
| **Wizard** | A designated spellcasting Unit eligible for rules specifically referencing Wizards. |
| **Ranged / Ranged Infantry** | Identifies Units with a specialized ranged role; ordinary possession of a ranged weapon does not automatically change recruitment eligibility. |
| **Mobile / Infiltrator** | Specialized mobility or infiltration labels used where explicitly assigned. |
| **Headquarters** | A Unit eligible to satisfy the Headquarters requirement under its army-list rules. |
| **Large Asset** | A Unit counted against the Large Asset maximum in formats that impose that limit. |

These labels may be referenced by an ability or an Anti-[Type] keyword **when the matching designation is explicitly present**. They do not automatically grant the Unit any additional Recruitment Slot.

### Recruitment Slots Are Separate

The **H, C, E, R, V, S, and A** symbols are **Recruitment Slots**, not Unit Tags. Slot eligibility is determined by the army-construction rules and the Unit's explicitly listed eligible slots.

For standard 2,000-point matched play, the principal distinctions are:

- **C** can recruit Generic Line Infantry or a designated faction Core Unit, even if that faction Core Unit is ranged, elite, cavalry, or monstrous.
- **R** can recruit Generic Ranged Infantry or eligible faction ranged formations.
- **S** can recruit Generic non-Elite Supports or faction Wizards and Supports, including faction specialists with Elite.
- **E** can recruit Generic Elite Infantry, Generic Elite Wizards, the Generic Captain (a non-Hero **Elite Character**), and eligible faction Elite Units. A Generic Elite Wizard uses E rather than S.
- **H** may recruit the Generic Captain only once it has the Heroic Commission upgrade, which makes it a **Hero** and adds H eligibility while it keeps Elite.
- **V** recruits eligible Cavalry, mobile, and infiltrating formations; **A** recruits eligible Artillery and Assets as defined by their profiles.

The precise recruitment permissions and composition limits are controlled by the **Army Construction & List Building** chapter. A Unit with `Elite` or `Monster` does not receive an E or A slot automatically. Likewise, selecting a ranged weapon does not automatically add R eligibility.

**Hero, Core, and Large Asset** are also independent of the slot a Unit occupies. A designated Core Unit recruited using R still counts as Core; a Hero recruited using S still counts against the Hero limit; a Large Asset recruited through an eligible alternate slot still counts against the Large Asset limit.

## 6. Anti-[Type] X

An Attack with **Anti-[Type] X** gains **X additional Attack dice** when its target Unit possesses the specified Faction, Unit Type, Trait, or explicitly assigned matching Role designation.

Examples include:

- **Anti-Undead 1** — extra Attack die against Undead Units.
- **Anti-Armored 1** — extra Attack die against Units explicitly bearing Armored.
- **Anti-Monster 2** — two extra Attack dice against Monster Units.
- **Anti-Construct 1** — extra Attack die against Construct Units.
- **Anti-Infernal 1** — extra Attack die against Infernal Units.
- **Anti-Spirit 1** — extra Attack die against Spirit Units.
- **Anti-Character 1** — extra Attack die against Units explicitly tagged Character.
- **Anti-Death Legions 1** — extra Attack die against Units whose Faction is Death Legions.

### Multiple Applicable Anti Keywords

If more than one Anti-[Type] keyword matches the target, **use only the highest applicable X value** unless a special rule expressly allows stacking.

Multiple instances of the **same** Anti-[Type] keyword also use the highest value; they do not add together.

Anti-[Type] modifies **Attacks**, not Hit, Damage, Impact, or any Save. It does not change the Unit's underlying Tags.

### Combined Unit Attacks

For a combined multi-Model Attack using the same weapon profile, first total the ordinary Attack dice contributed by eligible Models, then apply the highest relevant **Anti-[Type] X once to the combined Attack pool**, not once per Model.

**Example:** Four Models each contribute two dice using a weapon with Anti-Undead 1. Against an Undead target, the combined Attack has **9 dice** (8 base + 1 Anti), not 12.

**Example:** An Attack has Anti-Undead 1 and Anti-Armored 2 against an Undead, Armored Unit. Only the stronger applicable bonus applies: **+2 Attack dice**, not +3.

If different weapons or Models must make separate Attack pools, determine each pool's applicable Anti keyword independently. An Anti bonus belonging only to one weapon does not transfer to other weapons.

## 7. Unit-Wide Keyword Ranks

A Unit's shared ranked keywords, such as **Accurate X**, **Critical X**, **Tough X**, **Cover X**, and **Brace X**, apply at their listed value **once to the appropriate Unit roll or effect**. They are not multiplied by the number of Models contributing to the roll.

If another eligible rule grants a compatible additional rank, add that rank to the existing value unless the keyword or granting rule expressly says otherwise. All normal restrictions still apply; in particular, a die cannot be rerolled more than once.

| Existing Keyword | Bonus Granted | Final Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accurate 1 | Accurate 2 | Accurate 3 |
| Critical 2 | Critical 1 | Critical 3 |
| Tough 1 | Tough 2 | Tough 3 |
| Cover 1 | Cover 1 | Cover 2 |
| Brace 2 | Brace 1 | Brace 3 |

For different weapon profiles in the same Unit, apply weapon-specific keywords only to the Attack pool using that weapon. A keyword on one Model's weapon does not automatically modify another weapon's Attack.

**Having the same unranked tag more than once is never additive.** For example, multiple sources granting Armored do not create Armored 2.

## 8. Mixed and Changing Tags

Tags normally apply to the **entire Unit**.

If a Unit contains Models with different Traits, Types, or Role designations, its profile or special rule must specify **which tags apply to the Unit as a whole** and which, if any, apply only to particular Models or Attacks. Do not automatically combine every Model's individual traits into extra Unit-wide tags.

A temporary change in a characteristic, equipment appearance, movement method, or Armor Save does not modify a Unit's tags unless an ability explicitly states that it grants, replaces, or removes a tag.

When an effect explicitly changes a tag, apply the change immediately to all relevant targeting, Attack, Command, and faction interactions for its stated duration. Unless stated otherwise, the tag returns to its printed state when the effect expires.

A rule that changes a Unit's Faction must expressly state whether the change replaces its previous Faction tag or permits multiple Faction tags. Merely controlling, commanding, or allying with a Unit does not automatically change its Faction tag.

## 10. Rules Reference and Compatibility

This chapter defines **which tags a Unit has and how those tags are checked**. It does not replace the detailed effects of Attacks, Saves, Charge, Impact, or other Keywords in the **Keywords** chapter.

When preparing or revising a datasheet, separately record:

1. Faction.
2. Unit Type(s).
3. Trait(s), including Armored only if explicitly assigned.
4. Role or roster designations, such as Core, Elite, Hero, Wizard, and Large Asset.
5. Eligible Recruitment Slot(s), where army construction is used.

Older references that infer **Armored** from Armor Save, treat **Hero** and **Character** as interchangeable, or grant Recruitment Slots automatically from Tags should be updated to this chapter's definitions.
