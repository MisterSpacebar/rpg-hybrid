# CORE FACTIONS & SIGNATURE ROSTERS

Hybrid RPG / Wargame — Matched-Play Revision v10

## General Roster Rules

All factions retain access to appropriately themed generic Units. A generic Unit recruited into a faction gains that faction's relevant type and benefits from its faction rules unless otherwise specified.

Signature Units provide specialized alternatives, not mandatory replacements. Most factions receive two signature Core choices, alongside one Captain, two Wizards/Support specialists, and one or more Large Assets.

### Recruitment Classification

All Units listed under **Signature Core Units** have the **Core** classification and may occupy **C**; alternative choices (R/V/E) are set by the **Signature Core Recruitment Reference** below. These alternative choices do not remove Core classification. Signature Captains and Faction Leaders use **H** (faction Captains are Heroes unless explicitly printed otherwise); faction Wizards and Supports use **S**, or **E** when they carry Elite. Designated Artillery and Large Assets normally use **A**. Where another category is specifically printed in a roster, use its listed eligibility.

The table grants eligibility even when a Unit's battlefield tag is narrower or uses different vocabulary (for example, Outriders' carbine-equipped mobility allows R or V). It does not turn these Units into generic Infantry or change their existing abilities.

Faction-specific Core may purchase the **Double Formation** option under Army Construction: double the starting Models and double the paid formation cost, retaining one Unit, one compatible Slot, one phase activation, and one Core classification. Unit-wide Keywords, Commands, and objective-control value do not automatically double.

**All Large Assets** listed beneath a faction's Large Assets heading count toward the Large Asset cap unless expressly exempted; a designated Core Monster is not automatically a Large Asset.

A faction's unique Captain generally knows two Commands: its listed signature Command and one eligible generic Captain Command. Unique specialists receive their listed abilities and, if designated Wizards, may select compatible generic Arcana where appropriate.

Every Unit capable of Engagement has a melee Attack. Ranged specialists without a listed melee weapon use Improvised Weapons: Engaged / 2 / 6+ / 1. In a combined Unit Attack pool, shared Accurate X and Critical X apply **once at the printed value**, not once per Model; compatible bonuses add to X unless explicitly excluded. Tough X, Cover X, and Brace X are likewise Unit-wide ranks, never multiplied by Model count.

**Command economy:** In 2,000-point matched play, an army has **8 CP per Round**, represented by its eight selected Faction and Doctrine Command cards. To use a selected card's Command, exhaust that card to spend its 1 CP; if a Command costs 2 CP, exhaust its card and one other available card. To use an eligible **personal Unit Command**, exhaust one or more available cards instead of using their printed Commands. **Card exhaustion is the CP expenditure, not an additional cost**. All eight CP and exhausted cards refresh at the start of each Round. Ordinary faction Commands cost 1 CP unless specified otherwise; major Faction Leader abilities generally cost 2 CP and are once per battle. Phase Commands of a Unit are used during that Unit's activation, Faction Commands when the side has priority, and Reactions at their triggers.

**RPG/campaign:** The selected eight-card repertoire exists only when the campaign rules specifically provide it. Each PC normally has **2 CP per Round**, with the GM receiving **max(2, 2 × PCs − 1)**; CP can be tracked without cards, especially early in a campaign. The campaign's unlocked Commands, not matched-play army construction, determine which abilities are available.

Profile notation: M / W / Arm / Eva / optional Ward. Weapon notation: Range / Attacks / Hit / Damage / Keywords. Attacks are per Model except for single-model war machines and assets.

**Reinforcement:** Healing never returns a defeated Model unless expressly stated. Ordinary active reinforcement affects only surviving, unengaged Units; restore basic Models into legal Coherency, never above starting size unless the ability expressly permits it. A completely destroyed Unit cannot return except by a rule such as **Rise Again** that specifically permits returning a destroyed Unit. Passive casualty recovery, such as **Endless Dead**, may function for a surviving Engaged Unit, but cannot return a Unit whose final Model was destroyed during the Attack. All costs are provisional.

# 1. FREE REALMS

Doctrines: Combined Arms / Objective Play

### Worldbuilding

A collection of kingdoms, republics, city-states, frontier settlements, guilds, and mercenary companies. Their armies rely on conventional soldiers supported by specialists, adventurers, allied peoples, and practical engineering.

The Free Realms emphasize cooperation between different battlefield roles rather than unusually powerful individual troops.

### Faction Rule — Combined Arms

A friendly Unit gains Accurate 1 when attacking an enemy already Engaged by another friendly Unit.

### Faction Commands

- Coordinated Advance — Movement 1 CP: Choose up to two friendly Units within Short. Each may immediately Move up to 2", ending legally.
- Covering Fire — Reaction 1 CP: After a friendly Unit Disengages, choose a different friendly Unit within Short of it that has not yet used its Assault activation. That Unit immediately makes an eligible ranged Attack against the enemy left behind, **consuming its normal Assault activation**. This does not grant a second Attack.
- Secure Ground — Movement 1 CP: One friendly Unit counts as one additional Unit for objective control until the end of the Round.

# 2. SACRED ORDERS

### Worldbuilding

Military religious orders, fortress-monasteries, holy knights, warrior-priests, and sacred guardians. Their warriors are unusually disciplined, but their armies are smaller and more expensive than conventional forces.

### Faction Rule — Unyielding

Once per Round, each friendly **Core or Elite Unit** reduces the first instance of Damage it suffers by 1, minimum 0. This applies after Saves and cannot reduce a Critical Injury. [**Playtest:** compare restricting this trait to these Units versus allowing all friendly Units.]

- Righteous Retaliation — Reaction 1 CP: After a friendly Unit survives an Melee Attack, it gains +1 Attack per Model on its next Combat activation against that attacker this Round.
- Heroic Intervention — Reaction 1 CP, Reaction: When a nearby ally is attacked, one friendly Character may Move up to 3" toward it.

# 4. WILD COURTS

Doctrines: Mobility / Control

### Worldbuilding

Elven enclaves, fey courts, forest spirits, beast clans, and ancient wilderness kingdoms. Their armies control where Engagements occur, relying on terrain, ambush, and unconventional movement.

- Entangling Growth — Assault 1 CP: One visible enemy within Medium suffers Slow 2 or cannot Charge during the current Phase.
- Fade into the Wilds — Reaction 1 CP, Reaction: After being attacked, one unengaged friendly Unit may Move up to 3".

# 14. BALANCE AND DESIGN NOTES

## Generic troops remain important

Signature troops generally cost more than their nearest generic equivalent or exchange conventional advantages for unique abilities.

A faction with elite ranged troops should still have a reason to use ordinary archers. A faction with enormous infantry should still benefit from cheap conventional soldiers.

This is particularly important for the Free Realms, Sacred Orders, Arcane Orders, and Titan Clans, which otherwise risk concentrating too much of their point budget into expensive specialists.

## Smaller and larger Unit sizes

The signature roster uses a wider spread of starting Model counts:

| Model Count | Typical Application                                       |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| 2           | Extremely specialized weapon teams or monstrous cavalry   |
| 3           | Elite infantry, durable specialists, conventional cavalry |
| 4           | Trained ranged troops, assault infantry, infiltrators     |
| 5–6         | Regular formations and larger specialized infantry        |
| 8           | Expendable infantry hordes                                |

Each unit must be balanced by total Wounds, defensive saves, attack output, mobility, and available special abilities—not merely by the number of Models.

## Characters and Command efficiency

Faction Captains generally cost 140–225 points and possess one distinctive Command beyond their improved basic characteristics.

Faction Wizards and Support specialists cost approximately 135–210 points. Their Commands spend CP represented by cards in matched play (not both resources separately), making their distinctive effects the principal reason to field them.

Faction Leaders are priced as major army investments. Their powerful Commands usually cost 2 CP and may be used only once per battle.

## Weapon and keyword consistency

- Accurate and Tough reroll only their specified number of natural 1s per combined Attack/Save pool; printed ranks do **not** multiply by Model count. Bonuses to the same applicable ranked Keyword are additive.
- Critical X converts up to X failed Hit dice into Critical Hits **after rerolls**, without requiring a Surge unless an ability explicitly says so; Critical Hits bypass Armor/Evade, but allow applicable Ward Saves.
- Anti-[Type] increases the Attacks characteristic against the designated category.
- Firearm inherently provides Instant.
- Heavy prohibits its Attack after voluntary movement during the preceding Movement Phase unless another ability explicitly overrides the restriction.
- Brace reduces incoming Charge Impact.
- Direct Damage bypasses Armor and Evade, but applicable Ward Saves still apply.
- Healing does not restore defeated Models unless a separate resurrection ability explicitly does so.
- All Units capable of Engagement possess a melee weapon, even if it is an improvised or weak secondary Attack.

## Priority Playtesting

The first balancing tests should compare signature troops directly against equivalently priced generic configurations, particularly:

- Greatweapon Guard versus Heavy Weapon Elite Infantry.
- Glade Hunters versus upgraded generic Longbowmen.
- Ironbreakers versus defensive Elite Infantry.
- Brutes versus Dual Weapon Elite Infantry.
- Eight-model hordes versus generic 10-model Infantry.
- Blood Knights versus upgraded Heavy Cavalry.
- The Aether Ballista Team versus Heavy Artillery.
- Signature Support Characters versus generic three-model W2 Support Units.

The large faction traits, Command Abilities, and unique Character interactions should then be tested in complete 2,000-point armies under the established CP and Doctrine-card economy.

## v4 Faction Damage-Output and Extra-Core Rebalance

Following the same post-playtesting concerns that prompted the Generic Units damage rebalance (units taking too long to inflict meaningful damage), this chapter received two coordinated changes:

**Extra Core parity.** Sacred Orders was the only faction with three Signature Core choices instead of two. Rather than reducing Sacred Orders, the other eleven factions each gained one additional Core (or, in a few cases, a different category) unit, bringing every faction to the same three-choice baseline. Each addition was chosen to **reinforce what the faction already does well** rather than patch a weakness: Free Realms gained a cooperative-fire Ranged Core (Vanguard Skirmishers) extending Combined Arms synergy; Iron Holds gained a second stationary-gunline Ranged Core (Deep Watch); Wild Courts gained an ambush-melee Infiltrating Core (Briar Stalkers); Warclans gained a Charge-focused Cavalry Core (Boar Riders); Underclans gained a Reserve-deploying Infiltrating Core (Tunnel Rats) reinforcing their Deep Strike doctrine; Death Legions gained an attritional Ranged Undead Core (Bone Archers); Blood Courts gained a mobile lifesteal-skirmisher Core (Night Stalkers); Spirit Hosts gained a ranged Spirit Core (Keening Archers); Infernal Hosts gained a disposable Horde Core (Cultist Horde) explicitly meant to feed Blood Offering/Dark Bargain; Arcane Orders gained a Construct Infantry Core (Animated Guardians) immune to Control effects; and Titan Clans gained a stationary ranged-support Monster Core (Stone Hurlers) reinforcing Heavy Support.
- **Horde-style units were excluded from the attack-profile changes below**, keeping the Models-not-Attacks design used for the three originally buffed Horde units (Choppa Mob, Corpse Horde) and the two horde-like Core units (Sneaks, Thralls): their weapon dice remain exactly as previously printed.

**Attack-profile rebalance.** Across every other Core, Captain, Wizard/Support, Large Asset, and Faction Leader profile in this chapter, Damage 1 weapons gained **+1 Attack**, while Damage 2+ weapons kept their listed Attacks and instead gained a thematic **Anti-[Type] 2** (or **3** on a handful of standout Large Asset/Leader weapons already carrying multiple keywords), chosen to reflect what that unit or faction already hunts or fears most (e.g. Anti-Armored on siege/engineering factions, Anti-Elite on duelist Captains and Leaders, Anti-Monster on anti-Large-Asset specialists). A few weapons that already carried an Anti-[Type] keyword instead had their Hit characteristic improved by one step for variety, consistent with the option to improve accuracy instead of volume or category coverage. Point costs are unchanged pending further playtesting.
