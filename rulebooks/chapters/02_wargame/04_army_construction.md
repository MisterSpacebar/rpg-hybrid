# ARMY CONSTRUCTION & LIST BUILDING

## 2,000-Point Matched Play — Revised Draft v7

These rules govern **standard 2,000-point matched-play army construction**, not the progression or Command acquisition of a normal RPG campaign. They use the same Units, Factions, equipment, and abilities as the wider system. Campaign play normally begins with 2 CP per player per Round, sometimes with **no physical Command cards or selected army repertoire**; later campaign rules may introduce those separately.

Army construction uses two separate resources:

- Army Points determine how many Units and upgrades an army can afford.
- Recruitment Slots determine which types of Units an army can recruit.

An army must satisfy both requirements.



---



# 1. Standard Army Size

A standard game uses armies of up to 2,000 Points.

Each army receives 15 Recruitment Slots through its Faction, two selected Doctrines, and Command selections.

Every purchased Unit must occupy a compatible Recruitment Slot and fit within the army's Points allowance.

Recruitment Slots represent permission to purchase Units. They do not provide Units for free, and players are not required to fill every slot.

### Standard Army Limits

| Requirement                     | Limit                                     |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Army Points                     | 2,000 maximum                             |
| Recruitment Slots               | 15                                        |
| Faction                         | Choose 1                                  |
| Doctrines                       | Choose any 2 distinct Doctrines           |
| Faction Commands                | Choose 2 of the faction's 4               |
| Doctrine Commands               | Choose 3 of 5 from each selected Doctrine |
| Headquarters                    | Minimum 1 (may be non-Hero)               |
| Core Units                      | Minimum 3                                 |
| Heroes                          | Maximum 3                                 |
| Named Faction Leaders           | Maximum 1                                 |
| Large Assets                    | Maximum 2                                 |
| Large Assets with Heavy Support | Maximum 3                                 |

All requirements apply simultaneously.

Additional Recruitment Slots do not override the Points limit, while unused Points do not allow the army to exceed its available Recruitment Slots.



---



# 2. Recruitment Slots

Every purchased Unit consumes one compatible Recruitment Slot, regardless of the Unit's cost, number of Models, or battlefield capabilities.

| Slot | Category        | Eligible Units                                                                               |
| ---- | --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| H | Headquarters | Generic **Elite Captain with the Heroic Commission upgrade** (a Hero), eligible Hero Wizard, faction Captains and named Leaders |
| C | Core | Generic **Line Infantry**, or **any faction-specific Unit designated Core**, even if ranged, cavalry, elite, or monstrous |
| E | Elite | Generic **Elite Infantry**, **generic Elite Wizard** and the **generic Captain**; faction Elite Units, including eligible Elite Wizards and Supports |
| R | Ranged | Generic **Ranged Infantry**, or eligible **faction Ranged Infantry**, including faction Ranged Core |
| V | Vanguard | Generic **Cavalry**; faction Cavalry, Mobile, Infiltrator, and designated Vanguard Units |
| S | Specialist | Generic **non-Elite Support**; faction **Support or Wizard**, including those with the Elite tag |
| A | Asset | Generic **Artillery**; faction Artillery, Vehicles, War Machines, and designated Large Assets |

### Multiple Recruitment Categories

A Unit may be eligible for more than one Recruitment Slot.

For example:

- **Every designated faction Core Unit** qualifies for C, regardless of its battlefield type; a faction Ranged Core may also qualify for R, and Core Cavalry may also qualify for V when its tags apply.
- A faction Elite Wizard or Elite Support qualifies for S **or E**; the **generic Elite Wizard** requires E rather than S.
- Elite Cavalry may qualify for E or V; a monstrous Core Unit qualifies for C, with A eligibility only when explicitly designated.

Such Units still consume only one Recruitment Slot.

Choose one compatible available slot when recruiting the Unit and record that assignment on the army list.

### Keywords and Recruitment Categories

Unit keywords and recruitment eligibility are related but separate.

Keywords such as Infantry, Cavalry, Elite, Hero, Monster, Undead, Armored, and Mechanical determine how Units interact with rules and abilities.

They do not automatically grant additional Recruitment Slot options.

Use a Unit's explicitly listed recruitment eligibility. Where none is specified, use its primary army-list category.

Examples:

- Generic Elite Infantry remains an E Unit when equipped with a Longbow or Musket. It does not automatically gain R eligibility.
- Faction Wizards and Supports with Elite may use **S or E**; ordinary generic non-Elite Supports use **S**, while generic Elite Wizards use **E**.
- A Hero Specialist still applies to the Hero limit, regardless of whether its assigned Slot is H, S, or E.
- A Monster does not automatically qualify for A unless its Unit profile or recruitment rules permit it.
- A **generic Captain starts as an Elite Unit (E) and is not a Hero**. The Heroic Commission upgrade makes it a Hero and adds H eligibility; it keeps Elite and may still be recruited through E.

### Large Assets Versus A Slots

Not everything recruited through A is a Large Asset.

Only Units with the Large Asset classification count toward the army's Large Asset maximum.

Monstrous Core Units may occupy eligible A slots without counting toward that limit, unless they also have the Large Asset classification.

# 3. Required Army Composition

Every standard army must contain at least:

- One Headquarters Unit.
- Three purchased Core-classified Units.

These requirements refer to purchased Units, not available Recruitment Slots.

### Core Units and Core Slots

The Core classification is not the same thing as a C Recruitment Slot.

A **faction-specific Unit explicitly designated Core** counts toward the army's three-Core minimum regardless of whether it occupies C, R, V, or another permitted Slot. **Generic Line Infantry** is also Core. A generic Ranged Infantry or Elite Infantry Unit is **not automatically Core** simply because it has Ranged or Elite tags.

For example, an army may satisfy its Core requirement with:

1. Generic Line Infantry recruited through C.
2. Faction Core Ranged Infantry recruited through R.
3. Faction Core Cavalry recruited through V.

All three count as Core Units when their profiles explicitly carry that classification.

Conversely, having several unused C slots does not satisfy the minimum requirement.

A Hero, Specialist, or Large Asset does not count as Core merely because it has the Infantry, Cavalry, or Monster keyword.

### Headquarters and Heroes

An army must recruit at least one Headquarters Unit.

An army may contain a maximum of three Heroes. **Generic Elite Captains without Heroic Commission do not count against this limit.**

This limit counts all purchased Units with the Hero classification, even if they occupy different Recruitment Slots.

For example, a **Heroic-Commission Captain** recruited through H and a designated **Hero faction Wizard** recruited through S both count toward the three-Hero limit; a standard Generic Captain without that upgrade does not.

The Hero maximum is separate from the availability of H slots. An army must still possess appropriate Recruitment Slots for every Hero it purchases.

### Faction Leaders

A faction may recruit its named Faction Leader as a Headquarters choice.

A Faction Leader:

- Costs its full listed Points.
- Uses one compatible Recruitment Slot, normally H.
- Counts toward the Hero limit if classified as a Hero.
- May be recruited only once.

A Faction Leader is not mandatory. A **generic Captain with Heroic Commission** or an eligible faction Captain may satisfy Headquarters; a generic Captain without Heroic Commission is an E Unit and cannot. A named or faction Hero Captain still counts against the normal Hero cap.

### Large Assets

An army may normally recruit up to two Large Assets.

An army selecting the Heavy Support Doctrine may recruit up to three Large Assets.

This increases the composition limit only. It does not provide a free Unit or additional Recruitment Slot beyond those normally granted by Heavy Support and its selected Commands.

An army must still possess sufficient A or other explicitly compatible Recruitment Slots and enough Points to purchase its Large Assets.

# 4. Choosing a Faction

Choose one Faction before purchasing Units.

Each Faction provides:

1. A Faction Rule.
2. Two Faction Foundation Recruitment Slots.
3. Four Faction Commands, of which two are selected.
4. Access to its signature Units and Characters.
5. Access to appropriately themed Generic Units.
6. Suggested Doctrines representing its traditional battlefield strategies.

### Faction Rules

A Faction Rule represents a persistent or conditional advantage available to Units belonging to that Faction.

Eligible Generic Units recruited into a faction benefit from its Faction Rule unless the rule specifies otherwise.

Signature Units provide specialized alternatives to Generic Units rather than replacing them.

# 6. Choosing Doctrines

Each army selects exactly two distinct Non-Faction Doctrines.

Any faction may select any two of the sixteen available Doctrines.

There are no Favored Doctrine requirements, Compatible Doctrine restrictions, or restrictions on mixing particular Doctrines.

The same Doctrine cannot be selected twice.

### Doctrine Foundations

Every Doctrine automatically grants one Foundation Recruitment Slot.

An army receives the Foundation Slot of each of its two chosen Doctrines.

### Doctrine Commands

Every Doctrine provides five selectable Commands.

Choose three Commands from each selected Doctrine.

Each chosen Command:

- Grants one Recruitment Slot of the indicated type.
- Becomes available for use during battle.
- Uses the timing, CP cost, and effects in its rules.

Unselected Commands grant neither a Recruitment Slot nor their battlefield ability.

### Complete Doctrine Recruitment Table

The five Command Slots follow the order of the Commands in the Non-Faction Doctrine rules.

### Doctrine Commands and Recruitment

The Recruitment Slot granted by a Command does not determine which Units may benefit from that Command.

For example, selecting Elite's Perfect Technique grants an E Recruitment Slot, but the ability can enhance any friendly Unit that meets its stated targeting conditions.

Likewise, selecting Firing Line does not require every Unit in the army to be Ranged Infantry.

Doctrines influence army composition through their recruitment options while remaining broadly useful to different combinations of Units.

# 7. Calculating Recruitment Slots

Every army begins with three Universal Recruitment Slots:

H, C, C

Additional Recruitment Slots are provided by the Faction, Doctrines, and selected Commands.

### Recruitment Breakdown

| Source                                           | Slots |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ----- |
| Universal Foundation                             | 3     |
| Faction Foundation                               | 2     |
| First Doctrine Foundation                        | 1     |
| Second Doctrine Foundation                       | 1     |
| Two selected Faction Commands                    | 2     |
| Three selected Commands from the first Doctrine  | 3     |
| Three selected Commands from the second Doctrine | 3     |
| Total                                            | 15    |

Thus:

3 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 2 + 3 + 3 = 15 Recruitment Slots

Every standard army receives fifteen Recruitment Slots, regardless of its Faction or Doctrine pairing.

However, the distribution of those slots varies according to the player's choices.

### Using Recruitment Slots

A purchased Unit must occupy one compatible, unoccupied Recruitment Slot.

Recruitment Slots of the same type are interchangeable regardless of which Faction or Doctrine ability provided them.

For example, four C slots allow up to four eligible C Units, regardless of where the four slots originated.

### Unused Recruitment Slots

Recruitment Slots may be left empty.

Unused slots:

- Do not provide additional Points.
- Cannot be exchanged for different categories.
- Do not have to be filled before battle.
- Do not prevent their associated Commands from being used.

An army may therefore have fifteen available slots but recruit fewer than fifteen Units.

### Multiple Slot Eligibility

A Unit with multiple eligible Recruitment Categories may use any one compatible available slot.

For example, a faction-specific Core Ranged Infantry Unit eligible for C or R may occupy whichever slot the player prefers.

A Unit never occupies two Recruitment Slots unless an exceptional rule explicitly requires it.

# 8. Purchasing Units

After selecting the Faction, Doctrines, and Commands, purchase Units from the Generic and Faction rosters.

For each Unit:

1. Choose a Unit profile.
2. Check its recruitment eligibility.
3. Assign it one available compatible Recruitment Slot.
4. Pay its base Points cost.
5. Purchase any permitted additional Models, weapons, equipment, or upgrades.
6. Add all upgrade costs to the Unit's total.
7. Record the Unit's final characteristics, keywords, weapons, and abilities.

A Unit cannot be purchased without an appropriate Recruitment Slot, even when the army has enough unspent Points.

### Generic Units

Generic Units provide conventional, customizable battlefield profiles.

They are available to appropriate Factions unless otherwise restricted.

A Generic Unit retains its purchased characteristics and equipment while gaining applicable Faction traits and keywords.

Generic Units often provide cheaper or more flexible alternatives to specialized Faction troops.

### Double Formation — Faction Core Only

A purchased **faction-specific Core** Unit may be upgraded to a **Double Formation** when its Unit profile does not prohibit this option:

- Double its **starting Model count** and pay **twice the standard formation's total Points**, including any selected packages or fixed-price Unit-wide upgrades. For example, Greatweapon Guard (5 Models / 145 Points) becomes **10 Models / 290 Points** before any further permissible variations.
- The doubled group is **one Unit** occupying **one compatible Recruitment Slot** and counts as **one Core Unit** for the Core minimum; it has **one activation per eligible Phase** and counts as **one Unit** for objective control.
- Its individual Models retain their printed Wounds and Attacks. The Unit's ranked Keywords and effects (Accurate X, Critical X, Tough X, etc.) **are not doubled**; additional compatible bonuses add to their ranks normally. A Command that grants +X Attacks to one Unit's next Attack adds X once, unless it expressly says **per Model**.
- The doubled starting size is used to calculate thresholds, replenishment caps, and any restoration limited to the Unit's starting Models.
- A Core Unit may use this option only once. Other added-Model options require explicit permission and their usual Points.

This upgrade does not apply to generic Line Infantry, even though they are Core; those units use their own Additional Model options.

### Faction Units

Faction-specific Units provide distinctive battlefield roles, equipment, special abilities, and statlines.

They use their printed Points costs and recruitment eligibility.

Faction Units do not automatically replace Generic Units or override the normal composition limits.

### Equipment and Upgrades

The final Points cost of a Unit is:

Base Cost + Equipment + Additional Models + Other Upgrades

Unless otherwise stated:

- Equipment must be selected from the Unit's permitted options.
- Weapon packages generally apply to every eligible Model.
- Extra Models cost their listed Points.
- Upgraded Generic Units pay additional equipment surcharges for extra Models where specified.
- All upgrades must be paid for before battle.
- Weapons and options cannot be changed during battle unless an ability explicitly permits it.

A Unit possessing multiple weapon profiles does not automatically attack with all profiles simultaneously.

### Duplicate Units

Ordinary Units may be purchased multiple times when enough Points and compatible Recruitment Slots are available.

A Unit with the **Unique** tag is limited to one copy of its named profile per army. Named Faction Leaders have Unique. This restriction is independent of the Hero limit and recruitment slot.

Specific Faction or scenario rules may impose additional restrictions.

### Reinforcement, Model Restoration, and Exceptional Return

- **Heal X** removes accumulated Damage from a surviving Model or Unit; it does **not** return defeated Models unless specified.
- **Ordinary reinforcement** returns defeated basic Models to an existing, surviving Unit, never above its **starting Model count** unless the rule expressly permits it. Such active reinforcement cannot target an **Engaged Unit**, and new Models must be placed legally in Unit Coherency. Restored Models do not generate additional activations that Round.
- A **completely destroyed Unit** cannot normally be returned by healing, reinforcement, or resurrection. Only an ability explicitly saying it can return a destroyed Unit (such as Death Legions' **Rise Again**) is an exception. A restored Unit continues occupying its original Recruitment Slot; it is not purchased again.
- A passive casualty-return effect that explicitly resolves **during an Attack**, such as **Endless Dead**, may return Models to a surviving Engaged Unit, provided the wording allows it and placement is legal. It cannot save or revive a Unit whose last Model was destroyed during that Attack unless it expressly says so.
- If a rule grants new Models **above starting size** (such as **Call Retainers**), it must list its maximum. For Call Retainers, the maximum is **starting size +2 Models**; its target must still be surviving and unengaged.
- An ability that restores or creates Models never adds a separate Unit or activation unless explicitly stated.

### Reserves

Units beginning in Reserve must still be purchased normally.

They count toward:

- The army's Points limit.
- Its Recruitment Slot usage.
- Its Hero and Large Asset limits.
- Its Core minimum, where applicable.

Units in ordinary Reserve and Deep Strike Reserve together obey the **normal Reserve limits in Structured Combat**; a Doctrine or Faction ability may change their arrival procedure but not those limits unless explicitly stated. Abilities that deploy purchased Units from Reserve do not provide free Units.

Summoned or newly created Units follow their specific rules.

# 9. Command Abilities During Battle

The Faction and Doctrine Commands selected during army construction become part of the army's available tactical abilities.

They do not automatically grant additional activations or additional Command Points.

### Command Points and Selected Cards

In **2,000-point matched play**, each army has **8 CP per Round**, refreshed at the beginning of the Round; unused CP do not carry over. The army's **eight selected Faction and Doctrine Commands** are represented by **eight available Command cards** in physical play. The repertoire is not limited to a two-card prepared hand.

Each 1 CP expenditure is represented by **exhausting one available card**, not by both paying CP and spending a second card resource. Exhaust a card to use **its own printed Command**, or exhaust an available card to pay for an eligible **personal Unit Command** instead of that card's printed effect. A 2 CP ability exhausts **two** available cards and resolves just one effect. Exhausted cards cannot be used again until they refresh with CP at the start of the next Round. A legal 0 CP ability uses **no card**.

Each Unit activation permits at most one personal, Faction, or Doctrine Command by the acting side, regardless of cost; one eligible opposing Reaction may respond. An Attack, Charge, or Save sequence receives at most one friendly Command effect, including prepared effects from earlier Phases. Passives do not count. Personal phase Commands can normally be used only **during that Unit's activation**. Faction and Doctrine phase Commands are used while the side has **activation priority**. Reactions do not require priority; an explicitly timed End Step Command resolves during the End Step. Neither using a card nor a personal Command grants extra normal Phase actions unless expressly specified.

In RPG/campaign combat, the normal personal CP allowance can exist **without any selected Command cards**. Campaign progression—not the matched-play eight-card deck—determines which Commands are available; any physical cards used later are simply a tracking aid unless campaign-specific rules say otherwise.

### Number of Available Commands

During army construction, a player selects:

- Two Faction Commands.
- Three Commands from the first Doctrine.
- Three Commands from the second Doctrine.

This provides eight selected army-level Commands.

Purchased Characters and other Units may also have personal Command Abilities.

The number of available abilities does not increase the army's eight CP per Round.

### Faction Rules

Faction Rules are passive or conditional effects rather than selected Commands.

They apply automatically when their stated conditions are met.

# 11. Worked Example — Warclans Horde Breakthrough

This example demonstrates a **2,000-point Warclans army** that emphasizes large melee formations, numerical superiority, and committed Charges. It contrasts with the Free Realms gunline and shows the revised Horde/Breakthrough tactics without treating similar Command effects as stackable.

## Step 1: Choose Faction

## Step 2: Choose Doctrines

- **Horde** — Foundation **C**.
- **Breakthrough** — Foundation **V**.

## Step 3: Choose Two Faction Commands

| Command | Slot | Battlefield purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Get In There! | V | Coordinate successive Charges |
| Overwhelm | C | Add up to six Attack dice for 1 CP in Combat |

| Massed Volley | R | Support the advance with massed shooting |

# 12. Army List Checklist

Before beginning a standard game, verify:

- Exactly one Faction has been selected.
- Exactly two distinct Non-Faction Doctrines have been selected.
- Two of the Faction's four Commands have been selected.
- Three of five Commands have been selected from each Doctrine.
- All fifteen Recruitment Slots have been calculated.
- Every purchased Unit occupies one compatible Recruitment Slot, including flexible C/E/S choices.
- At least one Headquarters Unit has been purchased.
- At least three Core-classified Units have been purchased.
- No more than three Heroes have been purchased.
- No more than one named Faction Leader has been purchased.
- The appropriate Large Asset maximum has been respected.
- All additional Models, equipment, and upgrades have been paid for.
- The army's total Points cost does not exceed the agreed limit.
- All Reserve Units are included in the Points and composition limits.
- Faction, Doctrine, and Unit Commands have been recorded.

There is no requirement to select a Favored or Compatible Doctrine.

# 13. Alternative Army Formats

The 2,000-point, 15-slot system is the standard army construction format.

Other formats may use different Points limits, scenario restrictions, or recruitment procedures.

Unless explicitly modified, the normal Faction, Doctrine, Recruitment Slot, Hero, Core, and Large Asset rules remain in effect.

Narrative campaigns, independent armies, alliances, and other specialized formats may use different or additional rules described elsewhere.

## Rulebook Summary

To build a standard army:

1. Choose a Faction.
2. Choose any two different Non-Faction Doctrines.
3. Select two Faction Commands.
4. Select three Commands from each Doctrine.
5. Add the Universal, Faction, Doctrine, and Command Recruitment Slots.
6. Purchase Units using those slots, up to 2,000 Points.
7. Include at least one Headquarters and three Core Units.
8. Respect the Hero, Large Asset, and other applicable limits.

Design Principle: The Faction determines an army's identity and signature troops. The Doctrines determine how the player wishes to fight. Recruitment Slots encourage strategic specialization without preventing unconventional army compositions.

Draft Status: Standard army construction v8. All sixteen Doctrines are available to every Faction; Favored and Compatible labels are now recommendations only. Other composition and recruitment rules are unchanged.
