# STRUCTURED COMBAT

## Overview

Structured Combat is the tactical tabletop combat system used for major encounters, larger battles, and engagements where exact positioning and battlefield control are important.

Combat uses miniatures, terrain, six-sided dice, measuring tools, and objective markers.

Unlike Narrative Combat, Structured Combat uses alternating Unit activations within shared Phases.

A Round follows this sequence:

1. Priority Step
2. Movement Phase
3. Assault Phase
4. Combat Phase
5. Scoring Phase
6. End Step

During Movement, Assault, and Combat, both sides alternate activating individual Units until all eligible Units have acted.

The same Unit profiles, Attacks, Saves, Wounds, Talents, Keywords, and other combat mechanics are used in both combat modes.

## 1. Models and Units

A Model represents one miniature.

A Unit consists of one or more Models that act together.

Player Characters and important NPCs are normally single-model Units. Groups of ordinary enemies generally form multi-model Units.

When a multi-model Unit activates, all its Models act as part of the same activation.

Each Unit may normally activate once during each Phase for which it is eligible.

### Unit Coherency

After a Unit completes a Move, each Model must be within 3" of at least one other Model in the same Unit.

The Models must form one continuous group.

Single-model Units automatically satisfy Coherency.

### Combat Profiles

Each Model possesses the following statistics:

| Statistic   | Function                                      |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Move (M)    | Maximum normal movement distance              |
| Wounds (W)  | Damage the Model can accumulate before defeat |
| Armor (Arm) | Primary defensive Save                        |
| Evade (Eva) | Secondary Save representing avoidance         |
| Ward (Wrd)  | Extraordinary or supernatural defensive Save  |

Example:

M 4" / W 5 / Arm 5+ / Eva 6+ / Wrd —

### Attack Profiles

Attacks use the format:

Range / Attacks / Hit / Damage / Keywords

Example:

Longsword — Engaged / 2 / 5+ / 1

This Attack rolls 2D6, scores Hits on 5+, and inflicts 1 Damage per unsaved Hit.

Damaging Spells use Attack profiles and follow the normal Attack rules.

Attacks are normally Physical or Magic. Their types determine applicable Ward Saves and certain special effects.

## 2. Measurement and Engagement

Distances are measured between the closest edges of Model bases.

A Model is within X" if any part of its base is within that distance.

A Model is wholly within X" only if its entire base is within that distance.

Models may not normally move through enemy bases.

Friendly Models may move through one another if sufficient space exists to complete the Move legally, but cannot finish overlapping another base.

### Engagement

Enemy Models are Engaged while within 1" of one another.

A Unit is Engaged if at least one of its Models is Engaged.

An Attack listed as Engaged normally has a reach of 1".

Attacks with Engaged 2" or another listed reach may attack at that distance.

A Unit may enter Engagement by:

- Engaging during Movement.
- Successfully Charging during Assault.
- Using an ability that explicitly allows Engagement.

An ordinary Assault Move cannot enter Engagement.

## 3. Deployment and Reserves

Before the first Round, each side deploys its army according to the scenario's Deployment Zones and any special deployment rules. Standard five-Round matched battles use **Staggered Deployment** and **Escalating Reinforcements** unless their mission explicitly overrides these rules.

### Initial Deployment — Staggered Deployment

Each player must place **half of their purchased Units in Reserve, rounded up**. All remaining Units deploy on the battlefield. Count **Units, not Models or Point Value**; a reinforced or multi-model Unit counts as one Unit.

Sides alternate deploying one starting Unit at a time unless the scenario specifies otherwise. Every starting Unit must be placed wholly within its owner's Deployment Zone, without overlapping Models and while maintaining Unit Coherency.

If the army contains a Headquarters Unit, **at least one Headquarters Unit must deploy normally**. This requirement takes precedence if a very small army would otherwise have no Headquarters on the battlefield; such an exceptional army places as many Units in Reserve as possible.

Units designated as Reserve do not start on the battlefield. Both sides declare their Reserve Units during initial deployment. A player cannot voluntarily start with more Units on the battlefield than permitted by the mandatory Reserve requirement unless a mission says otherwise.

### Units in Reserve

A Unit in Reserve:

- Is included in the army's normal points, Recruitment Slots, and composition limits.
- Cannot activate, attack, or use abilities or Reactions unless expressly permitted.
- Cannot be targeted by effects that require a Unit to be on the battlefield.
- Cannot control or contest objectives.
- Does not provide battlefield auras, Headquarters ranges, or other positional effects unless a specific rule says otherwise.

Ordinary Reserve deployment costs no CP. There is **one shared Reserve pool** for each army; Deep Strike does not create a separate pool.

### Escalating Reinforcements

At the **end of each Movement Phase**, each player may deploy a number of Units from Reserve **up to the current Round number**. This is a per-player maximum across **all methods of Reserve arrival**, including ordinary arrivals and Deep Strike, unless a special rule explicitly grants an additional arrival.

| Round | Maximum ordinary/scheduled Reserve arrivals that Round |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 | 1 Unit |
| 2 | 2 Units |
| 3 | 3 Units |
| 4 | 4 Units |
| 5 | 5 Units |

A player may deploy fewer than the maximum or none at all. **Unused reinforcement allowances do not accumulate** or increase later limits. A Unit cannot enter from Reserve more than once unless a specific rule permits its return and redeployment.

*Example:* In a 15-Unit army, 8 Units begin in Reserve and 7 start on the battlefield. A player can bring in 1 in Round 1, 2 in Round 2, 3 in Round 3, and the remaining 2 in Round 4.

### Deploying Ordinary Reserves

After all Movement activations end and Assault Phase Priority is determined, resolve Reserve deployment:

1. Each side selects how many eligible Reserve Units it wishes to bring in, up to its remaining allowance for the Round.
2. Alternate placing one arriving Unit at a time, beginning with the side that held **First Activation in Movement**. If only one side has arriving Units, it places them without alternating.
3. Place each ordinary Reserve Unit **wholly within its owner's Deployment Zone**, in legal positions outside Engagement and with normal Unit Coherency.
4. Complete all legal Reserve arrivals, then begin the Assault Phase with the priority already determined.

Ordinary Reserves do not gain permission to enter from battlefield edges or forward positions outside their own Deployment Zone. Such placement requires a mission, Doctrine, Keyword, or other explicit exception.

A Unit that cannot be placed legally remains in Reserve. A player may choose not to deploy all permitted Units. If a side elects to stop placing Units, the other side may finish its own legal placements up to its allowance.

### Reserve Arrival Restrictions

An ordinarily arriving Reserve Unit counts as having **voluntarily Moved during the Movement Phase** and as having completed its Movement activation for the Round. Consequently, on the Round it arrives:

- It cannot perform a Movement Phase activation or Combat Skill Action, make another normal Move, Engage, Disengage, or use an ability requiring its Movement activation.
- It **cannot declare a Charge** during Assault.
- It **cannot use Heavy** Attacks requiring it not to have Moved.
- It **may** use eligible Assault actions other than Charge (such as an Assault Move or a ranged/Spell Attack), and may act in Combat if otherwise eligible.
- It can use legal Reactions and other abilities after arrival, subject to their normal requirements.

Deployment does not itself create an extra Unit activation. An arriving Unit is never eligible to act while it remains off the battlefield.

### Special Reserve Deployment and Doctrines

Specific missions, Keywords, or Command abilities can modify the arrival location, timing, limits, or activation restrictions **only where explicitly stated**. Unless an exception says the arrival is *additional* to the Round's normal reinforcement allowance, it consumes one of that allowance's slots.

**Alpha Strike — Rapid Mobilization (Rapid Deployment):** Once per battle, in **Round 1**, the player may use the selected Doctrine Command during one of their Movement activation opportunities to deploy **one additional** Reserve Unit wholly within the friendly Deployment Zone. It performs that Unit's normal Movement activation and **may Charge** in the ensuing Assault Phase. This arrival is expressly **outside** the Round 1 reinforcement allowance. It does not grant an extra Movement activation, and all Command costs and card-use requirements still apply.

**Deep Strike:** From **Round 2**, a selected Deep Strike Command may designate a Unit from the shared Reserve pool during a friendly Movement activation. Pay CP and reserve one arrival from the current Round's normal reinforcement allowance at designation; at most one Unit may be designated through Deep Strike per Round. The Unit is **placed during the End Step after Scoring**, not with ordinary end-of-Movement reinforcements. Unless the Command says otherwise, placement must be wholly more than **8"** from enemies, outside the enemy Deployment Zone, legally coherent, and unengaged. The arriving Unit has no activations, attacks, or scoring during its arrival Round; it acts normally from the following Round. Landing Fire and Heavy Descent apply in that following Round.

A Deep Strike Command may expressly vary placement restrictions (for example, **Flanking Insertion** permits edge entry with a more-than-6" enemy exclusion distance). **Heavy Descent** permits eligible Large Assets to arrive through Deep Strike. Normal Reserve deployment of a Large Asset does not require Heavy Descent.

For the full effects, conditions, and costs, use the current *Non-Faction Doctrines* rules. This chapter provides the universal procedures; specific Doctrine wording governs its stated exceptions.

### Remaining in Reserve

Reserve Units may remain off the battlefield for any number of Rounds unless the mission specifies otherwise. Any Unit still in Reserve when the battle ends is not automatically Defeated, but it cannot contribute to objective control or any other condition requiring battlefield presence.

## 4. Priority Step

Priority determines which side receives First Activation during a Phase.

At the beginning of each Round, compare both sides' Victory Points. The current Round number also determines each player's Reserve reinforcement allowance under Section 3.

The side with fewer Victory Points chooses which side receives First Activation during Movement.

The trailing side may choose to act first or second.

### Tied Victory Points

When Victory Points are tied, make a Priority Roll.

Each Player rolls 1D6 and adds the result to the Player total.

The GM rolls 1D6 per Player and adds the results together.

The side with the higher total chooses First Activation during Movement.

Reroll ties.

Scenarios without Victory Points may specify their own Priority system or use a Priority Roll.

## 5. Alternating Unit Activations

During Movement, Assault, and Combat, the two sides alternate activating one eligible Unit at a time.

The side with First Activation selects one eligible Unit and completes its activation.

The opposing side then activates one eligible Unit.

Continue alternating until both sides become Finished.

A Unit may normally activate only once during the same Phase.

A Unit may choose to do nothing when activated. This still counts as its activation.

### Finished Sides

A side becomes Finished when:

- All eligible Units have activated; or
- It voluntarily forfeits all remaining activations for the Phase.

Voluntarily becoming Finished is irreversible.

If one side becomes Finished while its opponent still has eligible Units, the opponent resolves its remaining activations normally.

### Phase Priority

At the end of Movement and Assault, the first side to become Finished chooses which side receives First Activation during the following Phase.

For example, if the Players finish Movement before the GM, they choose whether the Players or GM activate first during Assault.

Reserve deployment occurs after determining the next Phase's Priority and does not alter which side became Finished first. Reserve Units arriving at the end of Movement do not receive Movement activations; the special Round 1 Alpha Strike Rapid Deployment exception expressly uses a normal Movement activation opportunity.

If both sides become Finished simultaneously, retain the current Phase's First Activation side for the next Phase unless a scenario rule states otherwise.

### Eligible Units

During Movement and Assault, all non-Defeated Units currently on the battlefield are normally eligible.

During Combat, a Unit is eligible if it:

- Is Engaged.
- Has a Combat ability it can legally activate.
- Is made eligible by another rule.

Units still in Reserves are not eligible to activate.

## 6. Command Points and Abilities

Command Points (CP) represent coordination, tactical resources, and exceptional actions.

Unless a scenario states otherwise, the standard RPG allocation is:

- Each Player receives 2 CP.
- The GM receives a shared pool of **max(2, 2 × number of PCs − 1)** CP (for example, five PCs give the GM 9 CP against the players' combined 10 CP).

Standalone wargame scenarios may specify different CP allocations based on armies, leaders, objectives, or other command assets.

CP pools refresh at the beginning of each Round.

Unused CP do not carry over.

### Command timing and priority

**One Command per activation:** The acting side may use at most **one Command** (personal Unit, Faction, or Doctrine) during each Unit's activation, regardless of CP cost. A multi-target Command may affect other Units, but does not authorize another friendly Command during that activation. The opposing side may use **one eligible Reaction Command** against that activation; its trigger and priority rules still apply. Passive effects are not Commands.

**No stacking on one resolution:** An individual Attack, Charge, or Save sequence may benefit from **at most one friendly Command effect**, including effects prepared in earlier Phases. If several friendly prepared effects would apply, choose one; the others do not apply to that sequence. A defending player may use its eligible Reaction against an Attack enhanced by the attacker. Compatible passive abilities, equipment, and Talents still apply.


A normal Unit Command with **Movement**, **Assault**, or **Combat** timing may be used only **during that Unit's own activation** in that Phase, when its controlling side has priority and has selected that Unit to activate. The Command may affect another Unit if the ability allows it, but a player cannot activate an unselected ally's personal Command. A Command does not consume the Unit's chosen Phase action unless stated otherwise; its CP cost is paid normally. An eligible Unit may use a Command even if it chooses to forgo its ordinary Phase action.

**Faction and Doctrine Commands** belong to the controlling side. Unless they are Reactions, use them while that side holds activation priority in the permitted Phase, subject to their specific costs and restrictions. They do not permit a personal Command on another Unit.

**Reaction Commands** may interrupt the specified trigger even when the reacting side does not hold priority. Passives apply without spending CP or obtaining priority.

### Ability Timing

Command Abilities specify when they may be used.

Movement: Used during Movement.

Assault: Used during Assault.

Combat: Used during Combat.

Reaction: Used immediately when the specified trigger occurs.

Instant: Cannot be responded to with another Command Ability.

Unless otherwise stated, a Phase-specific ability is used during the Unit's activation.

### Reactions

A Reaction interrupts the triggering action.

Resolve the Reaction completely, then resume the interrupted action.

The reacting Unit does not need to be currently active.

Reactions do not ordinarily consume the Unit's normal activation.

## 7. Movement Phase

During Movement, sides alternate Unit activations according to current Phase Priority.

When activated, a Unit may:

- Make one normal Move.
- Engage an enemy.
- Disengage.
- Remain stationary.
- Use eligible Movement abilities.

Characters may additionally perform Combat Skill Actions by spending CP.

### Normal Movement

Each Model may move up to its Move characteristic.

For example, M 4" allows up to 4" of movement.

Movement is measured along the actual path traveled.

Models may change direction freely.

Each Model may normally be moved only once during the Movement Phase.

### Engaging an Enemy

A Unit may deliberately enter Engagement during Movement.

To Engage:

1. Declare the enemy Model being Engaged.
2. The enemy must be wholly within a distance equal to the moving Model's maximum Move.
3. A legal movement path must exist.
4. Finish the moving Model within 1" of the target.

A normal Engage causes no Impact or Damage.

### Disengaging

A Unit beginning its Movement activation Engaged may Disengage.

Move normally, ending outside Engagement with all enemies.

The Unit becomes Withdrawn until the end of the Round.

A Withdrawn Unit:

- May make an ordinary Assault Move.
- May use eligible non-Attack abilities.
- Cannot make an Assault Attack.
- Cannot Charge.
- Cannot voluntarily Engage another enemy.
- Cannot control objectives.

If subsequently Engaged by an enemy, a Withdrawn Unit may defend itself and make eligible Combat Attacks normally.

### Difficult Terrain

Unless another rule states otherwise, every 1" traveled through Difficult Terrain costs 2" of Move.

Talents, movement types, and other abilities may modify or ignore this penalty.

### Vertical Movement

Vertical distance counts toward movement normally.

For example, climbing 2" costs 2" of Move.

Special rules such as Climber may modify vertical movement.

### End of Movement Phase

After both sides become Finished, determine Assault Phase Priority.

Then resolve permitted Reserve deployments for both sides, including any previously designated Deep Strike arrivals, following **Section 3**. The maximum number of scheduled arrivals is the current Round number; unused allowances expire.

Once Reserve deployment is complete, proceed to Assault.

## 8. Combat Skill Actions

During its Movement activation, a Character may spend 1 Command Point to attempt one ordinary Skill Check.

The Character rolls its normal Characteristic and Skill combination. The GM determines the Difficulty.

Examples include:

- Stealth: Hide or move unnoticed.
- Athletics: Climb, jump, or overcome obstacles.
- Awareness: Search for hidden enemies or hazards.
- Survival: Overcome environmental dangers.
- Medicine: Perform an appropriate medical action.
- Other Skills: Attempt any reasonable action supported by the encounter.

A Combat Skill Action may occur before or after normal Movement, provided the action is compatible with that movement.

The GM may require the Character to remain stationary or use its Movement to perform certain actions.

A Character may normally attempt one Combat Skill Action per Movement Phase.

Combat Skill Actions do not provide additional movement or Attacks unless specifically permitted.

Talents, equipment, and special rules may modify their costs, timing, or effects.

### Stealth During Combat

Any Character may spend 1 CP to attempt an appropriate Stealth Check during Movement.

The Character must have a plausible way to avoid detection, such as obscuring terrain, darkness, distractions, or a suitable hiding place.

On success, the Character may become Concealed from enemies it successfully hides from, subject to the normal Concealed rules.

Concealment may end when the Character attacks, reveals its position, moves into an exposed location, or is discovered.

## 9. Assault Phase

During Assault, the two sides alternate selecting eligible Units according to Phase Priority. On activation, each Unit chooses **exactly one** of the following ordinary Assault actions:

| Action | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| **Assault Move** | Move up to its Move characteristic, without entering Engagement voluntarily |
| **Assault Attack** | Make one eligible ranged Weapon or Spell Attack; Models using different profiles resolve them separately as required |
| **Charge** | Roll the standard Charge and attempt to enter Engagement; resolve Impact on success |

A Unit may forgo its action. It does **not** make an Assault Move and Attack in the same activation without an explicit special rule. **Ordinary Reserves and Deep Strike arrivals cannot Charge on the Round they arrive; Alpha Strike Rapid Deployment expressly overrides this for its one special arrival.** Using a legal Assault Command costs CP and is **separate from the chosen action**, unless that Command says it replaces or prevents the ordinary action. See [Command timing and priority](#command-timing-and-priority).

### Assault Move

Move each Model up to its full Move characteristic; an ordinary Assault Move cannot enter Engagement. Engaged Units cannot take an ordinary Assault Move. A Unit that is Withdrawn may still make an Assault Move, but cannot make an Assault Attack or Charge.

### Assault Attack

Make one eligible ranged Weapon or Spell Attack against a legal target. Each eligible Model contributes its own printed Attack dice, using its NPC Unit profile or the PC equipment/spell profile as appropriate. Different Attack profiles are resolved separately as needed. Ranged Attacks normally require Line of Sight.

- **Shooting while Engaged:** An eligible Attack may target the Unit with which the attacker is Engaged; the target may use Evade after Armor, without an automatic Hit penalty.
- **Shooting into a friendly Engagement:** When a Ranged or Spell Attack targets an enemy Engaged with a different friendly Unit, worsen Hit by one step. **For every Blunder generated by the Attack after rerolls, the designated friendly Unit Engaged with the target suffers one instance of Direct Damage 2** (Physical from ordinary ranged weapons; Magic from Spell Attacks); applicable Ward Saves apply. Designate the endangered Unit when declaring the Attack. Blunders cause friendly fire even if the Attack inflicts no enemy Damage. **Pinpoint ignores both the Hit penalty and this friendly-fire damage**, but does not prevent other Blunder or Blast effects. Shooting at an enemy engaged with the attacking Unit itself remains governed by the preceding rule.
- **Heavy:** The attacker must not have voluntarily moved or performed another Movement Action during the preceding Movement Phase. The restriction does not prohibit an Assault Move on its own, but only one Assault action may be chosen.

### Assault Commands

An Assault Command may be used during the selected Unit's activation, independently of its chosen Assault action, provided it is legal and CP is paid. If the Command directs an Attack or Move as an additional effect, follow its specific wording; it does not generally give the Unit an additional ordinary Assault action.

## 10. Terrain, Cover, and Elevation

Terrain may restrict movement, block Line of Sight, or provide defensive advantages.

Unless otherwise specified, terrain benefits are determined when an Attack is declared.

### Physical Cover

A Unit benefiting from meaningful protection provided by terrain gains Cover 1 against ranged Attacks.

Cover X permits the defender to use Evade against eligible ranged Attacks and reroll up to X natural 1s when making Evade Saves.

The new result must be kept.

Cover does not improve Armor or Ward.

### Cover and Multi-Model Units

A multi-model Unit receives ordinary Cover if at least half of the Models targeted by the Attack are meaningfully protected.

If fewer than half are protected, the Unit does not receive the ordinary Cover benefit.

Multiple sources of ordinary physical Cover do not stack.

A Unit protected by several walls or obstacles receives Cover 1 from ordinary physical Cover rather than Cover 1 for each obstacle.

### Elevation

A Unit occupying meaningfully higher ground than its attacker gains Cover 1 against ranged Attacks originating from below.

Meaningful elevation includes rooftops, raised platforms, cliffs, fortifications, and similar positions.

A minor slope or negligible difference in height does not automatically grant Cover.

Elevation does not improve Armor or count as ordinary physical Cover. It provides a separate source of Cover 1.

### Elevation and Multi-Model Units

A multi-model Unit receives the elevation benefit if at least half of the Models targeted by the Attack occupy qualifying higher ground.

When Models occupy different elevations, determine eligibility from the positions of the Models receiving the Attack.

### Combined Cover and Elevation

Physical Cover and favorable Elevation may apply simultaneously.

A Unit occupying higher ground behind Cover gains:

Cover 2

For example:

- Physical Cover: Cover 1
- Higher Elevation: Cover 1
- Total: Cover 2

Additional Cover X from Talents, equipment, and other eligible sources adds to this value unless a rule specifies otherwise.

Each Evade die may only be rerolled once.

### Cover and Engagement

The Cover keyword functions whenever the defender is eligible to Evade and the source of Cover applies to that Attack.

Ordinary physical Cover and elevation bonuses apply against ranged Attacks only.

Other sources of Cover X may specify different conditions.

Cover does not permit Evade against Charge Impact unless a special rule explicitly overrides Impact's normal restriction.

## 11. Heavy

An Attack, Spell, or Action with Heavy may only be used if the Unit did not voluntarily move or perform another Movement Action during the immediately preceding Movement Phase.

This includes normal Moves, Disengaging, and any arrival from Reserves during the current Round, including Deep Strike or Rapid Deployment. The latter overrides Movement and Charge restrictions but does **not** waive Heavy unless stated.

Forced movement caused by an enemy does not normally prevent Heavy.

Heavy does not itself prevent an Assault Move unless another rule states otherwise.

## 12. Charging

A Charge allows an unengaged Unit to close with an enemy and inflict Impact.

During Assault, **Charge is one of three mutually exclusive ordinary actions:** Assault Move, Assault Attack, or Charge.

The target must be:

- Visible.
- Wholly within 12".
- Reachable through a legal movement path.

A Withdrawn Unit cannot Charge. A Unit that arrived from ordinary Reserves or through Deep Strike this Round also cannot Charge unless a specific exception, such as Rapid Deployment, explicitly grants permission.

### Universal Charge Roll

An eligible **non-PC Unit**, including NPCs and multi-model Units, rolls **2D6** for a Charge. Determine its available Charge distance as:

**Charge Distance = total of both D6 results + CHARGE X (if any).**

The declared target must be visible and wholly within the normal **12" declaration limit**, even if the roll would permit farther movement. A legal movement path is required, and the charging Unit must be able to establish Engagement within its Charge Distance. The Unit's Move characteristic is not added. A failed Charge (the Unit cannot reach Engagement within its Charge Distance) does not move and ends that Unit's Assault activation.

For a multi-model Unit, make **one Charge roll for the Unit**, not a separate roll per Model. Move its Models into legal positions within the rolled allowance while preserving normal Coherency. The Unit's Charge generates **one Impact pool** against the declared target, not Impact per charging Model.

### PC Charge Skill Check and Momentum

A Player Character instead rolls **Body + Athletics**. A Rogue may use **Agility + Athletics** instead. Roll D6 equal to the chosen Characteristic; each die meeting the Athletics rating generates one Success. Use the **two highest dice** for base Charge distance and the **highest die** for base Impact.

Each Athletics Success provides **1 Momentum**. After the roll, before moving, allocate each Momentum to **+1" Charge distance** or **+1 Impact Hit** for this Charge. Charge X applies to distance normally. There is no minimum Success requirement: a Charge succeeds if the final distance legally reaches the target. The normal 12" Charge declaration limit still applies. Unspent Momentum is lost. PC Charge Checks generate Surges and Blunders as ordinary Skill Checks (at most one of each unless a specific rule says otherwise).

**Example:** A Rogue rolls 1, 3, 4, 5 with Agility + Athletics 4+. The two highest dice give 9" base distance, highest die 5 gives base Impact 5, and the two successes provide 2 Momentum. The Rogue may choose +2" distance, +2 Impact, or one of each.

### Charge Impact

Every successful Charge generates **Impact equal to its highest Charge die**. A Unit with printed **Impact X** adds X Hits to that base amount; other explicitly applicable bonuses or reductions (including applicable Doctrine bonuses) then apply.

**Final Impact Hits = max(0, highest Charge die + PC Momentum spent on Impact (if any) + printed Impact X + other Impact bonuses − Brace X).**

Impact inflicts that many automatic **Physical Hits at Damage 1** against the target. Resolve **Armor → applicable Ward → Damage**; **Evade is ignored**. Impact is not an Attack roll and does not independently generate a Surge or Blunder. It does not multiply by the number of Models in either Unit.

**Example:** An ordinary Unit rolls 3 and 5, with CHARGE 1 and Impact 2. The Charge can travel 3 + 5 + 1 = **9"**, but may only have been declared against an enemy wholly within 12". It inflicts **7 automatic Hits** on success, reduced by the target's Brace X before Saves.

### Charge Surges and Blunders

The Charge roll is also checked for natural sixes and ones after allowed rerolls. Two sixes generate a Surge; two ones generate a Blunder. PC Charges follow the ordinary Skill Check limit of one Surge and one Blunder. Apply only abilities triggered by these results. A successful Charge that Blunders causes **Off-Balance** until the end of the Round (Evade worsens one step). The successful charging Unit may still attack during the Combat Phase if eligible.

## 13. Combat Phase

During Combat, eligible Units alternate activations according to current Phase Priority.

An eligible Unit may:

- Make one Melee Attack.
- Use eligible Combat abilities.

A Unit may omit its Attack and still use a legal Combat ability.

### Melee Attacks

Each eligible Model may contribute its Melee Attack if the target is within that Attack's listed Engagement Range.

A Unit normally attacks one enemy Unit unless a special rule allows otherwise.

Models using the same profile combine their Attack dice.

Different Attack profiles are resolved separately as necessary.

A Unit that Charged during Assault may make an Melee Attack during Combat if still eligible.

## 14. Attack Resolution

Resolve each Attack in the following order:

1. Choose an Attack profile.
2. Choose a legal target.
3. Determine the number of Attack dice.
4. Roll to Hit.
5. Resolve permitted rerolls (such as Accurate) and count Surges/Blunders from the final dice.
6. Apply passive Critical X **per Surge** and any CP-granted Critical conversions once, up to the available failed Hit dice.
7. Roll Armor Saves against ordinary Hits (Critical Hits ignore Armor and Evade).
8. Roll Evade Saves against remaining ordinary Hits, if eligible.
9. Roll applicable Ward Saves against remaining normal and Critical Hits.
10. Apply Damage.

### Attack Dice and Hits

Roll a number of D6 equal to the Attack profile's Attacks value.

Every die meeting or exceeding the Hit value generates one Hit.

For multi-model Units, combine the Attacks of eligible Models using the same profile.

Example

Three Models each have a Spear with 2 Attacks.

If all three may legally attack, the Unit rolls 6D6.

### Surges and Blunders

An **Attack roll** generates one Surge for every complete pair of natural 6s. An effect worded **when an Attack Surges** activates once per Attack unless it says **for each Surge**.

An **Attack roll** generates one Blunder for every complete pair of natural 1s. Resolve repeated Blunder consequences only where the triggering rule scales by the number generated.

Each additional complete pair generates another instance for an **Attack roll**. Other rolls normally generate only one Surge and one Blunder unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Surges and Blunders have no universal effects unless a rule specifies one.

They activate effects from weapons, equipment, Talents, abilities, terrain, and other sources.

A roll may generate both a Surge and a Blunder.

Each die may normally be rerolled once. The new result must be kept.

Determine Surges and Blunders after rerolls.

## 15. Defensive Saves

Defensive Saves cancel Hits before Damage is applied.

Each Save is resolved against the Hits remaining after the preceding defense.

### Armor

Armor is the primary defensive Save.

For each Hit, roll one D6 against the target's Armor.

Each successful Armor Save cancels one Hit.

### Tough X

Tough X allows the defender to reroll up to X natural 1s when making Armor Saves against an Attack.

Applicable sources of Tough X add together unless a specific rule states otherwise.

Each die may be rerolled once, and the new result must be kept.

Tough does not change Armor or generate additional Save dice.

Unused rerolls are lost if fewer qualifying dice are rolled than the Unit's Tough value.

### Evade

Evade represents dodging, parrying, repositioning, or otherwise avoiding an Attack.

Evade is normally available when:

- The defender is targeted by an Melee Attack.
- The defender is Engaged with its attacker and targeted by an Assault Attack.
- The defender possesses applicable Cover X against a ranged Attack.
- Another ability or special rule grants Evade.

For every Hit remaining after Armor, roll one Evade Save if eligible.

Each successful Evade Save cancels one Hit.

Evade does not normally apply to Charge Impact.

### Cover X

Cover X allows the defender to reroll up to X natural 1s when making eligible Evade Saves.

Cover X also permits Evade against ranged Attacks to which it applies.

Applicable sources of Cover X add together unless a specific rule states otherwise.

Each die may be rerolled once, and the new result must be kept.

Cover does not change the Evade value or generate additional Save dice.

Unused rerolls are lost if fewer qualifying dice are rolled than the Unit's Cover value.

A Unit without an Evade Save cannot benefit from Cover X unless another rule grants it an Evade value.

### Ward

Ward represents magical, divine, supernatural, psychic, or extraordinary protection.

For every Hit remaining after Armor and eligible Evade Saves, roll one applicable Ward Save.

A Ward may specify which Attack types it protects against.

Examples:

- Ward 6+ (Physical)
- Ward 5+ (Magic)
- Ward 6+ (All Types)

A Ward without a listed type applies against both Physical and Magic Attacks.

If multiple Wards apply, use only the best applicable Ward.

Ward Saves do not stack.

### Save Modifiers

Improving a Save lowers its required result by one step:

6+ → 5+ → 4+ → 3+ → 2+

Worsening a Save reverses this progression.

Saves cannot normally improve beyond 2+.

A Save worsened beyond 6+ becomes unavailable.

### Defense Summary

| Situation                                 | Defenses                       |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Normal Ranged Attack                      | Armor → Ward                   |
| Ranged Attack against Physical Cover      | Armor → Evade (Cover 1) → Ward |
| Ranged Attack against Higher Elevation    | Armor → Evade (Cover 1) → Ward |
| Ranged Attack against Cover and Elevation | Armor → Evade (Cover 2) → Ward |
| Assault Attack against an Engaged enemy   | Armor → Evade → Ward           |
| Melee Attack                         | Armor → Evade → Ward           |
| Charge Impact                             | Armor → Ward                   |

Applicable Tough and Cover values modify their respective Save rolls.

Special rules may modify these sequences.

## 16. Damage and Defeat

Every Hit remaining after all applicable Saves inflicts the Attack's listed Damage.

Damage accumulates against the target Model's Wounds.

### Multi-Model Units

Allocate unsaved Damage to Models within the targeted Unit.

Once Damage has been allocated to a wounded Model, continue allocating Damage to that Model until it is defeated before assigning Damage to another Model, unless a special rule states otherwise.

### Ordinary Models

When accumulated Damage equals or exceeds a Model's Wounds, the Model is defeated and removed.

### Player Characters and Important NPCs

Player Characters and important named NPCs become Defeated instead of being immediately removed.

A Defeated Character cannot normally:

- Move.
- Attack.
- Use Command Abilities.
- Control objectives.

Recovery, injuries, and death are resolved through the campaign rules.

## 17. Scoring Phase

After both sides complete Combat, resolve Scoring.

Victory Points are awarded according to the scenario.

Common objectives include:

- Controlling locations.
- Holding territory.
- Escorting Units.
- Protecting important Models.
- Completing mission actions.
- Defeating specific targets.

### Objective Control

Unless a scenario states otherwise, a Unit controls an objective while at least one eligible Model is within 3" of it.

If both sides have eligible Units within range, the side with more eligible Units controls the objective.

If tied, the objective is Contested.

Defeated and Withdrawn Units cannot control objectives.

Units still in Reserves cannot control objectives.

A multi-model Unit counts as one Unit for determining control unless the scenario states otherwise.

## 18. End Step

After Scoring:

1. Place designated Deep Strike arrivals after Scoring.
2. Resolve other End-of-Round effects.
3. Remove Withdrawn.
4. Remove Off-Balance unless a specific effect has a longer printed duration (such as No Quarter).
5. Expire other conditions and effects ending this Round.
6. Advance the Round counter.
7. Begin the next Priority Step.

Refresh CP and other Round-based resources at the beginning of the new Round.

## 19. Keywords and Special Rules

### Area effects and delayed Hits

A measured area affects a Unit if at least **half of its current Models, rounded up,** have any portion of their bases within the area at the time it resolves. Resolve the stated Hits or effects **once per qualifying Unit**, never per Model. Delayed automatic Hits such as Arcane Storm allow Armor and applicable Ward, but **not Evade**, unless specifically stated. Blast secondary Hits use their normal specific saves. See [Keywords](10_keywords.md#area-effects-against-units).

### Flying and Blink

Flying permits traversal of intervening terrain and Models (subject to legal destinations); Blink repositions a leading Model, then the rest of its Unit in Coherency, ignoring obstacles but not elevation restrictions. Neither automatically grants additional actions.

All Units use the shared Keyword glossary.

Keywords may modify Movement, Attacks, targeting, Saves, Damage, Charging, Impact, Command Abilities, or activation timing.

### Cover X

Reroll up to X natural 1s when making Evade Saves.

Cover X also permits Evade against eligible ranged Attacks.

Sources of Cover X stack unless specified otherwise. Each die may only be rerolled once.

Physical Cover grants Cover 1 against ranged Attacks.

Meaningfully higher Elevation grants Cover 1 against ranged Attacks originating from below.

### Tough X

Reroll up to X natural 1s when making Armor Saves.

Applicable sources stack unless specified otherwise. Each die may only be rerolled once.

### Other Keywords

Other Keywords follow their normal definitions. **Doctrine-granted Impact +X** (such as Alpha Strike’s Shock Assault) adds automatic Charge Impact Hits; it does not add Charge distance or extra Melee Attack dice.

Where a Keyword conflicts with a general rule, follow the more specific rule.

Both Narrative and Structured Combat use the same Keyword definitions. Narrative Combat interprets exact-distance effects through Range Bands where appropriate.

### Range Bands in Structured Combat

Unit, Faction, and Doctrine Commands, Talents, and Bestiary entries sometimes refer to a target **within Short**, **within Medium**, or **within Long** instead of printing an exact distance. In Structured Combat, measure these the same way as the Narrative Combat Range Bands:

- **Short** — up to 6".
- **Medium** — beyond 6", up to 12".
- **Long** — beyond 12".

These thresholds are measured normally (closest base edges), exactly like any other printed distance. They are shorthand for the stated inch values, not a separate measurement system.
