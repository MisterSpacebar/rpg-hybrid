# General Adventuring Rules

These rules govern ordinary roleplaying, Character survival, recovery, narrative encounters, advancement, and basic recordkeeping. They are intended for adventures involving exploration, investigation, social encounters, travel, and smaller-scale combat, including extended periods of narrative play. The RPG and wargame use the same Characters, combat profiles, Skills, equipment, and abilities.

Campaign Mode adds strategic Turns, Company Orders, military development, territorial control, political competition, and structured advancement. Those systems are used when relevant rather than replacing the ordinary RPG rules.

## 1. Narrative Play

Narrative Play represents the actions of individual Characters as they interact with the world. Rather than requiring fixed procedures for every action, the Game Master (GM) describes the situation, the players declare their intentions, and the GM determines the consequences. Most routine activities do not require a roll. Skill Checks are used when an action involves meaningful uncertainty and its outcome matters.

### 1.1 Skill Checks, Excess Successes, Surges, and Blunders

Use the universal [Skill Check rules](01_core_rules.md#1-skill-checks). The GM selects the Characteristic and Skill, sets Difficulty based on the situation (not a Character's strength), and resolves ordinary Skill Checks, Excess Successes, Surges, and Blunders according to that chapter. Specific abilities override those defaults only where explicitly stated.

### 1.2 Failure and Consequences

A failed Check means that the Character does not achieve their intended objective as declared.

Depending on the circumstances, failure may result in:

- Lost time.
- Consumed supplies.
- Increased danger.
- An unfavorable reaction.
- Incomplete information.
- Damage or another appropriate consequence.
- A new obstacle that must be addressed.

Failure does not always mean that the story stops progressing. The GM may reveal information, introduce a complication, or present an alternative approach when doing so is appropriate to the situation.

### Repeated Attempts

A Character may attempt an action again when circumstances permit. However, repeatedly making the same Check without changing the approach, resources, or circumstances should not automatically provide unlimited opportunities to succeed. The GM may require a different method, additional preparation, assistance, or the acceptance of an appropriate cost before another attempt becomes meaningful.

## 2. Narrative Encounters

Narrative encounters may involve exploration, investigation, negotiation, environmental hazards, travel, and other activities that do not require a full structured battle. The GM determines the appropriate scope of each encounter.

### 2.1 Exploration and Investigation

Exploration involves interacting with unfamiliar environments, discovering information, overcoming obstacles, and identifying opportunities. Characters may use Awareness, Survival, Athletics, Stealth, Mysticism, Medicine, and other appropriate Skills. The GM should distinguish between information that is readily observable and information requiring meaningful investigation. Important information necessary to understand the immediate situation should not always depend on a successful roll.

Skill Checks are most useful when they determine:

- How much additional information is discovered.
- How quickly the Characters accomplish something.
- Whether a danger is detected in advance.
- Whether the party avoids a complication.
- Whether the Characters can access something otherwise inaccessible.

A successful investigation may reveal new options without immediately resolving the larger challenge.

### Exploration Sites

When Narrative Play occurs during Campaign Mode, a strategically designated Exploration Site follows the campaign's normal Site rules. A unique completed Site may award a Discovery and 5 XP per PC. Ordinary rooms, encounters, landmarks, and incidental discoveries do not automatically qualify as separate campaign Sites.

### 2.2 Social Encounters

Social encounters involve negotiation, persuasion, deception, intimidation, diplomacy, and other interactions with Characters and organizations. Players describe their intended approach and desired outcome. The GM determines whether a Check is necessary and selects the relevant Characteristic and Skill. A successful social Check represents a favorable outcome that is plausible under the circumstances. It does not automatically grant unrestricted control over another Character or force them to disregard fundamental loyalties, interests, or obligations.

The outcome may instead involve:

- Obtaining cooperation.
- Improving the terms of an agreement.
- Securing information.
- Establishing trust.
- Reducing hostility.
- Gaining access to an appropriate opportunity.

Significant political agreements and territorial changes during Campaign Mode must still satisfy the appropriate strategic requirements. A social Check alone does not create an additional Company Order or automatically award a political Stake.

### 2.3 Travel and Environmental Challenges

Ordinary travel may be resolved through narration when the route is safe, familiar, and uncontested. Travel becomes an encounter when distance, hazards, hostile forces, limited supplies, or uncertain routes create meaningful challenges.

The GM may use Skill Checks to resolve:

- Navigation through difficult terrain.
- Dangerous crossings.
- Avoiding environmental hazards.
- Locating safe shelter.
- Tracking creatures or travelers.
- Remaining concealed during movement.

Failure may result in lost time, exposure, separation, resource consumption, or another appropriate consequence.

### Travel During Campaign Mode

When the strategic map is active, significant movement between hexes follows the applicable Company travel and movement rules. Ordinary local movement within an adventure does not require a separate Company Order. Narrative travel cannot be used to bypass established strategic movement restrictions, hostile territory, or required military operations.

### 2.4 Assistance and Cooperation

Characters may assist one another when their actions and circumstances permit it. Assistance should describe a meaningful contribution rather than simply declaring that multiple Characters are helping. The GM may resolve cooperation through an appropriate individual or group Check, or allow one Character's action to create favorable circumstances for another. When one Character leads a shared Check, one other Character offering meaningful assistance grants the Lead Character **+1D6**. Additional helpers do not normally stack extra dice. Use individual Checks if each Character faces the risk separately; see [Core Rules — Group Checks](01_core_rules.md#5-group-checks).

In situations requiring several different tasks, different Characters may contribute through separate relevant Checks.

## 3. Narrative Combat

When a narrative encounter develops into combat, use the game's Narrative Combat rules. Narrative Combat uses the same fundamental Character profiles, attacks, saves, Talents, keywords, and Command Points as Structured Combat. It is intended for encounters where precise miniature positioning and full battlefield objectives are unnecessary. The normal narrative range bands are:

| Range | Distance |
| --- | --- |
| Engaged | In direct engagement |
| Short | Within 6" |
| Medium | Within 12" |
| Long | Beyond 12" |

Use the Narrative Combat rules for movement, attack timing, engagement, and phase-based activations.

### 3.1 Skills During Combat

Characters may use appropriate Skills during combat when their intended actions are possible under the circumstances. Unless another rule provides otherwise, a Character may spend 1 Command Point during the Movement Phase to perform an eligible Skill action.

Examples include:

- Attempting to hide or move discreetly.
- Investigating an unusual battlefield feature.
- Providing emergency medical treatment.
- Interacting with a mechanism.
- Overcoming an environmental obstacle.

The GM selects the appropriate Check and Difficulty. A Skill action does not automatically replace an attack, bypass an enemy's defensive rules, or grant a mechanical effect equivalent to an existing Talent without satisfying its requirements. Outside combat, ordinary Skill use does not require Command Points.

### 3.2 Transitioning Between Narrative and Structured Combat

A narrative encounter may become a Structured Battle when the conflict involves substantial military forces, multiple formations, or battlefield objectives that benefit from precise positioning. The GM establishes the battlefield, participating units, and relevant scenario conditions. Characters retain their current Damage, equipment, and applicable ongoing conditions unless an explicit rule changes them. Transitioning between combat formats does not automatically restore Wounds, refresh consumed resources, or create additional campaign rewards.

## 4. Defeat and Critical Injuries

When a Player Character accumulates Damage equal to or greater than their Wounds, they become Defeated. A Defeated Character cannot perform Actions, Attacks, or Commands and is removed from active combat. Unlike ordinary Units, Player Characters may survive being Defeated.

### 4.1 Critical Injuries

Each Player Character has three Critical Injury slots. Whenever a Player Character is Defeated, they suffer one Critical Injury.

| Critical Injuries | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Healthy |
| 1 | Survives their first Defeat |
| 2 | Survives their second Defeat |
| 3 | Permanently dies or retires |

A Character suffering their first or second Critical Injury survives the encounter but remains incapacitated until they recover. Upon suffering their third Critical Injury, the Character permanently dies or retires, and their Character Sheet is retired. Critical Injuries are permanent and cannot normally be removed through healing or rest. They impose no additional mechanical penalties, although players may describe scars, lasting wounds, disabilities, or other consequences.

A single Defeat normally causes one Critical Injury. Remaining incapacitated does not repeatedly award new injuries merely because time passes. Certain exceptional narrative circumstances may result in permanent death regardless of remaining Critical Injuries, at the GM's discretion. Such dangers should generally be established clearly enough for players to understand the risks their Characters face.

### 4.2 Character Death and Replacement

A Character suffering their third Critical Injury can no longer continue as an active PC. The player may introduce a replacement Character. During Campaign Mode, replacement Characters begin with XP and equipment appropriate to the Company's current progression, as described in the campaign's Character Replacement rules. Replacement Characters begin with no Critical Injuries. Permanent Character loss does not automatically grant additional Gold, recruitment opportunities, or Company resources.

## 5. Recovery and Rest

Characters recover accumulated Damage through rest, medical treatment, and healing abilities. Rest represents opportunities to recover from ordinary exertion and injury. Critical Injuries remain permanent unless a specific exceptional rule provides otherwise.

### 5.1 Short Rest

A Short Rest represents approximately 10–30 minutes of relative safety.

Characters may:

- Receive medical treatment.
- Maintain equipment.
- Perform appropriate minor preparations.
- Recover abilities that specify a Short Rest.

A Character may receive one Mind + Medicine Check at Normal (2) Difficulty during a Short Rest. On success, they remove one accumulated Damage, plus one additional Damage for every Excess Success. A Character cannot benefit from repeated treatment Checks during the same Short Rest merely by changing the person administering treatment. Medical supplies or other circumstances may modify treatment when an applicable rule specifies an effect.

### 5.2 Long Rest

A Long Rest represents approximately eight hours of uninterrupted rest.

A Character completing a Long Rest:

- Removes all accumulated Damage.
- Recovers abilities that specify a Long Rest.
- May resume adventuring after surviving Defeat.

A Character must have a reasonable opportunity for uninterrupted recovery. A Long Rest does not remove Critical Injuries.

### 5.3 Healing Abilities

Healing spells, Talents, equipment, and other abilities follow their normal rules. They may remove Damage or provide other specified benefits. Healing abilities cannot normally return a Defeated Character to active combat unless explicitly stated. Removing Damage does not automatically remove Critical Injuries.

### 5.4 Rest During Campaign Mode

Ordinary Short and Long Rests do not automatically advance the Campaign Turn. A Character may rest several times during a prolonged adventure without generating additional Company Orders, seasonal Gold, or campaign XP. Longer periods of downtime may instead be handled through the appropriate Company Order, Campaign Turn, or narrative consequences.

## 6. Experience and Advancement

Experience Points (XP) represent a Character's continued development. Characters spend XP on Talents and other eligible advancements according to the Character Advancement rules. XP may be saved and is normally spent outside encounters. The method of awarding XP depends on whether the group is using Campaign Mode or playing independently through Narrative Play.

### 6.1 Experience During Campaign Mode

Campaign Mode provides the default advancement system for an organized campaign. Its ordinary awards include:

| Source | XP per PC |
| --- | --- |
| Completed Campaign Turn | 5 |
| Unique resolved Exploration Site | 5 |
| Beginning Acts II, III, and IV | 5 each |
| Eligible Act II or III Pivotal Objective | 5 each |

XP is normally awarded equally among the PCs. A Campaign Turn may require several gaming sessions to resolve. The Company still receives only one ordinary Campaign Turn XP award for that Turn. Players do not receive additional routine XP simply for the number of hours played, individual enemies defeated, or Skill Checks attempted.

### 6.2 Extended Narrative Adventures

A Company may undertake a substantial narrative adventure that occupies several sessions. When the adventure is part of an existing Campaign Turn, the Turn remains active until its declared operation is resolved.

During this time:

- The Season does not automatically advance.
- The Company does not receive additional Orders.
- NPC Maneuver commitments remain associated with the current Turn.
- Provincial Dividends do not occur again.
- The normal completed-Turn XP is awarded once.

Eligible Exploration Sites, Pivotal Objectives, and explicitly authorized rewards remain available when actually earned. A lengthy narrative mission does not need to be shortened merely to match the pace of the strategic campaign.

### 6.3 Narrative Play Without Campaign Turns

For adventures played outside an active Campaign Mode, the GM may award XP through completed objectives or narrative milestones. A recommended starting point is 5 XP per substantial completed adventure objective or chapter. Such milestones should represent meaningful progression rather than individual Skill Checks, ordinary encounters, or hours spent playing.

Examples include:

- Resolving a major investigation.
- Completing a significant expedition.
- Overcoming a central threat.
- Achieving an important personal or group objective.
- Concluding a substantial narrative adventure.

The GM may adjust the pace to suit the intended length of the adventure.

### Pausing a Structured Campaign

A group may temporarily suspend the strategic calendar to focus on a self-contained narrative interlude. While suspended, Company Orders, political Stakes, and seasonal NPC Maneuvers do not automatically occur. If the GM awards XP for significant narrative milestones during such an interlude, record it as explicitly authorized additional XP. It counts toward the Character's total earned XP and, when awarded to the whole Company, the shared XP Benchmark.

Do not award both a narrative milestone reward and the ordinary Campaign Turn reward for the same achievement unless the milestone is explicitly an additional reward. Extended narrative interludes providing significant extra XP may cause Characters to progress beyond the campaign's normal expected advancement.

### 6.4 Hero Point Value

When a Character participates in Campaign Mode or has a Structured Combat Point Value, calculate:

**Base Hero Points = max(100, XP spent on Talents).**

**Final Hero Points = Base Hero Points + Equipment Points + other explicitly additional costs.**

The Hero’s base remains 100 until they have spent 100 XP on Talents. Thereafter, spent XP replaces—not supplements—the base. Unspent XP contributes no Points, and XP-funded Talents are not charged twice. See [Advancement](03_experience_and_advancement.md) for examples.

Equipment contributes its normal points separately.

### 6.5 Advancement and Development

Spending earned XP on normal Character advancement does not require a Company Develop Order. Develop is used for its specified equipment, formation, detachment, and Asset projects. The Character must still satisfy all applicable advancement prerequisites.

## 7. Equipment and Inventory

Characters record their equipped weapons, armor, consumables, and other significant possessions. Equipment contributes its listed Point Value while available to the Character. Ordinary supplies, such as food, common ammunition, and inexpensive necessities, do not normally require individual tracking unless scarcity is relevant. Characters may carry a reasonable amount of equipment without recording exact weight or encumbrance. The GM may impose reasonable limitations or require Skill Checks when carrying unusually heavy, fragile, or cumbersome loads.

### 7.1 Acquiring Equipment

Equipment may be purchased, traded, crafted, recovered, lost, or otherwise acquired through play. Acquiring equipment does not ordinarily require XP. A Character must have legitimate access to the item and any necessary resources. When Campaign Mode is active, formal crafting and military equipment upgrades follow the appropriate Develop rules unless another established acquisition method applies. Finding or receiving an item as an actual adventure reward does not automatically require a separate Develop Order.

### 7.2 Consumables and Ordinary Supplies

Routine supplies may be handled abstractly when their exact quantities do not affect the adventure. Significant consumables, rare equipment, restricted ammunition, and other limited resources should be recorded when their availability matters. Consumables are removed from inventory when used according to their normal rules. The GM may make supply shortages an important challenge during wilderness expeditions, sieges, survival adventures, or other suitable situations.

Ordinary inventory management does not require a separate Company resource economy.

### 7.3 Company Property

During Campaign Mode, significant shared Gold and military equipment belong to the Company unless otherwise established. A Character may carry Company property without necessarily becoming its permanent owner. Military formations, Assets, and territorial resources remain governed by their own campaign rules. Ordinary narrative activities do not automatically provide free formations, additional recruitment rights, or constructed infrastructure.

## 8. Narrative Time and Campaign Integration

Narrative Play and Campaign Mode use different levels of timekeeping. Narrative Play may track minutes, hours, days, or longer periods according to the events being portrayed. Campaign Mode advances through seasonal Campaign Turns. The GM should distinguish between fictional time passing within an adventure and the completion of a strategic Turn.

### 8.1 Activities Within a Campaign Turn

One Campaign Turn may contain:

- Several travel scenes.
- Multiple encounters.
- Short and Long Rests.
- Conversations and investigations.
- A structured or narrative battle.
- An extended multi-session adventure.

These activities are all part of the current Turn when they serve its declared Company operation. They do not independently generate additional Orders or political Stakes.

### 8.2 Switching Between Play Styles

The group may move between Narrative Play, Narrative Combat, Structured Combat, and the Campaign Council as the situation requires.

When changing formats:

- Characters retain their current equipment and relevant conditions.
- Damage and injuries remain in effect unless legitimately recovered.
- Consumables already spent remain spent.
- Acquired items and Gold remain recorded.
- Political and military consequences continue to apply.
- The campaign calendar advances only when its appropriate Turn procedure is completed.

The GM should avoid awarding the same objective or reward more than once simply because it was resolved through several different play formats.

### 8.3 Persistent Consequences

Narrative decisions can affect the strategic campaign.

For example:

- A negotiation may establish the basis for a future alliance.
- An investigation may reveal an enemy military Asset.
- An expedition may discover a new resource.
- A defeated commander may no longer be available to lead a formation.
- Saving a settlement may change its attitude toward the Company.

Such consequences should be recorded when they matter. However, gaining information or favorable relationships is not automatically equivalent to obtaining a completed political agreement, territorial claim, or military formation. Strategic benefits follow their specific campaign requirements.

## 9. Character Records

Players should keep their Character Sheets updated throughout ordinary play.

| Category | Information |
| --- | --- |
| Character | Ancestry, Class, Subclass, Characteristics, Skills |
| Combat | Wounds, Damage, Attacks, Saves |
| Abilities | Talents, Commands, Spells, Special Abilities |
| Advancement | Total XP earned, XP spent, XP remaining |
| Equipment | Equipped items, consumables, significant possessions |
| Survival | Critical Injuries (0–3) |
| Point Value | Base Hero Points (100 or XP spent), Equipment Points, other explicit costs |
| Narrative | Significant relationships, obligations, injuries, and personal objectives |

During Campaign Mode, Company Gold, military formations, territorial control, and other strategic information are recorded separately using the campaign's tracking sheets.

### 9.1 Character Retirement

A Character suffering their third Critical Injury permanently dies or retires. Their Character Sheet is retired. The player may introduce a replacement according to the applicable Character Replacement rules. A replacement begins without Critical Injuries and receives an appropriate level of XP and equipment.

### 9.2 Legacy Characters

Surviving Characters from previous campaigns may return as narrative NPCs, political contacts, or Legacy Captains. Legacy Captains are represented by appropriate military profiles rather than their original Character Sheets. Their conversion, recruitment, and maximum 200-point value are governed by the Legacy Captain rules in Campaign Mode.

## 10. General Principles of Narrative Play

The following principles guide ordinary roleplaying and the interaction between narrative and strategic systems.

Describe Actions Before Rolling: Players declare their intended approach and objective. The GM determines whether a Check is necessary.

Use Checks When Outcomes Matter: Routine activities do not need a roll unless uncertainty and consequences make the result important.

Failure Should Have Consequences: An unsuccessful action may cost time, create complications, or change the situation rather than simply ending the adventure.

Preserve Character Competence: Difficulty represents the challenge itself and should not increase merely because a Character has become more capable.

Skills Remain Useful: All Characters may attempt eligible Skill actions. Talents and special abilities provide their specific advantages.

Combat Uses Combat Rules: When an encounter becomes combat, use the appropriate Narrative or Structured Combat procedures.

Recovery Does Not Remove Critical Injuries: Healing and rest restore ordinary Damage but do not erase accumulated Critical Injuries. Narrative Time Is Not Automatically Campaign Time: A long adventure may occupy multiple sessions without advancing the strategic Season. Advancement Is Not Based on Hours Played: Use Campaign Mode's normal awards or meaningful narrative milestones when playing independently.

Narrative Rewards Remain Meaningful: Information, relationships, discoveries, and personal accomplishments may have persistent consequences.

Strategic Benefits Follow Strategic Rules: Ordinary roleplaying does not automatically bypass Company Orders, recruitment rights, territorial requirements, or military point limits. The Same Character Persists Across Modes: Narrative Play, Narrative Combat, Structured Combat, and Campaign Mode use the same ongoing Character and their established circumstances.
