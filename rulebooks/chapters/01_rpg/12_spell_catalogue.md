# Spell Catalogue

Spell Attacks are magical techniques that characters may learn and use during combat. Spell Attacks follow the normal Attack rules and are considered Magic Attacks unless explicitly stated otherwise. A character may learn additional Spell Attacks from scrolls, magical texts, teachers, ancient inscriptions, or other suitable sources. Once learned, a Spell Attack is permanently known.

### Spell Acquisition

Starting spells granted by a Class or Subclass are included in the character's Base Character Value unless expressly optional with a listed cost. Additional learned Spell Attacks add their listed Points to the character's battlefield value. The printed `A` value is the **complete Attack dice value for a PC** before specific modifiers. These profiles use the provisional lower-dice rebalance described in [Equipment](11_equipment_and_crafting.md), subsequently adjusted upward again by the **v4 Damage-output rebalance** (also described there): Damage 1 spells gained **+1 Attack**, while Damage 2/Heavy spells kept their listed Attacks and gained a thematic **Anti-[Type] 1**. The **v5 Damage-output rebalance** (second pass) then gave Damage 1 spells a further **+1 Attack** and raised the Damage 2/Heavy spells' Anti-[Type] to **2**. NPC spell attacks use their own Unit profiles.

| Category | Points | Typical Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic Spell | 10 | Straightforward attack or minor specialization |
| Specialized Spell | 20 | Additional Keywords, control, or conditional effects |
| Powerful Spell | 30 | High Damage, strong effects, or unusual capabilities |

Spell Attacks:

- Do not occupy prepared Command Ability slots.
- Do not normally cost CP.
- May be improved through Prowess or Spell Prowess.
- Do not grant additional Attack actions or activations.
- Use the Assault Phase unless their Range is Engaged, in which case they use the Combat Phase.

Characters normally learn spells from their Class list and their own Subclass list. Spells from other subclasses may be made available through exceptional training, rare scrolls, or GM permission.

## MAGE SPELLS

Mage spells manipulate arcane energy, elemental forces, life and death, and perception.

### General Mage Spells

Available to all Mage Subclasses.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seeking Darts | 9" | 3 | 5+ | 1 | Magic, Pinpoint | 10 |
| Concussive Pulse | 6" | 4 | 6+ | 1 | Magic, On Damage: Push 1" | 20 |
| Arcane Lance | 8" | 1 | 5+ | 2 | Magic, Heavy, Anti-Armored 2 | 30 |

Seeking Darts: Small projectiles of arcane energy pursue exposed targets. Pinpoint allows the spell to ignore the normal penalty for attacking enemies Engaged with another Unit.

Arcane Lance: A concentrated magical projectile designed to inflict significant Damage. Its Heavy restriction prevents normal use after moving.

| --- | --- | ---: | --- | ---: | --- | ---: |

## Spell Effect Conventions

Spell Attacks follow the universal [Keywords](10_keywords.md), including **Critical X**, Blast X, Slow X, and Anti-[Type] X. An **On Damage** effect triggers only if the original target suffers at least one unsaved damaging Hit; it normally triggers once per Attack. A **Surge** effect activates only on a Surge, after rerolls. **Push X** is forced movement directly away from the caster, without illegal placements. Unless stated otherwise, area effects may affect friendly Units. For radius-based damage to whole Units (including secondary Blast Hits), use the [half-of-current-Models threshold](10_keywords.md#area-effects-against-units); each qualifying Unit receives only the listed Hits once, with no extra Hits or Saves per Model.

## Elemental Attributes

Spells may be both **Magic** and **Fire, Frost, Lightning, Radiant, or Nature**. The elemental descriptor adds no automatic mechanical effect; it matters only to abilities that reference it. For example, Troll and Hydra Regeneration is interrupted by **unsaved Damage** from attacks with the Fire attribute, not merely by a Fire Hit that was fully saved. New learned spells are used as the Unit's single Assault Attack action (or its Combat Attack action when Engaged), never as a free extra attack after Assault movement.

## NARRATIVE MAGIC

Not every magical effect needs a dedicated Spell Attack or Command Ability. Characters may use Mysticism and other appropriate Skills to attempt ordinary magical tasks outside combat.

Examples include:

- Igniting candles or small unattended objects.
- Producing harmless magical lights.
- Detecting traces of supernatural energy.
- Recognizing enchantments or magical symbols.
- Manipulating minor sounds or visual effects.
- Encouraging nearby plants to grow.
- Performing short prayers, blessings, or funeral rites.
- Communicating with cooperative spirits when narratively appropriate.

The GM determines what is reasonable based on the character's Class, training, and the circumstances. These effects generally do not cost CP, require separate Spell Attacks, or add battlefield Points. They cannot replace the mechanical effects of an existing Attack, Talent, or Command Ability without appropriate authorization.

## SPELL DESIGN GUIDELINES

The spell profiles above illustrate the intended range of ordinary magical attacks.

| Spell Role | Typical Profile |
| --- | --- |
| Basic Attack | 2–3 Attacks, Hit 5+ or 6+, Damage 1 |
| Accurate Attack | Fewer Attacks, better Hit, or Pinpoint |
| Control Attack | Damage 1 with Slow, Push, or a conditional penalty |
| Area Attack | Damage 1 with Blast, often fewer or less accurate Attacks |
| Heavy Attack | Damage 2, with Heavy or another meaningful limitation |
| Specialized Attack | Anti-[Category], conditional Critical, or a particular tactical effect |

Additional spells should generally trade one advantage for another.

For example:

- Higher Damage may require Heavy.
- Longer Range may come with fewer Attacks.
- Reliable control may require the Attack to deal Damage.
- Stronger secondary effects may require a Surge.
- Area Attacks should usually have weaker individual-target performance.

Spell Attacks should provide meaningful alternatives rather than simply replacing the starting spells with numerically superior versions. Powerful battlefield effects such as substantial healing, teleportation, broad protection, counterspelling, and major changes to terrain are generally better represented as Command Abilities granted through Talents or magical equipment.
