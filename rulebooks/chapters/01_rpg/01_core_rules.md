# Core Rules

## Terminology and Rule Priority

**Wounds** are a Character or Model’s maximum capacity for Damage; record accumulated **Damage** separately. When accumulated Damage equals or exceeds Wounds, the Character is **Defeated**. Do not use *Health* or *Downed* as separate rules terms.

Temporary Wounds are pending Damage, not increased maximum Wounds. They do not immediately defeat Models; on the Unit's next unsaved Damage event, add all pending temporary Wounds once before ordinary allocation, then clear them. Heal X may remove temporary Wounds before they convert into Damage, using the same healing allowance as for accumulated Damage. See [Structured Combat — Damage and Defeat](16_structured_combat.md#16-damage-and-defeat).

Specific rules override these general rules. See [Keywords](10_keywords.md) for combat effects, [Advancement](03_experience_and_advancement.md) for XP/Points, and the relevant full combat chapter for phase sequencing.

## 1. Skill Checks

Skill Checks resolve uncertain actions where both success and failure have meaningful consequences.

When a Character attempts an action:

- The Player describes what their Character is attempting and how.
- The GM determines the appropriate Characteristic and Skill.
- The GM sets the Difficulty of the Check.
- Roll a number of D6 equal to the Characteristic.
- Each die equal to or greater than the Skill rating generates 1 Success.
- If the number of Successes equals or exceeds the Difficulty, the Check succeeds.

Routine actions, or actions without meaningful consequences for failure, do not require a Check.

### Difficulty

Difficulty represents how challenging an action is under the circumstances.

| Difficulty | Required Successes | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Easy | 1 | A straightforward task with meaningful risk |
| Normal | 2 | A task requiring competence or favorable circumstances |
| Hard | 3 | A serious challenge requiring considerable expertise |
| Extreme | 4 | An exceptional feat beyond ordinary capabilities |
| Legendary | 5 | An extraordinary achievement requiring exceptional ability |

The GM determines Difficulty based on the task, circumstances, and the Character's approach. Difficulty is not restricted by the Character's available dice pool. Some actions may be beyond a Character's current capabilities without assistance, preparation, or improved abilities.

### Example: Climbing a Damaged Wall

**Character:** Body 4, Athletics 5+. **Task:** Climb a damaged wall (Normal, 2 Successes). **Roll:** 2, 3, 5, 6.

The 5 and 6 generate 2 Successes, meeting the Difficulty. The Character successfully climbs the wall.

### Excess Successes

Successes beyond the required Difficulty are called Excess Successes. Excess Successes may improve the quality or effectiveness of a successful action at the GM's discretion.

Examples include:

- Completing the action more quickly.
- Accomplishing the task with greater precision.
- Producing a higher-quality result.
- Gaining additional information.
- Improving the effectiveness of a crafted item.

Excess Successes do not automatically generate additional mechanical benefits unless a rule specifically allows them. Surges remain separate from Excess Successes and provide their own benefits.

### Specific Rules and Abilities

The standard Difficulty table is intended primarily for narrative actions, exploration, social interactions, and other unstructured situations. Talents, Commands, Spells, and other abilities may establish their own Success requirements or resolution methods. These specific rules override the standard Difficulty system.

For example, an ability may:

- Require a fixed number of Successes to activate.
- Produce an effect for each Success generated.
- Scale its effectiveness according to Excess Successes.
- Use the highest die rolled rather than the total number of Successes.
- Resolve its effects using a separate combat mechanic.

When an ability scales with X Successes, X represents the number of Successes generated unless its description specifies otherwise. There is no additional cap on success-scaled effects unless the ability specifically establishes one. Skill Checks made during Structured Combat follow the relevant combat or ability rules wherever those rules differ from the standard Skill Check procedure.

## 2. Characteristics

Characteristics represent a Character's fundamental physical, mental, and supernatural capabilities.

| Characteristic | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| Body | Strength, endurance, physical resilience |
| Agility | Dexterity, coordination, reflexes, fine motor control |
| Mind | Reasoning, memory, perception, technical knowledge |
| Spirit | Willpower, personality, courage, supernatural aptitude |

Characteristics determine the number of D6 rolled during a Skill Check. Characteristics range from 2–5 during Character Creation and cannot normally exceed 6. Some Ancestries, Talents, and other abilities may modify Characteristics.

## 3. Skills

Skills represent training, experience, and practical expertise.

| Skill | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| Athletics | Climbing, swimming, jumping, feats of strength |
| Stealth | Sneaking, hiding, concealment, sleight of hand |
| Survival | Tracking, navigation, wilderness knowledge, animals |
| Awareness | Observation, searching, vigilance, intuition |
| Lore | History, monsters, cultures, scholarship |
| Craft | Engineering, machinery, traps, locks, repairs, professions |
| Medicine | Treatment, diagnosis, first aid, anatomy |
| Influence | Persuasion, deception, intimidation, performance |
| Mysticism | Magic, religion, rituals, supernatural phenomena |

Each Skill has a rating representing the minimum result required to generate a Success on each D6.

| Skill Rating | Description |
| --- | --- |
| 6+ | Untrained |
| 5+ | Familiar |
| 4+ | Trained |
| 3+ | Expert |
| 2+ | Master |

All Skills begin at 6+ unless improved through Character Creation or another rule. Improving a Skill reduces its required result by one step: 6+ → 5+ → 4+ → 3+ → 2+ Skills cannot normally improve beyond 2+.

### Characteristic and Skill Pairings

Characteristics and Skills are not fixed pairs. The Skill represents what the Character is attempting. The Characteristic represents how the Character approaches the task. The same Skill may therefore be paired with different Characteristics.

| Combination | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Body + Athletics | Force open a gate, swim against a current |
| Agility + Athletics | Balance on a beam, perform a difficult leap |
| Mind + Survival | Identify tracks, navigate unfamiliar terrain |
| Body + Survival | Endure a forced march |
| Spirit + Survival | Remain composed while isolated in hostile wilderness |
| Spirit + Influence | Inspire, intimidate, persuade, perform |
| Mind + Influence | Construct a legal argument, exploit specialized knowledge |
| Mind + Craft | Repair a mechanism, identify a mechanical fault |
| Agility + Craft | Disarm a delicate trap, perform precision work |
| Mind + Medicine | Diagnose an illness |
| Agility + Medicine | Perform delicate surgery |
| Spirit + Mysticism | Channel magical energy, resist a supernatural influence |
| Mind + Mysticism | Interpret a ritual, identify a magical phenomenon |

Players should describe their approach before the GM determines the appropriate pairing. If multiple pairings are reasonable, the GM may allow the Player to choose the approach most appropriate to their Character.

## 4. Surges and Blunders

Exceptional dice results may generate additional effects independently of success or failure.

| Result | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Surge | At least two natural 6s in the same roll |
| Blunder | At least two natural 1s in the same roll |

A roll normally generates no more than one Surge and one Blunder unless another rule specifically permits otherwise. Surges and Blunders are determined from the final dice results, after all rerolls have been resolved. They do not change the number of Successes generated unless an ability specifically states otherwise. A Check may succeed or fail while also generating a Surge, a Blunder, or both.

### Surges

A Surge represents an exceptional opportunity, favorable development, or unusually effective execution. Talents, equipment, and abilities may specify their own Surge effects.

Otherwise, the GM may grant an appropriate secondary benefit, such as:

- Completing the action more quickly.
- Discovering additional information.
- Remaining unnoticed.
- Conserving supplies or resources.
- Improving the Character's position.
- Impressing an NPC or establishing a favorable impression.
- Discovering another opportunity.
- Granting +1D6 to a closely related follow-up Check.

A Surge normally improves the result rather than replacing the Successes required for an ability.

### Blunders

A Blunder introduces an unexpected complication, even when the action succeeds.

Possible consequences include:

- Taking additional time.
- Making noise or attracting attention.
- Consuming additional supplies.
- Damaging or losing equipment.
- Revealing information.
- Losing advantageous positioning.
- Introducing a temporary disadvantage.
- Creating a new hazard or complication.

A Blunder does not automatically cause failure. If a roll generates both a Surge and a Blunder, resolve both effects. The Character may accomplish something exceptionally well while simultaneously creating an unexpected problem.

### Example: Success with a Blunder

A Rogue attempts to disable a mechanical alarm using Agility + Craft. They generate 2 Successes and a Blunder. The alarm is successfully disabled, but a component breaks during the process. The mechanism can no longer be reactivated without repairs. The intended action succeeds, but the Blunder introduces a meaningful consequence.

## 5. Group Checks

When multiple Characters cooperate, the GM determines whether the situation calls for a Lead Character or Individual Checks.

### Lead Character

One Character makes a Skill Check on behalf of the group. Meaningful assistance from another Character grants the Lead Character +1D6 to the Check. Additional Characters may contribute narratively, but the assistance bonus does not normally stack.

Use a Lead Character Check when the group shares a single objective, such as:

- Navigating unfamiliar territory.
- Researching an ancient ruin.
- Negotiating with an NPC.
- Tracking a creature.
- Repairing a vehicle or mechanism.
- Preparing a shared campsite.

The outcome generally applies to the group as a whole.

### Individual Checks

Each Character makes their own Check when success or failure affects them independently. Examples include sneaking past guards, crossing hazardous terrain, swimming through a dangerous current, or resisting supernatural fear. Each Character resolves their own Successes, Surges, and Blunders.


## Combat-specific Surge exception

For an **Attack roll**, each complete pair of natural sixes creates one Surge and each complete pair of natural ones creates one Blunder, after rerolls. Ordinary Skill Checks and Charge rolls normally generate only one Surge and one Blunder, regardless of how many qualifying dice are present, unless a specific rule says otherwise. Effects that say **when an Attack Surges** trigger once per Attack unless they specifically say **for each Surge**. The combat chapters and [Keywords](10_keywords.md) explain this exception.
