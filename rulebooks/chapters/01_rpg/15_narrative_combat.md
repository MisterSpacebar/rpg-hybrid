# NARRATIVE COMBAT

## Overview

Narrative Combat is a streamlined combat system for ordinary encounters, minor bosses, smaller skirmishes, and theatre-of-the-mind play.

It uses the same fundamental mechanics as Structured Combat, including Unit profiles, Attacks, Wounds, Armor, Evade, Ward, Command Points, Talents, equipment, Keywords, and Surge and Blunder rules.

The principal differences are positioning, movement, and encounter flow.

Narrative Combat uses Player and GM Turns rather than alternating individual Unit activations. Exact distances are replaced with Range Bands, allowing encounters to be resolved without a tabletop or miniatures.

## 1. Units and Positioning

A Unit represents a single Character, creature, or group of combatants acting together.

Player Characters and important NPCs are normally individual Units. Groups of ordinary enemies may be represented as multi-model Units to reduce the number of separate actions.

### Range Bands

Units are positioned relative to other Units and important locations using four Range Bands.

| Range Band | Approximate Distance            |
| ---------- | ------------------------------- |
| Engaged    | Melee range, normally within 1" |
| Short      | Outside Engagement, up to 6"    |
| Medium     | Beyond 6", up to 12"            |
| Long       | Beyond 12"                      |

Range Bands describe relationships rather than fixed areas of the battlefield.

For example, a Warrior might be Engaged with an Orc, while a Rogue is at Short Range and a Mage is at Medium Range from that Orc.

A Unit may have different Range Band relationships with different targets.

### Attack and Ability Ranges

Convert listed distances into Range Bands:

- Engagement-range Attacks remain Engaged.
- Ranges up to 6" become Short.
- Ranges beyond 6" and up to 12" become Medium.
- Ranges beyond 12" become Long.

An Attack may target enemies within its maximum Range Band, provided all other targeting requirements are satisfied.

Long Range does not represent unlimited distance. The GM determines whether particularly distant targets remain within an Attack's actual reach.

Abilities with unusual distances or precise positioning requirements may use their original measurements when necessary.

### Line of Sight and Accessibility

The GM determines whether a Unit can see or reach another Unit based on the established fiction.

Terrain, structures, obstacles, and environmental conditions may:

- Block Line of Sight.
- Provide Cover.
- Create elevation differences.
- Restrict movement.
- Prevent Engagement.
- Require Skill Checks to overcome.

These conditions should be established before the relevant action is resolved.

## 2. Deployment and Reserves

Before an encounter begins, the GM establishes the initial positions of participating Units and any important entry points.

Unless an encounter specifies otherwise, allied and enemy forces begin in their respective starting areas.

### Reserves

When an encounter uses an army roster, each side may keep up to half of its total army Point Value in Reserves.

Reserve Units begin the encounter outside the battlefield.

Units in Reserves:

- Cannot activate or make Attacks.
- Cannot normally use abilities or Reactions.
- Cannot be targeted by enemy effects.
- Cannot control or contest objectives.
- Do not provide battlefield auras or other positional effects.

### Deploying Reserves

At the end of its Movement Phase, after its active Units have completed their Movement activations, a side may deploy any number of its remaining Reserve Units.

Reserve Units enter through friendly or otherwise established entry points, as determined by the encounter.

They must enter in positions consistent with the fiction and cannot enter directly into Engagement unless a special rule permits it.

A Unit deployed from Reserves:

- Counts as having completed its Movement activation.
- Cannot make a normal Move or Engage during that Movement Phase.
- Cannot perform Combat Skill Actions.
- Cannot use Movement Phase Command Abilities.
- May act normally during its subsequent Assault and Combat Phases, if eligible.

Deployment counts as a Movement Action for determining eligibility to use Heavy during the same Turn.

A Unit may remain in Reserves for any number of Rounds unless the encounter specifies otherwise.

Undeployed Units do not count as defeated merely because they never entered the encounter.

### Encounters Without Point Values

For ordinary narrative encounters without an army roster, the GM may allow up to half of each side's participating Units, rounded down, to begin in Reserves.

The GM may instead establish Reserve availability according to the encounter's circumstances.

Special deployment abilities may override normal Reserve entry restrictions.

## 3. Rounds and Turns

Combat is divided into Rounds.

Each Round normally follows this sequence:

1. Player Turn
2. GM Turn
3. End of Round

Players normally act first.

If enemies begin an encounter with an ambush, surprise, or another clear advantage, the GM may take the first Turn during the opening Round.

Subsequent Rounds return to the normal Player-first order unless a special rule states otherwise.

### Turn Structure

Each Turn contains three Phases:

Movement → Assault → Combat

During the Player Turn, Player Characters and allied Units act.

During the GM Turn, enemy Units act.

Within each Phase, the acting side may resolve its Units in any order.

Each Unit may normally receive:

- One Movement activation.
- One Assault activation.
- One Combat activation, if eligible.

A Unit may choose to do nothing during an activation. This still counts as its activation for that Phase.

All eligible Units on the acting side finish the current Phase before proceeding to the next.

There is no alternating Unit activation between opposing sides during Narrative Combat.

Reactions and other triggered abilities may interrupt either side's Turn whenever their requirements are satisfied.

## 4. Movement Phase

During Movement, each eligible Unit may:

- Make a normal Move.
- Engage an enemy.
- Disengage.
- Reposition.
- Remain stationary.
- Use eligible Movement abilities.

Characters may also perform Combat Skill Actions by spending CP.

### Normal Movement

A Unit may normally move one Range Band toward or away from another Unit or important location.

Examples:

- Long → Medium
- Medium → Short
- Short → Engaged
- Engaged → Short, by Disengaging

Alternatively, a Unit may substantially reposition within its current Range Band, such as moving behind Cover, changing its approach, or crossing a room.

A Unit must have a plausible and accessible path to its destination.

### Movement Characteristics

Exact Move characteristics are normally ignored.

However, Move remains relevant when relative speed matters, including chases, races, contested movement, dangerous terrain, and special movement abilities.

When one Unit is meaningfully faster than another, the GM may grant +1D6 to an appropriate movement-related Skill Check.

Special movement abilities and penalties may alter what movement is possible.

### Engaging

A Unit at Short Range may use its Movement activation to enter Engagement with an accessible enemy.

This is a normal Engage and causes no Impact.

### Disengaging

A Unit beginning its Movement activation Engaged may Disengage.

The Unit moves from Engaged to Short Range and becomes Withdrawn until the end of the Round.

A Withdrawn Unit:

- May make an ordinary Assault Move.
- May use eligible non-Attack abilities.
- Cannot make an Assault Attack.
- Cannot Charge.
- Cannot voluntarily Engage another enemy.
- Cannot control objectives.

If an enemy subsequently Engages a Withdrawn Unit, it may defend normally and make eligible Combat Attacks.

## 5. Combat Skill Actions

During its Movement activation, a Character may spend 1 Command Point to attempt one ordinary Skill Check relevant to the encounter.

The Character rolls its normal Characteristic and Skill combination. The GM determines the Difficulty based on the circumstances.

Examples include:

- Stealth: Hide from enemies or move unnoticed.
- Athletics: Climb obstacles, leap gaps, or overcome physical hazards.
- Awareness: Search for hidden enemies, hazards, or important details.
- Survival: Navigate dangerous terrain or identify environmental threats.
- Medicine: Perform an appropriate medical action.
- Other Skills: Attempt any reasonable action supported by the fiction.

A Combat Skill Action may be performed before or after normal Movement if the action permits it.

The GM may require the Character to remain stationary or use its Movement to complete certain actions.

A Character may normally attempt one Combat Skill Action per Movement Phase.

Combat Skill Actions do not automatically grant additional movement or Attacks.

Talents, equipment, and other rules may modify the cost, timing, or effects of Skill Actions.

### Stealth During Combat

Any Character may spend 1 CP to attempt an appropriate Stealth Check during Movement.

A plausible means of avoiding detection is required, such as obscuring terrain, darkness, distractions, or a suitable hiding place.

On success, the Character may become Concealed from enemies it successfully hides from, subject to the normal Concealed rules.

Concealment may end when the Character attacks, reveals its position, leaves its hiding place, or is discovered.

The GM may require additional checks when the Character attempts to move through exposed areas.

## 6. Terrain, Cover, and Elevation

Terrain is represented through established positioning rather than exact coordinates.

The GM determines whether terrain provides protection, restricts movement, or affects Line of Sight.

### Difficult Terrain

Crossing difficult or dangerous terrain may require an appropriate Skill Check.

The GM may also restrict movement or require additional movement where appropriate.

Abilities that ignore Difficult Terrain apply normally.

### Cover

A Unit benefiting from meaningful protection provided by terrain gains Cover 1 against ranged Attacks.

Cover X allows a Unit to use its Evade Save against an eligible ranged Attack and reroll up to X natural 1s when making those Evade Saves.

The new result must be kept.

Multiple applicable sources of Cover X add together unless a rule states otherwise.

For example:

Cover 1 + Cover 1 = Cover 2

Multiple obstacles providing ordinary physical Cover count as one source and do not grant multiple instances of Cover 1.

Cover does not improve Armor or Ward.

### Elevation

A Unit occupying meaningfully higher ground than its attacker gains Cover 1 against ranged Attacks originating from below.

Meaningful elevation includes rooftops, balconies, raised platforms, fortifications, cliffs, and similar positions.

A negligible height difference does not grant this benefit.

Elevation does not automatically count as physical Cover. It provides a separate source of Cover 1.

### Combined Cover and Elevation

Physical Cover and favorable Elevation may apply simultaneously.

A Unit behind Cover on meaningfully higher ground gains:

Cover 2

For example, a Character with an existing Cover 1 ability standing behind a wall on a rooftop would have Cover 3 against eligible ranged Attacks from below.

Each die can still only be rerolled once, and only natural 1s are eligible.

### Cover and Engagement

The Cover keyword also functions whenever another rule already permits an Evade Save, provided the source of Cover applies to that Attack.

Ordinary terrain Cover and elevation bonuses apply against ranged Attacks only.

A separate ability granting Cover X may specify different conditions.

Cover does not permit Evade against Charge Impact unless a special rule explicitly overrides Impact's normal restriction.

## 7. Assault Phase

When a Unit is selected during the acting side's Assault Phase, it chooses **exactly one** ordinary action:

| Action | Resolution |
| --- | --- |
| **Assault Move** | Shift one Range Band or meaningfully reposition without entering Engagement |
| **Assault Attack** | Make one eligible ranged Weapon or Spell Attack |
| **Charge** | Attempt to enter Engagement from an eligible Range Band and inflict Impact if successful |

A Unit may choose to do nothing. An ordinary Assault Move cannot be combined with an ordinary Assault Attack during the same activation. **Eligible Assault Commands** are separate CP-paid abilities used by the selected Unit during its own activation, unless their text replaces the chosen action.

A Withdrawn Unit may Assault Move or use eligible non-Attack Commands, but cannot make an Assault Attack or Charge. An Engaged Unit cannot make an ordinary Assault Move but may use a legal Assault Attack. Shooting while Engaged permits the defender to Evade. Shooting into a friendly Engagement worsens Hit by one step; Pinpoint ignores that penalty.

### Heavy

Heavy requires that the Unit did not voluntarily move during its preceding Movement Phase. Changing Range Bands, substantially repositioning, or entering from Reserves counts as movement. Forced movement does not ordinarily prevent Heavy.

## 8. Charging

Narrative Charges use **number of successes**, rather than computing a distance in inches. Eligible Units cannot Charge while already Engaged or Withdrawn.

| Starting relationship | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| **Short → Engaged** | At least **1 Success** |
| **Medium → Engaged** | At least **2 Successes**, and only if the fiction, movement, or a special mobility effect permits a Charge across that separation |
| **Long → Engaged** | Not normally possible in one Charge; requires a specific exceptional ability or scenario rule |

A Character rolls **Body + Athletics** normally; a Rogue may instead roll **Agility + Athletics**. An ordinary NPC Unit without full Characteristics rolls **2D6**, with **4+ per die** counting as a Success. The GM may apply contextual modifiers or an explicit special rule. **CHARGE X** increases a Unit's effective reach in the fiction and can justify a longer permitted Charge, but **does not automatically grant Successes**; a specific ability may say otherwise.

If the required Successes are generated and the path is plausible, the Unit enters Engagement. Otherwise, it remains in place and its Assault activation ends. A PC may spend each Success above the Range Band requirement to add +1 Impact on a successful Charge. The Structured Combat option to spend Momentum for +1" Charge distance applies only to measured-inch combat.

### Charge Impact and Special Results

On a successful Charge, **Impact equals the highest die rolled**, plus printed **Impact X** and other bonuses, minus the defender's **Brace X**, to a minimum of zero. This generates one pool of automatic Physical Hits at Damage 1 against the target Unit, regardless of Model count. Resolve **Armor → Ward → Damage**, without Evade. Successful Chargers may fight during their later Combat Phase if eligible.

The Charge may generate one Surge from at least two natural sixes, or one Blunder from at least two natural ones, after rerolls. A successful Charge that Blunders becomes **Off-Balance** until the end of the Round (Evade worsens one step). Surges have only expressly granted effects.

## 9. Combat Phase

During Combat, each eligible Unit may:

- Make one Melee Attack.
- Use eligible Combat abilities.

A Unit is eligible if it:

- Is Engaged.
- Possesses a Combat ability it may legally activate.
- Is made eligible by another rule.

An Melee Attack requires the target to be within the chosen Attack profile's Engagement Range.

A Unit normally attacks one enemy Unit during its Combat activation unless a special rule permits otherwise.

Multi-model Units combine eligible Attacks, resolving different profiles separately as necessary.

Non-Engaged Units may use Combat abilities that explicitly permit their activation.

## 10. Attack Resolution and Defense

All Attacks follow the same sequence:

1. Choose an Attack profile and legal target.
2. Determine the Attack dice and roll to Hit.
3. Resolve rerolls (Accurate and other permitted effects), then count Surges and Blunders from final dice.
4. Apply Critical X after rerolls, including any Critical granted by a Surge. Converted failed dice become Critical Hits; their rolled faces do not change.
5. Roll Armor Saves against ordinary Hits. Critical Hits bypass Armor and Evade.
6. Roll Evade Saves against remaining ordinary Hits, if eligible.
7. Roll applicable Ward Saves against remaining normal and Critical Hits.
8. Apply Damage.

Every Attack die meeting or exceeding the Attack's Hit value generates one Hit.

### Armor

For each Hit, roll one D6 against the target's Armor.

Each successful Armor Save cancels one Hit.

### Tough X

Tough X allows the defender to reroll up to X natural 1s when making Armor Saves.

Applicable sources add together unless stated otherwise.

Each die may be rerolled once, and the new result must be kept.

Tough does not change Armor or grant additional Save dice.

### Evade

Evade is normally available against Melee Attacks and ranged or Spell Attacks made by an enemy Engaged with the defender.

Cover X also permits Evade against eligible ranged Attacks.

For every Hit remaining after Armor, roll one Evade Save when eligible.

Each successful Evade Save cancels one Hit.

### Cover X

Cover X allows the defender to reroll up to X natural 1s when making eligible Evade Saves.

Cover X does not improve Evade or grant additional Save dice.

Applicable sources add together, but each die may only be rerolled once.

A Unit with no Evade value cannot benefit from Cover X unless another rule grants it an Evade value.

### Ward

Ward represents extraordinary or supernatural protection.

For each Hit remaining after Armor and Evade, roll the best applicable Ward.

Wards may specify Physical or Magic protection.

When multiple Wards apply, use only the best applicable Ward.

Ward Saves do not stack.

### Defense Summary

| Situation                                 | Save Sequence                  |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Normal Ranged Attack                      | Armor → Ward                   |
| Ranged Attack against Cover               | Armor → Evade (Cover 1) → Ward |
| Ranged Attack against Higher Elevation    | Armor → Evade (Cover 1) → Ward |
| Ranged Attack against Cover and Elevation | Armor → Evade (Cover 2) → Ward |
| Melee Attack                         | Armor → Evade → Ward           |
| Charge Impact                             | Armor → Ward                   |

Other applicable Keywords modify their respective Saves.

### Save Modifiers

Improving a Save moves its required value one step toward 2+:

6+ → 5+ → 4+ → 3+ → 2+

Worsening a Save reverses this progression.

Saves cannot normally improve beyond 2+.

A Save worsened beyond 6+ becomes unavailable.

## 11. Damage and Defeat

Every Hit remaining after all applicable Saves inflicts the Attack's listed Damage.

Damage accumulates against a Model's Wounds.

Ordinary Models are defeated and removed when their accumulated Damage equals or exceeds their Wounds.

Player Characters and important named NPCs instead become Defeated.

A Defeated Character cannot normally:

- Move.
- Attack.
- Use Command Abilities.
- Control objectives.

Recovery, injury, and death follow the campaign rules.

Damage allocation for multi-model Units follows the normal Unit rules.

**Temporary Wounds:** Abilities may record temporary Wounds as pending Damage without immediately removing Models. When the Unit next suffers Damage after Saves, add all pending temporary Wounds once to that Damage, clear them, and allocate normally. Heal X may remove temporary Wounds or accumulated Damage before conversion, up to its normal X total. See [Structured Combat — Damage and Defeat](16_structured_combat.md#16-damage-and-defeat) for the complete limits and conversion rule.

**Concealed detection:** In tactical measurement, an enemy Unit coming within 4" of a Concealed Unit ends Concealed immediately; it cannot be regained while the enemy remains within 4". In unmeasured narrative scenes, use the established close-range positioning to represent discovery.

## 12. Command Points and Abilities

Command Points (CP) represent tactical coordination and exceptional actions.

Unless an encounter states otherwise:

- Each Player receives 2 CP.
- The GM receives a shared pool of **max(2, 2 × number of PCs − 1)** CP (for example, five PCs give the GM 9 CP against the players' combined 10 CP).

All CP pools refresh at the beginning of every Round.

Unused CP do not carry over.

Players spend their own CP on eligible Character abilities, equipment, Talents, and other effects.

The GM spends its shared pool on enemy abilities and encounter effects.

### Command timing and priority

A normal Unit Command with **Movement**, **Assault**, or **Combat** timing may be used only **during that Unit's own activation** in that Phase, when its controlling side has priority and has selected that Unit to activate. The Command may affect another Unit if the ability allows it, but a player cannot activate an unselected ally's personal Command. A Command does not consume the Unit's chosen Phase action unless stated otherwise; its CP cost is paid normally. An eligible Unit may use a Command even if it chooses to forgo its ordinary Phase action.

**Faction and Doctrine Commands** belong to the controlling side. Unless they are Reactions, use them while that side holds activation priority in the permitted Phase, subject to their specific costs and restrictions. They do not permit a personal Command on another Unit.

**Reaction Commands** may interrupt the specified trigger even when the reacting side does not hold priority. Passives apply without spending CP or obtaining priority.

### Ability Timing

Movement: Used during Movement.

Assault: Used during Assault.

Combat: Used during Combat.

Reaction: Used when its listed trigger occurs.

Instant: Cannot be responded to with another Command Ability.

Phase-specific abilities are normally used during the relevant Unit's activation.

### Reactions

A Reaction interrupts the triggering action.

Resolve the Reaction completely, then resume the interrupted action.

A Unit does not need to be currently active to use a Reaction.

Reactions do not ordinarily consume the Unit's normal activation.

### Area Effects

The GM identifies affected Units from their established positions. Where a measured area is relevant, a Unit qualifies if at least **half its current Models, rounded up**, are inside the radius. Resolve the effect **once per Unit**, not per Model. Delayed automatic Hits (such as Arcane Storm) ignore Evade but allow Armor and Ward; Blast uses its specified secondary-hit saves. See [Keywords](10_keywords.md#area-effects-against-units).

## 13. Surges, Blunders, and Keywords

Normal Surge and Blunder rules apply.

- Ordinary Checks and Charges: **at least two** natural sixes generate **one Surge**; at least two natural ones generate **one Blunder**.
- Attack rolls: **one Surge for each complete pair of sixes** and **one Blunder for each complete pair of ones**.
- Effects worded **when an Attack Surges** trigger once per Attack, unless they explicitly say **for each Surge**.
- A roll may generate both a Surge and a Blunder.
- Each die may normally be rerolled only once.
- Determine Surges and Blunders after rerolls.

Surges and Blunders have no universal effects unless otherwise specified.

They activate effects provided by equipment, weapons, Talents, abilities, or other rules.

All normal Keywords remain available during Narrative Combat.

Effects involving exact movement, distance, or positioning are interpreted through Range Bands and the fiction.

## 14. GM Turn and End of Round

After the Player Turn, the GM resolves:

Movement → Assault → Combat

Enemy Units use the same rules.

The GM chooses the order of eligible Units within each Phase.

Reserve Units enter at the end of the GM's Movement Phase, before Assault.

Players may still use Reactions triggered during the GM Turn.

After both sides have completed their Turns:

1. Resolve End-of-Round effects.
2. Remove Withdrawn and Off-Balance.
3. Expire other conditions and effects ending this Round.
4. Advance the Round counter.
5. Refresh CP and other Round-based resources at the beginning of the next Round.

Narrative Combat has no separate Scoring Phase unless the encounter requires one.

Objective control and progress are normally resolved at the end of the Round.

## 15. Ending an Encounter

Narrative Combat ends when the conflict has been resolved.

Enemies may be defeated, surrender, flee, withdraw, or complete their objectives.

Players may similarly succeed or fail without eliminating every opponent.

The GM determines when further combat is unnecessary.

## 16. Switching to Structured Combat

An encounter may transition to Structured Combat when exact positioning and tactical decisions become important.

Place Models according to their established relative positions:

- Engaged: Within melee reach.
- Short: Outside Engagement, approximately 1–6".
- Medium: Approximately 6–12".
- Long: Beyond 12".

Preserve terrain relationships, including Cover, elevation, Line of Sight, and Engagement.

Carry over accumulated Damage, remaining Wounds, current Command Points, conditions, ongoing effects, and Reserve status.

Whenever possible, transition between Rounds.

If transitioning during a Round, preserve which Units have already acted to prevent unintended additional activations.

Continue using Structured Combat's Priority, Deployment, and activation rules.
