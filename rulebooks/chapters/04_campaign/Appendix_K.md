# APPENDIX K — CAMPAIGN EXAMPLES

## Campaign Mode v4.3

These examples illustrate the interaction of narrative adventures, political Stakes, Company Orders, army-building, and persistent strategic consequences.

They demonstrate intended procedures rather than prescribing particular campaign outcomes.

# K.1 Standard Campaign Setup

Four PCs select Concord as their affiliated Grand Faction.

The Principal Opposition is the Legions, represented by the Iron Regency.

The Conclave and Wayfarers are participating independent powers.

| Setting                    | Value                                 |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Campaign Format            | Standard                              |
| Total Turns                | 25                                    |
| Act Lengths                | 4 / 5 / 7 / 9                         |
| Grand Factions             | Concord, Legions, Conclave, Wayfarers |
| Capital Tiles              | C2, C3, C4, C5                        |
| Frontier Tiles             | N1, N2, N3                            |
| Starting Sites             | 7                                     |
| Starting Council Influence | PCs                                   |
| Starting Hero Base         | 100 points per PC                     |

The GM assembles a connected map and identifies important crossings, commercial institutions, and the possible routes between Capitals.

Each Grand Faction controls its own Capital Province.

Each Frontier has one Local Authority-controlled region and two unclaimed regions.

The Company begins without its Act II Charter or Patron formations.

# K.2 Campaign Calendar

| Act                       | Turns | Opening Season |
| ------------------------- | ----- | -------------- |
| I — The Gathering Storm   | 1–4   | Spring         |
| II — Founding the Company | 5–9   | Spring         |
| III — Rising Powers       | 10–16 | Summer         |
| IV — Final Reckoning      | 17–25 | Spring         |

The seven Company Orders refresh in Spring.

Therefore:

- Turn 1 begins with a full Order hand.
- Turn 5 begins with a refreshed hand.
- Turn 9 begins another Year and refreshes the hand.
- Turn 10 begins Act III, but its Order availability carries over from Turn 9.
- Turn 17 begins Act IV and also begins a new Year.

# K.3 Act I — Exploration and Political Contact

The PCs investigate unrest around Stonebridge Corridor.

They learn that the Iron Regency is increasing patrols, the Wayfarers wish to preserve established caravan rights, and the Conclave seeks access to regional research sites.

## K.3.1 Exploring a Site

During Turn 2, the Company selects Explore.

The PCs investigate an unresolved Site at N1-A.

After completing the adventure, they draw Crystal Vein.

The reward provides 1 Gold and reveals an additional Stone resource source.

The Site also awards 5 XP to every PC.

At the conclusion of the Campaign Turn, each PC receives another 5 XP.

Thus, the Turn provides 10 XP per PC in total.

The Site is now resolved.

Its discovered Stone source does not automatically provide political control of N1-A.

## K.3.2 An Independent Political Winner

During Turn 3, the Wayfarers win a Warfare Stake concerning a regional crossing.

They receive:

- One Wayfarer-colored Act marker.
- One Development reward draw.
- Their ordinary eligible Warfare map action.

The PCs receive neither the marker nor the Development reward merely because they supported the Wayfarers.

The crossing's legal territorial ownership does not automatically change.

## K.3.3 Concluding Act I

Act I lasts four Turns.

Suppose the Act Track finishes:

| Faction   | Colored Markers |
| --------- | --------------- |
| Concord   | 1               |
| Legions   | 1               |
| Conclave  | 0               |
| Wayfarers | 1               |
| Neutral   | 1               |

Three factions tie for the highest colored-marker total.

The Act is Contested.

Each participating Grand Faction receives one eligible minor Development consolation.

The Legions do not receive an additional second consolation merely because they are the Principal Opposition.

# K.4 Entering Act II

At the beginning of Turn 5:

1. Every PC receives 5 Act Advancement XP.
2. Concord receives its Provincial Dividend.
3. The Company receives its Concord Charter.
4. The Company receives two free eligible Patron formations.
5. Each PC gains two appropriate Patron recruitment slots.
6. The GM establishes the Act II Pivotal Objective and first important Opposition Operation.
7. A new Act Agenda and Task pool are prepared.
8. NPC Maneuver decks refresh.
9. Company Orders refresh because Turn 5 is Spring.

Concord controls its Capital Province.

With four PCs, the Provincial Dividend is:

4 Gold.

The Company does not yet control a second Province.

# K.5 Hero Point Calculation

The battlefield value of a PC is based on **XP spent on Talents**, not XP merely earned:

`Hero Points = max(100, XP spent on Talents) + Equipped Equipment Points + other explicit Point costs`

Assume a PC has earned **30 XP** by Act II, spent **25 XP on Talents**, and owns **15 Points** of equipped gear. Their Hero has **100 + 15 = 115 Points**. Five unspent XP does not raise battlefield value. Under the Standard Act II allowance of 250 Points, **135 Points** remain for legal formations and other purchases.

Later in Act III, the same Character has earned **110 XP**, spent **85 XP on Talents**, and has **20 equipment Points**. Their Hero is worth **100 + 20 = 120 Points**, leaving **255 Points** under their Act III 375-Point allowance. If they legally spend an additional 25 XP on Talents before the next battle, the Hero becomes **110 + 20 = 130 Points**, leaving 245 Points.

This distinction does not change actual Talent prerequisites or grant combat value for unspent XP. Finalize Talent spending and equipped items before choosing a battle detachment.

# K.6 Turn 6 — Commerce and Income

Turn 6 is Summer.

The PCs select Commerce (2) and choose Earn Income.

Four participating PCs generate:

4 Gold.

If Commerce qualifies for Supremacy, the Company receives one additional Gold, for a total of 5.

Suppose the PCs previously played Explore (1) during the current Year.

Commerce (2) is higher and gains Supremacy.

The Company receives 5 Gold.

This income does not depend on how many Provinces Concord controls.

The Company does not also hire a mercenary through this Commerce action.

The political Stake and NPC commitments still resolve normally.

# K.7 Turn 7 — Constructing a Province

At the beginning of Turn 7, the Company controls only its Capital Province.

It selects Construct (4).

The number 4 exceeds the previously played Commerce (2), so Construct receives Supremacy.

The Company undertakes two legal Minor Improvements:

- Frontier Outpost at an eligible unclaimed hex N1-A: 2 Gold.
- Frontier Outpost at an eligible unclaimed hex N1-B: 2 Gold.

Assume both have valid approaches and satisfy the necessary resource requirements, and that N1-B becomes accessible through N1-A as the projects resolve.

The combined cost is 4 Gold.

Construct Supremacy reduces it by 1 Gold.

Total: 3 Gold.

Concord now controls N1-A and N1-B.

Stonebridge Council retains N1-H.

Since Concord owns two of the three regions, it controls the N1 Province.

This is recorded immediately.

However, the additional Provincial Dividend does not arrive until the beginning of Act III.

# K.8 Turn 8 — Develop

The Company has four PCs.

Develop provides:

1 + 4 = 5 Minor Project opportunities.

The players choose:

| Project                      | Cost in Opportunities |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Expand Detachment for PC 1   | 2                     |
| Talent Retraining for PC 2   | 2                     |
| Upgrade a formation for PC 3 | 1                     |
| Total                        | 5                     |

The Expand Detachment project costs 2 Gold.

Talent Retraining costs 2 Gold.

The formation upgrade uses its own normal Gold and point cost.

Develop (3) **does not receive Supremacy** on Turn 8, because the Company already played Construct (4) during the same Campaign Year. No 2-Gold Develop discount applies to this transaction; the Company pays the normal eligible costs.

The Company cannot buy a second Flex slot through the same Order because Expand Detachment may be selected only once.

It cannot retrain a second Talent because Talent Retraining may be selected only once.

PC 4 does not need a separate dedicated project. This is a shared Company allocation.

# K.9 Act II Pivotal Objective

The GM establishes an Iron Regency operation directed against a frontier crossing.

The PCs learn that a Legion Captain is preparing to occupy the area and threaten Stonebridge's military access.

They could:

- Mobilize to intercept the Captain.
- Secure a defensive agreement through Politics.
- Explore an alternate route or source of information.
- Sabotage a military supply point.
- Allow the attack to proceed and accept the consequences.

Suppose the Company negotiates a settlement that successfully averts the operation and changes the Legion's intended deployment.

The Pivotal Objective has been meaningfully resolved.

Every PC receives 5 Pivotal Objective XP.

This reward is separate from ordinary Campaign Turn XP.

No additional political marker is awarded unless the Company also qualified for the current Stake.

# K.10 Entering Act III

Act III begins at Turn 10, during Summer.

At this milestone:

- Every PC receives 5 Act Advancement XP.
- Concord receives its Provincial Dividend.
- The GM selects and publicly reveals the Principal Opposition's first Doctrine and its selected Commands **before the PCs choose their Doctrines**.
- Each PC then receives the appropriate first Doctrine progression.
- The Act III Pivotal Objective is established.
- A new Act Agenda and Task pool are prepared.
- NPC Maneuver decks refresh.

Concord controls its Capital and Stonebridge Corridor.

The four-PC Company's dividend is:

2 Provinces × 4 PCs = 8 Gold.

Turn 10 is Summer, not Spring.

If the Company played Recruit during Turn 9, Recruit remains exhausted for Turn 10.

The Act transition does not refresh it.

# K.11 Turn 12 — Competing Over Military Access

During Act III, the PCs learn that the Legions rely on a Conclave manufacturing agreement for one specialist Elite Support formation.

The PCs pursue a Politics operation to secure replacement manufacturing rights.

Suppose they complete the necessary negotiation.

The agreement establishes a future right for Concord and terminates the Legions' outside recruitment access when the existing contract expires.

This has a persistent military consequence.

The Legion retains its ordinary home roster and normal scenario point allowances.

But the specified Conclave Support formation will no longer be available without another legal source.

If the Company also satisfies the Active Stake, it receives the corresponding political recognition and Development draw.

The agreement and the political reward remain separate results.

# K.12 Act III Military Confrontation

The Principal Opposition prepares a major military operation involving a named specialist formation and a strategic supply facility.

The PCs obtain information about the operation during Act III.

Suppose they undertake a successful military mission to disable the facility.

The facility's status changes to Disabled.

The named formation loses access to the support it requires.

The Company also resolves the Act III Pivotal Objective.

Each PC gains 5 Pivotal Objective XP, plus the ordinary Turn XP.

The resulting military restriction carries into Act IV unless the Legions find a legitimate way to restore that support.

# K.13 Entering Act IV

Act IV begins at Turn 17, which is Spring.

At the beginning of the Act:

- Every PC receives 5 Act Advancement XP.
- The Company receives its Provincial Dividend.
- PCs gain their next Doctrine progression.
- The Principal Opposition selects its **second, distinct Doctrine**, informed by the PCs' Act III development.
- The new Agenda and Task pool are prepared.
- NPC Maneuver cards refresh.
- All Company Orders also refresh because the new Turn begins in Spring.

Assume Concord still controls two Provinces.

The Company receives another 8 Gold.

Across the eligible Act milestones, the Company has earned:

- Beginning Act II: 4 Gold.
- Beginning Act III: 8 Gold.
- Beginning Act IV: 8 Gold.

Total Provincial Dividends: 20 Gold.

This is a meaningful shared military reserve without requiring individual PC treasuries.

# K.14 Rebuilding Military Losses

After a significant Act IV encounter, consider a purchased **100-Point** formation. Its full recruitment cost is `ceil(100 / 25) = 4 Gold`.

**If it survives** but has missing Models or unrecovered Damage, it may continue to deploy at reduced strength. A single flat **2-Gold replenishment payment** at eligible Upkeep returns it to its full purchased profile; the cost does not depend on whether it lost one Model or several. Ordinary surviving-Model Wounds recover as specified by the campaign rules.

**If it was completely Defeated**, roll a casualty D6:

| D6 | Result | Recovery for a 100-Point Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Destroyed | Remove from roster; must legally replace for 4 Gold unless a specific exception applies |
| 2–4 | Mauled | Unavailable until eligible paid recovery for 2 Gold |
| 5–6 | Scattered | Unavailable; return at full strength after one completed Campaign Turn or eligible early return for 1 Gold |

Two Mauled 100-Point formations require 4 Gold to restore; four surviving damaged 100-Point formations would require 8 Gold to replenish fully, each as a separate flat payment.

The Company's 8-Gold Act IV Provincial Dividend could therefore cover four such 2-Gold transactions or be allocated to other military needs. The optional Reconstitution rule, if selected for the campaign, does not bypass recruitment sources or legal deployment restrictions.

# K.15 Final Army Example

In a Standard Campaign, four PCs each have an Act IV ceiling of **500 Points**. Suppose each PC has earned 150–160 XP, but two retain some unspent XP:

| PC | Earned XP | XP spent on Talents | Equipment Points | Hero Points | Maximum other deployment Points |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| PC 1 | 155 | 155 | 20 | 175 | 325 |
| PC 2 | 155 | 150 | 35 | 185 | 315 |
| PC 3 | 160 | 160 | 15 | 175 | 325 |
| PC 4 | 150 | 145 | 25 | 170 | 330 |
| **Total** | — | — | — | **705** | **1,295** |

A possible detachment selection is:

| PC | Hero | Formations and Assets | Total |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| 1 | 175 | 120 + 100 + 100 | **495** |
| 2 | 185 | 120 + 100 + 75 | **480** |
| 3 | 175 | 125 + 100 + 100 | **500** |
| 4 | 170 | 125 + 100 + 100 | **495** |
| **Company** | **705** | **1,265** | **1,970** |

This leaves **30 Points** unused out of the combined 2,000-Point allowance. These formation values are illustrative; the selected Units must actually exist at those purchased values and satisfy each PC's Patron/Doctrine/Flex slot eligibility, military access, and any applicable Asset or Hero limits. The Company may own additional undeployed formations without increasing its deployment ceiling.

Double Formations occupy **one** compatible slot each while costing their entire doubled Points and associated Gold.

# K.16 Final Opposition Battle

The public Principal Opposition in this example is the **Legions Grand Faction**. The GM may have concealed its actual **home military faction** during Act I but revealed it by the end of Act II. At Act III, the GM selected its first Doctrine **before** the PCs selected theirs; at Act IV, it selected a second Doctrine to address the Company's evolving strengths.

The Standard Campaign Opposition has **15 recruitment slots in Act IV** (8 in Act II, 12 in Act III). It builds **one shared army**, not four 500-Point detachments. For a comparable four-PC pitched battle it ordinarily deploys near **2,000 Points** and has **7 shared CP per Round**, while the four PCs receive 2 CP each. None of those slots provides free formations.

During Act IV, the Legions attempt to field their final army.

Their available options include:

- Their home faction roster.
- Their first and second Doctrines.
- Surviving named military Assets.
- Any valid allied or contracted formations.
- Appropriate military facilities still operating.

Previously disrupted support sources cannot simply be ignored.

However, the Legions may use other legal home-faction options within the normal scenario allowance.

The PCs' earlier political and military decisions therefore affect enemy composition without arbitrarily reducing its entire army's points.

# K.17 Final Political Result

At the end of Turn 25, count the colored Act IV markers.

The winning faction receives the selected Act Agenda's final consequence.

Gray Neutral markers do not count toward political victory.

A tie for the highest colored-marker total produces a Contested Act and the corresponding consolation results.

The final campaign state includes political ownership, surviving military Assets, agreements, important casualties, and the actual resolution of the central conflict.

# K.18 Example Summary

The revised campaign demonstrates several principles:

1. Acts and Campaign Years use separate calendars.
2. XP advances Heroes independently of military Gold.
3. Hero point value uses XP spent on Talents above the 100-Point minimum, not total XP earned.
4. Commerce provides reliable income without depending on Province count.
5. Develop uses a shared project pool, not individual PC allocations.
6. Provincial Control is determined by two of three controlled hexes.
7. Provincial Dividends scale with party size.
8. Pivotal Objectives reward meaningful involvement in the campaign's central conflict.
9. Military agreements affect available unit composition.
10. The Owned Roster may be larger than the legal Deployed Army.

End of Appendix K — revised for Campaign Mode v4.3.
