# Campaign Mode

*Core Campaign Rules — Revised Draft v4.4 (pre-appendix integration)*

A strategic campaign framework for the Hybrid RPG/Wargame.

# 1. Overview

Campaign Mode connects roleplaying, tactical combat, army-building, and strategic play into a persistent campaign. Players control a group of adventurers who gradually establish a military company, develop a warband, acquire territory, forge alliances, and influence a larger political struggle. The Game Master (GM) controls the world and the Principal Opposition, while independent Grand Factions pursue their own strategic interests.

Campaign play operates across three connected levels:

- **Narrative Play:** Exploration, investigation, negotiation, character advancement, and roleplaying encounters.
- **Structured Combat:** Tactical battles involving PCs, formations, commanders, military Assets, and battlefield objectives.
- **Strategic Play:** Company Orders, Faction Maneuvers, territorial control, political Stakes, alliances, and army development.

All three levels contribute to a persistent campaign state.

## 1.1 Terminology

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Campaign Turn | One strategic Turn, representing one Season |
| Campaign Year | Four Seasons: Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter |
| Act | One of four stages of the campaign |
| Company | The PCs and their collectively maintained military organization |
| Grand Faction | A major political power participating in the campaign |
| Principal Opposition | The primary adversarial Grand Faction, strategically controlled by the GM |
| Independent Power | A participating Grand Faction other than the PCs' affiliate or Principal Opposition |
| Local Authority | A settlement, guild, clan, or other regional government |
| Company Order | One of seven strategic actions selected collectively by the PCs |
| Faction Maneuver | A strategic card played by a non-PC Grand Faction |
| Stake | The political objective contested during a Campaign Turn |
| Act Marker | A colored or gray marker recording the result of a Stake |
| Political Influence | A faction's established political presence in a region |
| Province | One Tri-Hex tile containing three individually controlled hexes |
| Owned Roster | All military formations and Assets available to the Company |
| Deployed Army | The subset of the roster selected for a particular battle |
| Pivotal Objective | A major Act II or III conflict that can provide additional XP |
| Opposition Operation | A declared military objective pursued by the Principal Opposition |

**Core Principle:** Every Campaign Turn permits meaningful strategic activity, but awards exactly one political Stake marker. Winning a Stake does not automatically provide territory, military formations, infrastructure, or the material outcome described by a Task. Such consequences must be earned separately through the applicable actions or missions.

# 2. Campaign Structure

A campaign consists of four Acts. The Acts represent changes in the PCs' political standing, military resources, and relationship with the Principal Opposition.

## 2.1 The Four Acts

**Act I — The Gathering Storm**

The PCs begin as individual adventurers. They investigate regional problems, establish contacts, encounter competing political interests, and become involved in the developing conflict. The PCs primarily operate through their Hero profiles and narrative adventures. They may obtain equipment, discoveries, information, Gold, and political recognition, but do not yet possess their full Company organization.

**Act II — Founding the Company**

The PCs formally establish their military company. They receive their Grand Faction Charter and first Patron formations, gaining access to military development and larger structured battles. Territorial development, trade, and political agreements become increasingly important. The Principal Opposition begins pursuing recognizable regional military objectives.

**Act III — Rising Powers**

The company gains its first Doctrine command and additional detachment flexibility. The Principal Opposition activates its first Doctrine. The campaign increasingly involves large military formations, infrastructure, contested territory, and agreements that affect army composition. Important Opposition military preparations should become increasingly apparent to the PCs.

**Act IV — The Final Reckoning**

The PCs and Principal Opposition receive further Doctrine capabilities. The company prepares for decisive military and political conflict. Territorial control, alliances, surviving military Assets, equipment, and accumulated agreements determine which strategic options remain available. The campaign concludes through a decisive conflict, political settlement, or another appropriate final outcome.

## 2.2 Campaign Lengths

Acts need not contain equal numbers of Turns. Longer campaigns provide additional opportunities for character advancement, territorial expansion, recruitment, and military specialization.

| Campaign | Act I | Act II | Act III | Act IV | Total Turns |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Local | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 18 |
| Standard | 4 | 5 | 7 | 9 | 25 |
| Long | 5 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 29 |
| Grand | 6 | 7 | 9 | 11 | 33 |

| Campaign | Tri-Hex Tiles | Participating Grand Factions | Starting Sites |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Local | 5 | 3 | 5 |
| Standard | 7 | 4 | 7 |
| Long | 9 | 4 | 11 |
| Grand | 11 | 5 | 13 |

One participating Grand Faction is affiliated with the PCs. One is the Principal Opposition. The remaining participating Grand Factions are independent powers. Nonparticipating factions may still appear as narrative organizations, commercial partners, or recruitment sources, but do not receive ordinary Faction Maneuver decks or colored Act markers.

## 2.3 Seasons and Campaign Years

Every Campaign Turn represents one Season. The seasonal sequence is **Spring → Summer → Autumn → Winter → Spring**. A Campaign Year contains four Turns. Acts and Years are independent structures. An Act may begin or end in the middle of a Year.

At the beginning of Spring:

- All seven Company Orders refresh.
- The annual Supremacy sequence resets.

At the beginning of a new Act:

- NPC Faction Maneuver decks refresh.
- A new Agenda and Task pool are prepared.
- Relevant Act progression and milestone rewards occur.

An Act transition does not automatically refresh Company Orders. A gaming session does not automatically advance the Campaign Turn; a major mission may occupy multiple sessions while representing only one strategic Turn.

# 3. Campaign Setup

## 3.1 Establish Participating Grand Factions

Select the Grand Factions required by the chosen format.

Designate:

- The Grand Faction affiliated with the PCs.
- The Principal Opposition.
- The remaining independent Grand Factions.

Each power begins with its corresponding Capital tile and associated political interests. The PCs' affiliated Grand Faction is their political sponsor, not an unlimited source of military units or resources. Specific military and economic rights arise from the Charter and subsequent agreements.

## 3.2 Construct the Strategic Map

The campaign map uses connected Tri-Hex tiles. Each tile contains three individually addressable hex regions.

| Campaign | Capitals | Developed Frontier | Wild Frontier |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Local | 3 | 1 | 1 |
| Standard | 4 | 2 | 1 |
| Long | 4 | 2 | 3 |
| Grand | 5 | 3 | 3 |

Every participating Grand Faction controls its Capital tile at setup. Frontier tiles contain Local Authorities, unclaimed territory, regional resources, and exploration Sites. Arrange the map so that every participating power can reasonably interact with the frontier and with competing factions. The exact tile layouts, resource distributions, and geographic restrictions appear in Appendix O.

## 3.3 Starting Positions

Place:

- Political Control markers on Capital hexes.
- Local Authority markers on applicable Frontier hexes.
- Starting Roads and other printed infrastructure.
- Unresolved exploration Site markers.
- One strategic Force marker for every participating non-PC Grand Faction.
- One PC Party marker.

The PCs acquire their Company Force marker upon establishing their Company in Act II. A Force marker represents strategic military presence, not a specific deployed army list.

## 3.4 Prepare Campaign Components

Prepare:

- Seven Company Order cards.
- Twelve Maneuver cards per participating non-PC Grand Faction.
- Act Agenda cards and eligible Political Task cards.
- Development and Discovery rewards, plus Neutral Opportunities.
- Five Grand Faction Charters.
- The Act Track and Council Influence token.
- Control, Influence, Province, Force, Site, Improvement, and resource markers.
- The campaign record sheets.

The detailed component inventory and setup checklist appear in Appendix J.

# 4. Characters, XP, and Army Progression

## 4.1 Starting Characters

PCs begin using the normal RPG character creation rules. They retain their Characteristics, Skills, Talents, class abilities, spells, equipment, and other applicable character features. Campaign Mode does not provide additional starting Talents or automatically advance a character merely because the campaign has entered a later Act. Character XP and military Gold are separate progression systems.

## 4.2 Hero Point Valuation

For deployment and army-building purposes, a PC Hero has a minimum base value of **100 Points**. Above that floor, its base value equals **XP actually spent on Talents**; earned but unspent XP does not add to battlefield Points.

**Base Hero Points = max(100, XP spent on Talents)**

**Final Hero Points = Base Hero Points + Equipped Equipment Points + Other Explicit Point Costs**

Ordinary Talent effects are included in the XP-spent valuation and are not added again. Purchases such as explicitly point-valued Animal Companions, equipment, and other exceptional additions are added separately according to their rules. The starting floor is replaced, not added to the value of Talents, once spending exceeds 100 XP.

| XP earned | XP spent on Talents | Equipment | Final Hero Points |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 75 | 45 | 20 | 120 |
| 115 | 110 | 20 | 130 |
| 180 | 155 | 20 | 175 |

Campaign XP is awarded per completed Turn and through additional specified sources (Section 4.3). Spending XP does not require a Develop Order. A PC must finalize Talent spending and equipment before recording the Hero's Points for a battle; unspent XP remains on the Character Sheet and is not separately valued. This section follows the RPG Experience and Advancement rules.

## 4.3 Campaign Experience

PCs receive XP from the following defined sources.

| Source | XP per PC | Frequency |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Completed Campaign Turn | 5 | Every Turn |
| Unique resolved exploration Site | 5 | Once per Site |
| Act Advancement | 5 | Beginning Acts II, III, IV |
| Pivotal Objective | 5 | One opportunity in each of Acts II and III |

XP awards apply equally to all participating PCs in the Company. The base Campaign Turn reward is earned even if the Company Order or mission fails. A mission spanning multiple gaming sessions still provides one ordinary Campaign Turn XP award.

**Exploration XP**

Successfully completing an unexplored Site awards 5 XP per PC in addition to its normal Discovery or fixed reward. A Site awards this XP only once. Scouting, revealing, revisiting, or drawing two Discovery options through Supremacy does not award additional Site XP. A Site may be completed through Explore or another legal mission.

**Act Advancement XP**

At the beginnings of Acts II, III, and IV, award 5 XP per PC. This does not depend on the previous Act's political winner.

**Pivotal Objective XP**

At the beginnings of Acts II and III, the GM establishes one Pivotal Objective involving the central conflict or Principal Opposition. Meaningfully resolving that objective awards 5 XP per PC. The outcome may arise through combat, negotiation, investigation, sabotage, evacuation, defense, or another consequential operation. The PCs need not achieve a military victory, but must meaningfully participate in the objective's resolution.

An objective ignored and resolved entirely without PC involvement does not award XP. A routine political Stake or minor map action is not automatically a Pivotal Objective. Different legitimate XP awards may be earned during the same Turn.

## 4.4 Expected XP Progression

| Campaign | Base Turn XP | Act Milestone XP | Potential Pivotal XP | Typical Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Local | 90 | 15 | 10 | 125–130 |
| Standard | 125 | 15 | 10 | 155–160 |
| Long | 145 | 15 | 10 | 185–190 |
| Grand | 165 | 15 | 10 | 210–215 |

Typical totals assume different amounts of exploration for each format. They are planning estimates, not guaranteed awards or hard XP limits. Standard Campaigns are intended to provide approximately 150–160 XP through ordinary participation and limited exploration. Long and Grand Campaigns provide more opportunities to advance characters and then focus increasingly on military development, specialized formations, and the composition of individual vanguards.

Detailed Talent capacity and progression limits remain governed by the character advancement rules.

## 4.5 Deployment Ceilings

Each PC has an individual deployment allowance, covering their Hero, formations, assigned deployable Assets, and explicit Point additions.

| Campaign | Act II | Act III | Act IV |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Local | 200 | 275 | 375 |
| Standard | 250 | 375 | 500 |
| Long | 325 | 475 | 625 |
| Grand | 375 | 575 | 750 |

All figures are Points **per PC**. For four PCs, the final Company ceilings are Local 1,500, Standard 2,000, Long 2,500, and Grand 3,000 Points. These are deployment limits, not ownership limits. A PC cannot transfer unused personal deployment points to another PC unless a scenario explicitly permits it.

## 4.6 Owned Roster and Deployed Army

The Owned Roster contains every permanently owned and currently accessible formation or military Asset belonging to the Company. The Deployed Army contains only those Heroes, formations, and Assets selected for a specific battle. The Company may own more points than it can deploy. Undeployed units may remain in reserve. Every deployed formation must satisfy its applicable points, detachment slots, recruitment rights, tags, and other restrictions.

## 4.7 Founding the Company and Patron Slots

At the beginning of Act II, the Company receives its **Grand Faction Charter**. The Charter grants one shared passive benefit and one shared Charter Command; these benefits are not duplicated for each PC.

Each PC gains **two Patron recruitment slots** according to the Patron arrangement and its legal choices. The Company collectively receives **two free eligible Patron formations at their normal starting sizes**, assigned among PCs by agreement. Further Patron formations may be legally recruited and developed through the normal Gold and source requirements.

Patron slots and rights are governed by the Campaign Patron arrangements and the current recruitment eligibility rules, not by matched play's 15-slot template. A Patron relationship does not provide the full faction roster beyond explicitly granted rights.

## 4.8 PC Doctrines and Detachment Slots

Each PC begins Act I with **one reserved H slot for their own PC Character**. This starting H slot may not be used for another Captain or Hero. PCs do not use the matched-play Universal H, C, C foundation.

| Stage | Additional recruitment slots and Command access | Total ordinary slots (per PC) |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Act I | One PC-only H slot | 1 |
| Beginning Act II | Two legal Patron slots | 3 |
| Beginning Act III | Select one eligible Doctrine, gain its Foundation slot and first chosen Command's recruitment slot | 5 |
| Beginning Act IV | Gain a second selected Command and its recruitment slot | 6 |
| Grand Campaign special progression | Gain a third selected Doctrine Command and recruitment slot according to the campaign's Grand progression | 7 |

Each selected Doctrine Command must be an eligible option from that Doctrine, and its recruitment slot is granted as printed. A PC has only **one Doctrine** in the ordinary campaign progression; matched play's requirement to select two Doctrines does not apply. Newly unlocked Commands are known options, not automatic additional CP.

**Flex slots:** Each PC may purchase at most two additional Flex slots through eligible Develop projects, normally 2 Gold apiece. Their categories are chosen when purchased and fixed thereafter. Flex slots remain subject to normal Point, roster-source, classification, and army limits. Accordingly, a Standard-format PC may normally reach eight slots with both Flex slots, and a Grand-format PC may reach nine.

**Double Formations:** An eligible faction-specific Core formation can be doubled using the normal Double Formation rule. Pay the additional formation Points and corresponding campaign recruitment or upgrade Gold; it still occupies **one compatible detachment slot**, controls objectives as one Unit, receives one activation per eligible phase, and keeps Unit-level keyword ranks. A free Patron formation is granted at its normal size; any later doubling requires payment.

**Source limits:** Recruitment slots permit a category, but a PC must also possess the relevant Patron, Charter, contract, alliance, discovery, or other legal recruitment right. An Elite faction Support may occupy an eligible S or E slot as defined by its profile; a generic Elite Wizard normally uses E. A slot does not grant Units, free equipment, or separate CP.

## 4.9 Character Loss and Replacement

### 4.9.1 Permanent Character Loss

Player Characters follow the normal General Adventuring rules for Defeat and Critical Injuries. Each PC may survive their first two Critical Injuries. Upon suffering their third Critical Injury, the Character permanently dies or retires, and their Character Sheet is retired. A permanently lost PC is no longer eligible for active play. The player may introduce a replacement Character.

Character replacement does not require a Company Order, Gold payment, or additional campaign resource.

### 4.9.2 Replacement Character XP

A replacement Character begins with total earned XP equal to the Company's current shared XP progression. Ordinarily, this is the total XP already earned by the surviving PCs through Campaign Turns, exploration, Act milestones, and Pivotal Objectives. The replacement may spend this XP on legal character advancements using the normal rules.

The new Character:

Begins with no Critical Injuries. Uses the normal character creation rules. May select a different legal Class and Subclass. Must satisfy the normal Talent and advancement prerequisites. Begins with an appropriate level of equipment for the current campaign. Introducing the Character does not award the Company additional XP or another campaign reward.

### 4.9.3 Replacement Hero Point Value

A replacement uses the normal Hero formula: Base Hero Points = max(100, XP spent on Talents); Final Hero Points = Base Hero Points + Equipment Points + other explicitly additional costs. The replacement receives the Company's earned-XP catch-up allowance, may legally spend XP before deployment, and is valued according to what is actually spent. For example, a replacement with 155 earned XP who spends 125 XP on Talents and equips 20 Points of items is a 145-point Hero. Their 30 unspent XP does not affect deployment Points.

### 4.9.4 Equipment and Company Property

Equipment carried by the previous Character remains subject to the circumstances of their death or departure. It may be recovered, lost, inherited, or transferred through appropriate narrative circumstances. The replacement receives an appropriate starting equipment allocation, normally comparable to the functional equipment available to other PCs at that stage of the campaign. This does not duplicate the previous Character's unique equipment or automatically recover items lost during the encounter.

The Company retains its shared treasury, territory, institutions, military agreements, and Owned Roster.

Replacing a Character does not grant:

Additional Company Gold. New free Patron formations. Additional Company Orders. Another Charter. Free military Assets. Additional detachment capacity.

### 4.9.5 Company and Doctrine Progression

A replacement entering during Act II or later receives the normal Company benefits available to an active PC at that campaign stage.

These may include:

Eligible Patron recruitment slots. The current Act's deployment allowance. Appropriate Doctrine selection and commands. Existing Company Charter benefits. Access to legal Company formations and military agreements. The replacement does not cause previously awarded Company-wide benefits to be awarded again. Existing formations may be reassigned to the replacement's detachment when legally eligible.

The replacement must still satisfy all slot categories, recruitment rights, and deployment points.

### 4.9.6 Timing

A replacement normally joins between encounters, after the current mission, or at another narratively appropriate opportunity. A player does not gain an additional active PC or another battlefield activation merely because their previous Character was Defeated. If the third Critical Injury occurs during an ongoing battle, the replacement does not automatically deploy into that battle.

The new Character participates in subsequent XP awards once introduced. The Company continues operating normally with the surviving PCs until the replacement joins.

### 4.9.7 Previous Campaign Characters

Characters from previous campaigns may return as narrative NPCs or be represented as Legacy Captains under Appendix P. Their previous XP and equipment do not automatically carry into the current campaign's military roster. Returning characters remain subject to the current campaign's legal recruitment sources, deployment points, and detachment slots. End of Section 4.9 — Character Loss and Replacement.

# 5. Strategic Map and Territorial Control

## 5.1 Hexes and Provinces

Each Tri-Hex tile contains:

H — Primary Hex. A — Secondary Hex. B — Secondary Hex. Each hex has its own political controller, resources, Improvements, and possible military presence.

## 5.2 Political Control

A hex may belong to one Grand Faction, a Local Authority, or remain unclaimed. Control changes through qualifying construction, treaties, territorial missions, military occupation, or other explicit effects. Military presence alone does not transfer political ownership.

## 5.3 Provincial Control

A Grand Faction controls a Province when it politically controls at least two of that tile's three hexes. Place a Province marker for that faction. The remaining hex retains its existing controller. Provincial Control does not automatically grant ownership of that third hex or its resources. Recalculate Province ownership whenever individual hex control changes.

## 5.4 Provincial Dividend

At the beginnings of Acts II, III, and IV, the PC Company receives:

1 Gold per participating PC for every Province controlled by its affiliated Grand Faction. The Gold enters the shared Company treasury. There is no Provincial Dividend cap. The affiliated Capital normally counts as one Province. Provincial Dividends are awarded once per eligible Act milestone, not every Turn. A Province obtained after that milestone does not produce an immediate additional payment.

For NPC Grand Factions, Provincial Control remains abstract. At each eligible milestone, an NPC with at least one Province may restore or reinforce one eligible existing military Asset or formation under its normal restrictions. NPC factions do not receive an unrestricted Gold treasury from Provincial Dividends.

## 5.5 Regional Resources

The map uses three regional resources:

Stone. Supply. Influence. These represent accessible infrastructure and logistical capabilities, not consumable counters. Resource access depends on political control, valid agreements, and legal connections. Accessible ratings range from 0 to 3. Resources are checked when used as prerequisites and are not spent like Gold.

## 5.6 Political Influence

Each participating Grand Faction may maintain up to two Political Influence markers. These represent recognized representatives, contacts, or institutional relationships. Political Influence does not provide political ownership, armies, or resource access by itself. Political Influence is distinct from the printed Influence resource and the Council Influence token.

## 5.7 Local Influence

When contesting a political Stake, a faction receives +1 Strength if it controls the target hex or an adjacent hex, or possesses a Political Influence marker at the target or an adjacent hex. The bonus is nonstacking. For a Stake involving an edge, either endpoint may qualify. The bonus is determined when the Stake and target are declared, before that Turn's Maneuver effects resolve.

Province ownership does not grant an additional bonus beyond qualifying local control.

# 6. Act Agendas and Political Tasks

## 6.1 Act Agendas

At the beginning of each Act, reveal one appropriate Agenda. The Agenda identifies the principal political issue of that stage of the campaign and the consequence of winning it. The twelve Agenda cards are described in Appendix C. An Agenda does not replace seasonal political Stakes.

## 6.2 Political Task Pool

Prepare a number of Political Tasks equal to the number of Turns in the Act. Use the following classification distribution:

| Turns in Act | Open | Sponsored | Exclusive |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 4 | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| 5 | 2 | 2 | 1 |
| 6 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| 7 | 3 | 2 | 2 |
| 8 | 3 | 3 | 2 |
| 9 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| 10 | 4 | 3 | 3 |
| 11 | 3 | 4 | 4 |

At the beginning of the Act, reveal up to three eligible Tasks. These form the public Task Display. Each Turn, one displayed Task becomes the Active Stake. Remove the resolved Task and refill the display from the remaining pool.

## 6.3 Task Categories

Open: Any eligible participating political power may claim the Task. Sponsored: A claimant requires suitable backing or an established sponsoring relationship. Exclusive: The designated eligible beneficiary receives the political reward; other factions may support or obstruct it without automatically stealing its recognition. The full Task catalogs appear in Appendices D and E.

## 6.4 Flexible Targets

A Task identifies an objective and eligibility requirements rather than a permanently fixed map location. Its target may be an institution, settlement, region, route, commander, military force, Site, or agreement. When selected, identify one primary target for eligibility and Local Influence. The selected Stake and primary target are locked before the PCs commit their Company Order.

Tasks without a legal target are replaced.

## 6.5 Political Recognition Versus Material Consequences

Winning a Task's political Stake provides recognition and Act Track progress. The Task's described material consequence requires an appropriate completed mission, agreement, construction, or other valid action. For example, winning recognition for a proposed bridge does not automatically construct that bridge. A Warfare Stake victory does not automatically destroy the opposing army.

A political victory may occur without the Task's material objective being completed, or the company may complete a material objective without receiving the corresponding political marker.

# 7. Council Influence and Political Initiative

## 7.1 Council Influence

One Council Influence token alternates between the PCs and GM.

Its holder:

Selects the Active Stake. Chooses a legal target and suit. Designates the Lead commitment. May direct one eligible independent Grand Faction Maneuver. The token does not provide an additional Faction Maneuver or map action. It passes after every completed Campaign Turn and does not reset at Act boundaries.

## 7.2 Principal Opposition

The GM always controls the Principal Opposition's Maneuver commitment. The Opposition commits one card per Turn like every other non-PC Grand Faction. It does not receive an additional card because the GM holds Council Influence.

## 7.3 Directing Independent Powers

The Council Influence holder may direct one participating independent Grand Faction. The commitment must respect that faction's obligations, political interests, and existing agreements. The independent faction retains ownership of its troops, territory, Influence markers, political markers, and Development rewards. Directing a faction does not transfer its political victory to the director.

## 7.4 Political Mandates

A successful Politics operation may establish a Political Mandate from an independent power. A Mandate permits the PCs to direct that power's next eligible Maneuver, including during a GM-held Council Turn. Only one unused Mandate may be held. Mandates expire at the end of the Act if unused. A Mandate does not create another card play or permit actions violating the agreement.

# 8. Company Orders

## 8.1 General Rules

The Company possesses seven numbered Company Order cards. The players collectively select one available Order per Campaign Turn. During Act I, these represent the adventuring party's available strategic opportunities: an Order never grants formations, military access, detachment slots, a Company Force, or infrastructure rights before those prerequisites have actually been unlocked.

Each Order may be played once per Campaign Year and refreshes at the beginning of Spring. An Order determines the Company's principal strategic undertaking for that Turn. It may lead to narrative play, structured combat, or ordinary strategic resolution.

## 8.2 Supremacy

An Order gains Supremacy if its number is higher than every Order previously played during the current Campaign Year. The first Order played each Year always qualifies. Supremacy provides the bonus printed on the card. Discounts apply once to combined eligible Company expenditures, not separately for every PC. Discounts cannot reduce a positive eligible expenditure below 1 Gold.

## 8.3 Order Reference

| # | Order | Normal Action | Supremacy |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Explore | One expedition, Site investigation, or reconnaissance operation | Reveal two eligible Discoveries and choose one |
| 2 | Commerce | Earn 1 Gold per PC or secure one contract | +1 Gold income or −1 Gold contract cost |
| 3 | Develop | Receive 1 + PCs Minor Project opportunities | −2 Gold to combined Develop expenditures |
| 4 | Construct | One Major or up to two Minor Improvements | −1 Gold construction cost |
| 5 | Recruit | One eligible formation acquisition or replenishment per PC | −2 Gold recruitment expenditure |
| 6 | Politics | One significant political objective or agreement | One additional related minor concession |
| 7 | Mobilize | Move Company Force and undertake one military operation | One Battlefield Edge |

## 8.4 Explore

The Company undertakes one eligible expedition, investigates an unresolved Site, or pursues reconnaissance. A successfully resolved unique Site provides its Discovery or fixed reward and 5 XP per PC. Explore Supremacy permits revealing two eligible random Discoveries and selecting one. If no eligible Site is available, Explore may instead pursue intelligence, scouting, or another valid exploratory objective.

Reconnaissance alone does not automatically award a Discovery or exploration XP.

## 8.5 Commerce

Choose one of two modes.

**Earn Income**

Gain 1 Gold per PC into the shared Company treasury. The Company must have access to a legal market, settlement, or commercial institution; its affiliated Capital normally qualifies. The baseline income does not require a separate Skill Check. Commerce Supremacy grants 1 additional Gold.

**Secure Contract**

Pursue one significant commercial agreement, including:

One eligible mercenary acquisition. A trade or logistical agreement. Resource access. Specialist services. A commercial recruitment right. A contracted formation requires its legal source, applicable Gold cost, and deployment eligibility. Commerce Supremacy reduces one eligible contract expense by 1 Gold. The Contract option does not also grant the standard Commerce income.

## 8.6 Develop

The Company receives 1 + the number of participating PCs Minor Project opportunities. Each Minor Project consumes one opportunity. The Company may exchange two Minor Project opportunities for one Major Project. Minor Projects may be repeated. Each Major Project category may be selected only once per Develop Order.

**Minor Projects**

Craft one eligible piece of equipment. Improve or modify existing equipment. Purchase one ordinary printed formation upgrade. Refit an owned formation's legal equipment or loadout. Complete another eligible standard training or specialization project. The Gold or equipment-point value of an item does not by itself make its crafting project Major. Projects retain their normal costs, checks, prerequisites, and upgrade limits.

**Major Projects**

Project Benefit Expand Detachment Purchase one Flex slot for an eligible PC Talent Retraining Replace one PC Talent with another of the same Tier Asset Research Research one specific Asset or advanced modification Asset Development Complete one substantial approved Asset improvement

Expand Detachment: Costs 2 Gold. Each PC may purchase at most two Flex slots over the campaign. The slot category is selected when purchased. Flex slots count toward the PC's maximum slot allowance and do not grant units. Talent Retraining: Costs 2 Gold. Replace one existing Talent with another of the same Tier, provided the entire Talent structure remains legal. Ranked Talent requirements, class restrictions, and other prerequisites must still be met. No XP refund is granted; any positive XP difference must be paid. The change becomes effective after the current Turn.

Only one Talent Retraining project may be performed per Develop Order. Asset Research: Normally costs 2 Gold plus any required materials or access. Research requires an appropriate source, such as a blueprint, Workshop, industrial contact, or recovered military Asset. A suitable research check may be required. Successful research unlocks the specified option but does not automatically provide a free Asset.

Asset Development: Improves or reconstructs an owned Asset using its approved rules, relevant resources, and normal Gold and point costs. Routine printed formation upgrades remain Minor Projects. Asset Research and Asset Development are separate Major categories and cannot duplicate their respective categories within the same Order.

**Develop Supremacy**

Reduce the total eligible Develop expenditure by 2 Gold. Supremacy does not award more project opportunities. Ordinary character progression using earned XP does not require Develop.

## 8.7 Construct

The Company completes one Major or up to two Minor map Improvements. Projects must satisfy Gold, resource, location, and ownership conditions. Construct Supremacy reduces combined eligible construction expenses by 1 Gold. Detailed costs appear in Section 11.

## 8.8 Recruit

Each PC may recruit or replenish one eligible formation. The normal cost is 1 Gold per 25 formation points, rounded up. The formation requires a legitimate military source. Recruit Supremacy reduces combined eligible recruitment expenditure by 2 Gold. Recruit does not automatically grant an additional detachment slot.

## 8.9 Politics

The Company pursues one significant political or institutional objective.

Possible outcomes include:

Establishing an alliance. Securing military recruitment rights. Negotiating passage or regional resources. Obtaining a Political Mandate. Acquiring an allied contingent. Negotiating territorial control. Changing an existing agreement. Obtaining specialist military access. A successful Politics operation produces one principal result. Politics Supremacy grants one related minor concession, not an additional full agreement, independent formation, or Act marker.

## 8.10 Mobilize

Move the Company Force up to two legal hexes and conduct one appropriate military operation. Movement using at least one qualifying Road may increase its maximum distance by one hex. Operations may involve interception, attack, defense, escort, relief, occupation, or disruption of military infrastructure. Mobilize Supremacy grants one Battlefield Edge for an eligible battle. Movement alone does not transfer political control.

## 8.11 Company Travel

The PC Party may travel through an appropriate Company operation. Normal strategic travel permits up to two legal hexes, with an additional hex from an eligible Road. Special routes, sea travel, spells, and other established methods may change the legal route. The Party and Force markers may travel independently when circumstances allow. Travel does not itself create an additional Company Order.

## 9.1 Faction Decks

Every participating non-PC Grand Faction receives twelve Maneuver cards per Act: three Discovery, three Industry, three Authority, and three Warfare.

## 9.3 Leading, Following, and Pivoting

The Active Stake determines the leading suit. A matching-suit card may Follow or Lead and potentially win the Stake when its faction satisfies the Task's eligibility. An off-suit commitment normally Pivots to its legal strategic action and cannot defeat an eligible matching-suit claimant. If no eligible matching-suit commitment exists, an eligible off-suit Lead may win. The Lead is designated as part of the political commitment procedure and matters for tie-breaking.

## 9.4 Resolving the Political Trick

Determine whether a qualifying PC mission successfully determines the Stake. If not, identify eligible NPC claimants. Eligible matching-suit commitments take precedence. Compare printed Strength plus applicable Local Influence. Resolve ties. Award the eligible result or Neutral marker. A PC mission must satisfy actual Task conditions to determine the Stake. Selecting a related Order is insufficient by itself.

## 9.5 Tie-Breaking

When NPC commitments tie:

An eligible incumbent defending an existing relevant position or right wins against non-incumbent claimants. Otherwise, an eligible Lead wins if among the tied claimants. If neither resolves the tie, the result is Neutral. There are no additional ordinary numerical bonuses from being Lead, holding Council Influence, or controlling a wider Province.

## 9.6 Eligibility

Card Strength represents political commitment, not unrestricted practical capability. A Warfare card does not automatically defeat a Fortress. An Authority card does not automatically annex a Local Authority. A Discovery card does not automatically complete an exploration Site. Such consequences require their own legal outcomes.

# 10. NPC Maneuver Map Actions

Each committed NPC Maneuver provides one basic strategic effect, whether or not its faction wins the Stake. Winning never creates a second basic Maneuver action.

## 10.1 Discovery

Scout an eligible Site, investigate a region, or establish useful intelligence. Scouting alone does not complete the Site or award its Discovery reward.

## 10.2 Industry

Complete one eligible free Minor Improvement per Act or advance a legal industrial alternative. Once the faction has used its free Minor Improvement for that Act, later Industry Maneuvers may establish contacts or prepare industrial objectives. Routine Industry Maneuvers cannot construct Frontier Outposts to annex territory.

## 10.3 Authority

Place or relocate one Political Influence marker through an appropriate connection, or establish a minor diplomatic contact. Political Influence is limited to two markers per participating Grand Faction.

## 10.4 Warfare

Move one eligible strategic Force marker one legal hex. Movement may prepare an engagement, reinforce an approach, or establish a threatening military position. Ordinary Warfare movement alone does not grant conquest or a free full battle.

## 10.5 Independent Powers

Independent Grand Factions pursue public primary and secondary interests.

When not directly controlled:

They Follow qualifying threats to existing defensive obligations when legally possible. They otherwise favor Tasks advancing their primary interest. They pursue secondary interests and legal map development as opportunities permit. The GM should not retrospectively change an independent faction's priorities to counter the PCs.

Construction requires Gold, accessible resources, and a legal location. Roads and Bridges occupy edges. Other Improvements normally occupy one principal Improvement slot in a hex. Printed landmarks do not automatically provide purchased Improvement benefits.

## 11.3 Frontier Outposts

A Frontier Outpost claims one eligible unclaimed hex.

The hex must:

Be unclaimed. Contain no Local Authority. Have no incompatible existing Improvement. Border controlled territory or have a qualifying occupied and supplied approach. Upon completion, place the Outpost and the affiliated Grand Faction's political Control marker. The Outpost claims only its own hex. It does not annex adjacent territory or the whole Province. An Outpost may be replaced later by another legal principal Improvement without automatically removing existing political control.

## 11.4 Changing Control

Territory may change hands through a valid treaty, Outpost, completed occupation, mission consequence, or another explicit effect. Neither military presence nor political card Strength alone is sufficient.

**Purchased upgrades during Develop:** An owned formation acquiring a printed equipment, Veterans, or formation upgrade pays the printed additional Points toward its roster value and **1 Gold per 25 added Points, rounded up**, unless a special benefit waives the Gold payment. This is based on the added Points, not the complete Unit cost. Upgrading does not consume Recruit when performed through the legal Develop projects described in Section 8 and Appendix A.

## 12.6 Formation Casualties and Replenishment

Campaign casualties are resolved **after** the battle and recorded in the Owned Roster. Player Characters follow Critical Injury rules instead of formation casualty rolls. Named Unique Assets may have additional persistence restrictions.

**Surviving formations.** A formation that still has at least one surviving Model after the encounter remains available. If it lost Models or has unresolved Damage, it may be replenished to its purchased full profile during eligible Upkeep by paying **half its normal full recruitment Gold cost, rounded up**. This is a **single flat payment per Unit**, irrespective of whether one or many Models were lost; no per-Model casualty accounting in Gold is needed. Record the surviving Model count and, for significant single-model Units, any unresolved Damage until replenished. Until replenished, a surviving formation can deploy at its reduced Model count, subject to normal eligibility; it cannot regain defeated Models without paying or receiving a specific recovery benefit. Between battles surviving Models recover ordinary nonpersistent Wounds; single-model Large Assets with persistent damage use their specific recovery rules, or pay the same half-cost replenishment fee to restore full Wounds.

**Defeated formations.** After a completely defeated non-Hero formation is removed from battle, roll once on this table:

| D6 | Condition | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Destroyed | Remove from Owned Roster; requires legal replacement or an explicit Reconstitution exception |
| 2–4 | Mauled | Remains owned but unavailable until paid recovery is completed |
| 5–6 | Scattered | Unavailable; returns after one completed Campaign Turn, or early for 1 Gold |

Mauled recovery costs **half the Unit's normal full recruitment Gold cost, rounded up**, and requires an appropriate source; it may be completed during eligible Upkeep without another Recruit Order. A recovered Mauled formation returns at its normal purchased strength. A Scattered formation returns at normal purchased strength after its delay; eligible early recovery costs 1 Gold. A Destroyed formation requires normal recruitment, a legitimate source, and its full Gold cost.

**Optional Rule — Reconstitution.** For campaigns with frequent structured battles, each PC may reconstitute one previously Destroyed ordinary formation per Act during eligible Upkeep by paying its full ordinary recruitment Gold cost. The replacement must use the same legal profile and a suitable source, does not consume Recruit or an additional slot, and is still subject to deployment limits. Named unique Assets remain subject to their own recovery rules. Reconstitution stays optional until selected for the campaign.

**Double Formations:** Treat a Double Formation as one purchased formation for a single casualty roll or surviving-Unit replenishment payment. Its normal full recruitment Gold cost is calculated from its actual doubled Points; it does not roll separately for each half.

# 13. Military Alliances and the Principal Opposition

## 13.1 Military Access

The Company and Principal Opposition may obtain military resources through legal sources, including:

Home regular-faction rosters. Charter and Patron arrangements. Mercenary contracts. Political alliances. Local recruitment charters. Industrial agreements. Military facilities. Unique discoveries. Rights are limited to the formations or categories actually authorized. They do not automatically unlock an entire outside faction roster.

## 13.2 Military Agreements

An agreement should state:

The eligible formation or Asset. Whether it is purchased, loaned, or provided freely. The agreement's duration. Geographic or political obligations. What happens if the agreement expires or is broken. Temporary borrowed formations remain owned by their sponsors unless the agreement states otherwise.

## 13.3 Principal Opposition Identity and Army Construction

The **Grand Faction** is the Opposition's political identity; the **home military faction** determines its regular faction roster and military Faction Commands. The GM must select both, but may conceal one for a limited period:

1. **By the end of Act I:** Reveal either the opposing Grand Faction **or** its home military faction. The other remains unknown to the PCs unless legitimately discovered.

2. **By the end of Act II:** Reveal the remaining identity. These selections are fixed at that point.

3. **At the beginning of Act III:** The GM chooses and publicly reveals its **first Doctrine and its eligible selected commands before the PCs choose their Doctrines**. Each PC then selects their own Doctrine according to campaign progression.

4. **At the beginning of Act IV:** The GM selects its **second, distinct Doctrine**, after the PCs' Act III doctrine choices and prior campaigning are known. This permits strategic adaptation to the PCs' army composition; all selections are thereafter fixed.

The Principal Opposition has **one shared army**, not one PC-sized detachment per player. It uses purchased Units, the Grand Faction's political rights, its home military roster, and the following campaign-specific recruitment-slot progression rather than the ordinary 15-slot matched-play template.

**Opposition slot sources:** At Act II it begins with Universal H, C, C (three slots), two home-faction foundation slots, and the two slots associated with selected home Faction Commands: seven base slots. It also receives one **Campaign Expansion slot**, for **eight slots in Act II**. At Acts III and IV it gains one Foundation slot from each newly chosen Doctrine and one recruitment slot per newly selected Doctrine Command. Longer formats grant additional Campaign Expansion slots according to the table below.

| Campaign format | Act II total | Act III total | Act IV final total | Extra Campaign Expansion slots earned (Act II / III / IV) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Local | 8 | 11 | **13** | 1 / 0 / 0 |
| Standard | 8 | 12 | **15** | 1 / 1 / 0 |
| Long | 8 | 13 | **17** | 1 / 1 / 1 |
| Grand | 8 | 13 | **19** | 1 / 1 / 2 |

Campaign Expansion slots are selected when gained from **C, E, R, V, S, or A**, and their category is then fixed. They grant no free Unit or CP. Units must still meet actual source rights, purchase costs, deployment Point ceilings, and any applicable Hero or Large Asset restrictions. The normal H foundation remains sufficient for the required Headquarters; extra Headquarters recruitment requires a specifically eligible slot source or scenario exception.

**Doctrine command progression:** Local receives two first-Doctrine Commands in Act III and one second-Doctrine Command in Act IV (3 total); Standard 2 + 2 (4); Long 3 + 2 (5); Grand 3 + 3 (6). The first Doctrine Command choices are locked when selected; choosing the second Doctrine does not replace them. Each chosen Command grants its printed recruitment slot and becomes an available Command option, not a permanent increase to the GM's CP.

**Army Points:** The Opposition's deployed army is bounded by the scenario's agreed opposition Point allowance, normally near the PCs' combined legal Company ceiling for a comparable pitched battle. Final four-PC guide: Local 1,500; Standard 2,000; Long 2,500; Grand 3,000 Points. The GM may not simply fill all slots for free; every Unit must have a lawful military source and Points allowance. Scenario asymmetry (defensive terrain, ambushes, independent third parties) must be declared and balanced as part of the mission.

**Allied formations:** The Opposition may normally deploy up to two outside-faction allied or contracted formations, subject to legitimate agreements and the encounter's Points/slots. Political defeats may limit its available sources but do not inherently reduce its tactical Point ceiling. The GM may use legal home-faction substitutes to rebuild an available force.

## 13.4 Persistent Military Assets

Important enemy commanders, specialist formations, and military Assets should remain persistent. A destroyed named Asset cannot return without a valid recovery or replacement source. The GM need not maintain detailed casualty records for every unnamed ordinary infantry formation.

## 13.5 Opposition Operations and Encounter Pacing

The Principal Opposition should pursue recognizable objectives rather than waiting passively for the PCs to initiate combat. At the beginnings of Acts II and III, the GM establishes a major Opposition objective that can also serve as that Act's Pivotal Objective.

An Opposition Operation identifies:

Its objective and target. The relevant Force or military source. Its intended timing. The consequences of success or failure. Ways the PCs can oppose, redirect, or avert it. The Opposition advances its operation through legal Maneuvers, existing agreements, Force positions, and appropriate narrative developments. Opposition Operations do not automatically grant extra Maneuver cards, army points, or free strategic actions.

A military operation must still have a legitimate means of occurring. The PCs should receive enough information to make meaningful responses.

**Recommended Encounter Progression**

Act I: Establish the Opposition through patrols, agents, local conflicts, or consequences of its activities. Act II: Provide an opportunity for the first meaningful military confrontation. Act III: Escalate to larger battles, advanced units, and the Opposition's first Doctrine. Act IV: Reveal and allow interference with the final army's significant support sources, culminating in a decisive confrontation.

The PCs may resolve a threat through diplomacy, sabotage, or another legitimate alternative rather than fighting the expected battle.

## 13.6 Preparing the Final Army

By the latter part of Act III, the GM should make important Opposition support sources discoverable. During Act IV, the PCs should have opportunities to affect allied contingents, military facilities, logistics, special Assets, and comparable advantages. The final Opposition army must respect the resulting persistent campaign state.

# 14. Strategic Warfare and Commands

## 14.1 Structured Battles

Significant military encounters use the structured combat rules. Campaign Mode determines participating forces, legal deployment, strategic circumstances, and battlefield objectives. The tactical combat rules determine movement, attacks, activations, saves, commands, and battle outcomes. Narrative combat may be used when a full tabletop battle is unnecessary.

## 14.2 Principal Opposition Doctrines

The Principal Opposition chooses the first Doctrine **before PC Doctrine selections at the beginning of Act III**, and chooses its second distinct Doctrine at the beginning of Act IV to adapt to the developing Company. Its Command progression and recruitment-slot totals are defined in Section 13.3.

| Campaign | First Doctrine Commands (Act III) | Second Doctrine Commands (Act IV) | Total Known Doctrine Commands |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Local | 2 | 1 | 3 |
| Standard | 2 | 2 | 4 |
| Long | 3 | 2 | 5 |
| Grand | 3 | 3 | 6 |

These are available Command options in addition to eligible Faction and Unit Commands. Knowing additional Commands does not independently increase CP or authorize extra ordinary activations. Doctrine progression is not an additional Faction Maneuver.

## 14.3 Campaign Command Points

Campaign CP is **separate from the eight-Command-card pool used in 2,000-point matched play**. CP refreshes at the start of each tactical Round. A Player Character normally receives **2 CP per Round**, usable on any number of eligible Commands within their remaining allocation. Early in the campaign these are simply tracked as points: no physical Command cards are required. Later, Doctrine and Unit Commands provide more options but do not automatically increase a PC's ordinary two-CP allowance.

The GM controls one shared pool for the Principal Opposition. For Local, Standard, and Long Campaigns the normal allocation is `max(2, 2 × participating PCs − 1)` CP each Round. **For Grand Campaigns only**, use the campaign-scale exception `max(2, 2 × participating PCs + 1)` CP per Round. This is an intentionally stronger command budget for the Grand format's larger 19-slot army, independent of the number of Command cards the GM knows.

| Participating PCs | PC combined ordinary CP | GM CP, Local/Standard/Long | GM CP, Grand |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 4 | 3 | 5 |
| 3 | 6 | 5 | 7 |
| 4 | 8 | 7 | 9 |
| 5 | 10 | 9 | 11 |

This Grand CP exception is a **provisional balance parameter** for playtesting and does not apply to matched play. Physical cards, if used, represent the available CP; exhausting a card spends that CP rather than consuming a second resource.

## 14.4 Battlefield Edges

A Battlefield Edge represents one specific strategic advantage in an upcoming tactical encounter. It is not a free Command, additional Unit, or general CP increase.

**Before deployment**, the owning side publicly declares that it is committing an Edge and selects **one exact Round** of the upcoming battle and **one opposing participant** who will be affected. When that Round begins and CP refreshes, the named participant has **1 fewer CP** available for that Round (minimum 1 CP). An Edge aimed at the GM reduces the GM's shared pool by 1; an Edge aimed at the PC side names a particular PC, not every PC.

An Edge is spent when committed **even if the battle ends before the selected Round** or its target becomes unable to act. A side may commit at most **two Edges per battle**; the same participant may be affected by multiple Edges, respecting the minimum-1-CP floor. A side cannot wait until the chosen Round to declare its use or change its target later. Edges may arise from Mobilize Supremacy, Fortresses, Watchtowers, Field Preparations, or specified missions.

The Edge functions even during early Acts with no Doctrine cards: simply reduce the named participant's available CP for the selected Round. In matched play, an Edge is used only if the scenario explicitly makes it available, and its CP effect follows the matched-play Command economy for that game.

# 15. Neutral Opportunities and Exploration

## 15.1 Neutral Opportunities

A scheduled Neutral Opportunity may appear during a Campaign Turn alongside the ordinary political Stake. It represents an objective whose primary benefit is not political recognition for a participating Grand Faction.

A successfully completed Neutral Opportunity awards:

One gray Act marker. Its stated reward. No automatic Development draw. If it fails or is not pursued, resolve the ordinary political Stake. Only one Act marker is awarded for that Turn.

## 15.2 Treasury Opportunities

Each Act contains one scheduled Treasury Opportunity. A successful Treasury Opportunity awards 2 Gold per PC. The Gold enters the shared treasury.

## 15.3 Special Opportunities

| Campaign | Specials per Act |
| --- | ---: |
| Local | 0 |
| Standard | 1 |
| Long | 1 |
| Grand | 2 |

Special Opportunities can award formations, Assets, crafting benefits, infrastructure, or unusual political access. Their rewards remain subject to stated source, ownership, slot, and deployment restrictions. The complete catalog appears in Appendix F.

## 15.4 Exploration Sites

Exploration Sites may be resolved through Explore or another qualifying mission.

When successfully completed for the first time, a Site awards:

5 XP per PC. Its normal random Discovery or fixed reward. Any legitimate material consequences of the expedition. Scouting does not itself complete a Site. A resolved Site does not normally provide another Discovery or exploration XP. The Discovery catalog appears in Appendix G.

# 16. Act Track and Political Victory

## 16.1 Act Markers

Each Campaign Turn produces exactly one:

Colored marker for a qualifying Grand Faction winner; or Gray marker for a Neutral result. A faction receives a marker only when it wins the political Stake.

## 16.2 Development Reward for Colored Stakes

Winning a normal colored Stake awards the victorious faction:

One colored Act marker. One Development reward draw. Any separate material consequence must be earned through its own legal requirements. If the PCs direct an independent faction that wins, that independent faction receives its own marker and Development reward. Neutral results do not automatically grant Development draws.

## 16.3 Winning an Act

At the end of an Act, count colored markers separately for each participating Grand Faction. The faction with the highest individual total wins the Act Agenda. Gray markers do not contribute to any faction's score.

## 16.4 Contested Acts

If two or more participating Grand Factions tie for the highest colored-marker total, the Act is Contested. No faction receives the full Agenda victory. Each participating Grand Faction receives one appropriate minor Development consolation. The Principal Opposition does not receive an additional second consolation solely for being the Principal Opposition. This replaces the older extra-Opposition consolation rule.

## 16.5 Persistence

Act transitions do not reset territorial control, military agreements, infrastructure, exploration progress, the Owned Roster, or important military casualties. Agenda rewards and Act outcomes change the next stage of the campaign without automatically determining its final outcome.

# 17. Campaign Turn Procedure

Campaign Turns use two Council stages surrounding the Company's main operation.

## 17.1 Beginning an Act

When an Act begins:

Resolve the previous Act's result, if applicable. Award Provincial Dividends for Acts II, III, and IV. Award 5 Act Advancement XP per PC for Acts II, III, and IV. Apply Charter, Patron, and Doctrine progression. Apply the Principal Opposition's relevant Doctrine progression. Reveal the new Act Agenda. Refresh participating NPC Maneuver decks. Prepare the Act Task pool and public Task Display.

Establish the new Act's major political and military situation. In Acts II and III, establish the Pivotal Objective. Review expired agreements, Mandates, and temporary Development rewards. Retain the current Council Influence holder. Company Orders refresh only if the next Turn begins in Spring.

## 17.2 Preparation Council

**Step 1 — Reveal Available Opportunities**

Show the current Task Display and any scheduled Neutral Opportunity.

**Step 2 — Select the Stake**

The Council Influence holder chooses one eligible Task and declares its primary target and suit. Identify its eligible Lead.

**Step 3 — Commit Faction Maneuvers**

The Principal Opposition commits one available card. Apply Council Influence and Political Mandates to eligible independent powers. Other independent powers commit according to their established interests and obligations. Every participating non-PC Grand Faction commits exactly one card. Record legal intended map actions.

**Step 4 — Select the Company Order**

The PCs select one available Order. Determine Supremacy. Declare the Company's intended strategic objective and whether it intends to pursue the Stake, a Neutral Opportunity, or another activity.

**Step 5 — Establish the Scenario**

Identify the relevant locations, participating characters and formations, challenges, and potential consequences.

## 17.3 Resolve the Company Operation

Play the declared Company operation using narrative play, structured combat, or appropriate strategic resolution. A major mission may occupy several gaming sessions while still consuming one Campaign Turn. The Company does not gain extra Turn XP or Orders merely because the mission required more sessions.

## 17.4 Resolution Council

**Step 1 — Record the Mission Outcome**

Apply the actual results of the Company's operation. Determine whether it completed a scheduled Neutral Opportunity or fulfilled the current Stake's requirements.

**Step 2 — Resolve the Political Outcome**

A successfully completed Neutral Opportunity takes precedence if it was the Company's declared primary objective. Otherwise, determine whether the PCs' qualifying mission resolves the Stake. If neither applies, reveal and compare eligible NPC commitments.

**Step 3 — Award One Act Marker**

Place the correct colored or gray marker. A colored Stake winner receives one Development draw. A completed Neutral Opportunity receives its stated reward instead.

**Step 4 — Apply NPC Map Actions**

Each participating non-PC Grand Faction resolves its one legal basic Maneuver action, irrespective of the political winner.

**Step 5 — Update the Campaign**

Record political ownership, Provinces, infrastructure, resources, military access, Force locations, agreements, named losses, and other persistent consequences.

**Step 6 — Upkeep**

Apply eligible Gold transactions, formation recovery, reward expiration, and other Upkeep.

Award:

5 XP per PC for the completed Turn. 5 XP per PC for every newly resolved unique Site. 5 XP per PC if the current Act's eligible Pivotal Objective was resolved. Prevent duplicate awards for previously resolved objectives.

**Step 7 — Advance the Calendar**

Advance the Season and resolve any Year or Act boundary.

**Step 8 — Pass Council Influence**

Transfer the Council Influence token to the other side. The next Campaign Turn may now be prepared.

## 17.5 Multiple Turns Between Sessions

A Company action that does not require a substantial adventure may be resolved during the Campaign Council. Several straightforward Turns may be resolved in sequence. Every Turn still requires its normal political commitments, one Company Order, one Act marker, applicable map actions, and Upkeep.

# 18. Game Master Responsibilities

## 18.1 Referee

The GM applies rules consistently, determines legal outcomes, runs encounters, and adjudicates uncertain circumstances. This role takes priority over the GM's interest in winning as the Principal Opposition.

## 18.2 Principal Opposition

The GM controls the Opposition's strategic decisions and legal military preparations. The Opposition should pursue coherent long-term objectives and meaningfully threaten regional interests. Its available armies and Assets must respect established campaign sources and consequences.

## 18.3 Independent Grand Factions

The GM administers independent powers according to their public interests and obligations. Independent powers should behave consistently and remain recognizable political actors rather than functioning as additional arbitrary GM armies.

## 18.4 Maintaining Military Pressure

The GM should provide significant opportunities to interact with the Principal Opposition during Acts II, III, and IV. The encounters need not all end in structured battles. Players may defeat an operation militarily, negotiate its cancellation, undermine its logistics, or suffer its consequences. The aim is to make the Opposition's military growth and the PCs' responses visibly connected to the campaign.

# 19. Example Campaign Turn

The Company is affiliated with Concord. The Principal Opposition is the Legions. The Conclave and Wayfarers are independent Grand Factions. The campaign is in Act III. The PCs hold Council Influence.

**Step 1 — Stake**

The available Tasks include a Warfare objective concerning a contested crossing near Stonebridge. The PCs select that Task. The Wayfarers possess a recognized defensive agreement covering the crossing.

**Step 2 — Independent Commitment**

The PCs direct the Wayfarers. They commit Warfare 5. Their qualifying Local Influence gives +1, for a total of 6. The Legions commit Warfare 6 and have no Local Influence. Their effective Strength is also 6. The Conclave Pivots with an Industry card toward a legal industrial objective.

**Step 3 — Company Order**

The PCs select Develop (3). Four PCs provide five Minor Project opportunities.

The Company spends:

Two opportunities on one Talent Retraining Major Project. Two opportunities on one Expand Detachment Major Project. One opportunity on an ordinary formation upgrade. The relevant Gold costs are paid. The Company does not directly pursue the crossing Stake.

**Step 4 — Political Resolution**

The Wayfarers and Legions tie at Strength 6. Because the Wayfarers are the eligible incumbent defenders, they win. Place one Wayfarer-colored Act marker. The Wayfarers receive one Development draw. The PCs do not receive the draw.

**Step 5 — Map Actions**

The Wayfarers move their Force one legal hex. The Legions move their Force one legal hex. The Conclave resolves its legal Industry effect. No territory changes ownership automatically.

**Step 6 — Upkeep**

Record the Company's Develop expenditures, new Flex slot, formation upgrade, and completed Talent retraining. Each PC receives 5 XP for the completed Campaign Turn. The Turn produces no exploration XP because no Site was resolved. The Season advances and Council Influence passes to the GM.

# 20. Core Campaign Principles

One Turn, One Order: The Company selects one available Company Order per Campaign Turn. One Faction, One Maneuver: Every participating non-PC Grand Faction commits one Maneuver and resolves one associated basic map action per Turn. One Turn, One Stake: Exactly one colored or gray Act marker is awarded. Political Recognition Is Not Material Control: Winning a political contest does not automatically grant territory, units, construction, or completed missions.

Character Points Use Spent Talent XP: A Hero has a base value of 100 Points until XP spent on Talents exceeds 100; afterward, its base equals Talent XP actually spent. Equipped equipment and explicitly additional Point costs are added separately; unspent XP is not counted. Character and Military Progression Are Distinct: XP improves PCs; Gold and legal opportunities improve the Company and its military organization. Exploration Advances Characters: A unique completed Site provides 5 XP per PC in addition to its normal reward.

Territory Supports the Warband: Provincial Dividends provide 1 Gold per PC for each majority-controlled Province at eligible Act milestones. Development Uses Shared Capacity: Develop provides 1 + PCs Minor Project opportunities; two may be exchanged for one Major, and each Major category may be selected once per Order. Resources Determine Eligibility: Terrain, connections, territorial ownership, and agreements determine what strategic actions are possible.

Political Influence Is Not Ownership: Influence provides limited political presence and possible Strength bonuses, not unrestricted control. Independent Powers Remain Independent: Directing a faction's commitment does not transfer ownership of its rewards or troops. Military Access Must Have a Source: Borrowed, contracted, and unusual formations require valid agreements or other established rights.

Point Ceilings Limit Deployment, Not Ownership: Reserve formations may exist beyond an army's legal deployed point total.
